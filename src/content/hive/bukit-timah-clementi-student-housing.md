If you're at NUS, start with Clementi, Dover, Buona Vista, Ghim Moh, or Queenstown. All of them sit one or two MRT stops from Kent Ridge, or a short bus ride from the campus gate, and between them they cover almost every budget. Doing law or public policy at the Bukit Timah campus instead? Bukit Timah itself is the obvious pick. NTU students should look at Pioneer, Boon Lay, or Jurong West, the towns that actually sit next to the Jurong campus, or Jurong East if a bigger town centre is worth a longer commute.

That's the short answer. The longer one covers what each area actually offers, what things cost in 2026, and one rule that trips up more students than any of it: Singapore doesn't allow private rentals under three months, semester length or not.

## What the University Belt Looks Like

Singapore doesn't have one single "student area" the way some cities do. What it has is a loose belt running along the west side of the island, from Bukit Timah down through Queenstown, Commonwealth, Buona Vista, Dover, and Clementi, then further out to Jurong East, Boon Lay, and Pioneer.

NUS's main Kent Ridge campus sits at the southern end of that belt, on the Circle Line, with Buona Vista and Clementi both a short bus ride or walk away. NUS Law and the Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy run from a separate site, the Bukit Timah campus on Bukit Timah Road, a few kilometres north.

NTU sits well to the west, near Jurong West, closer to Pioneer and Boon Lay MRT than to anything in the Clementi corridor. A free shuttle runs students between the MRT stations and the middle of campus, since the walk itself is long.

That geography is why this guide covers two areas that don't touch each other on a map. A Clementi room suits an NUS student well and an NTU student badly. Match the campus to the area first, then start comparing rooms.

## The Student Rental Market in 2026

On-campus housing is still the cheapest legal option, when you can get it. NTU's cheaper hall rooms run in the low hundreds of dollars a month, with the full range across room types landing roughly between S$400 and S$700+ a month depending on type and air conditioning [VERIFY: current NTU hall rates against the live Student Housing Portal]. NUS is similar in shape: hall rooms start from around S$494 a month for AY2026-27, with Prince George's Park Residences billed weekly instead, from roughly S$145 to S$240 a week depending on room type, across an 18-week first semester and a 17-week second one [VERIFY: current OSA rate sheet, these figures move most years].

Off campus, the range is wider. A shared room in an HDB flat typically runs S$700 to S$1,200 a month. A managed studio or a room in a student residence sits around S$1,200 to S$1,800. A whole private apartment starts from about S$1,800 and climbs from there depending on size and area. Co-living rooms, which bundle furnishing, utilities, and wifi into one price, tend to land in the HDB-room-to-studio range rather than at the top.

The other thing shaping 2026 is timing, not price. NUS hall applications for the August intake typically open in February and close by April, before most offer letters even land in May. Miss that window, or simply don't get a hall place, and the private market becomes the default, which is most of the reason this guide exists.

## On Campus vs Off Campus vs Co-Living

| | On campus (hall/PGP) | Off campus (private) | Co-living |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical monthly cost | Cheapest, roughly S$400 to S$1,000+ | Widest range, S$700 to S$1,800+ | Middle of the private range, usually furnished |
| Availability | Not guaranteed, application-based | Whatever's on the market | Whatever's on the market |
| Minimum stay | Set by the university | 3 months, by law | 3 months, by law |
| Furnishing | Basic, provided | Usually bare, tenant furnishes | Fully furnished, move-in ready |
| Who you deal with | University housing office | Individual landlord or agent | A managed operator |
| Best fit | Full-degree undergrads who apply early | Students who want a full flat or specific area | Exchange, postgrad, or anyone who doesn't want to chase a landlord |

None of the three is automatically right. On campus wins on price if you get a spot. Off campus wins on space and control if you're renting with friends. Co-living wins on speed, since the room, price, and terms are published rather than negotiated one listing at a time.

## 11 Areas Within Reach of the Main Campuses

1. **Clementi**. One MRT stop from Dover, a short bus from Kent Ridge, and close enough to the AYE for NTU too. Big mall, wide mix of HDB and condo stock.
2. **Dover**. The stop closest to NUS's Science and Medicine side. Quiet, mostly residential, fewer eating options than Clementi.
3. **Buona Vista**. Circle Line and East West Line interchange, one bus from NUS, right next to the one-north tech hub. Good transport, higher rent.
4. **Holland Village**. Cafes, bars, and a real social scene, a short ride from Buona Vista. Priced accordingly.
5. **Kent Ridge**. Literally on the NUS doorstep via the Circle Line. Limited residential stock immediately around it, but what's there needs no commute at all.
6. **West Coast**. A more affordable, residential stretch 15 to 20 minutes from NUS by bus. West Coast Park nearby for anyone who wants green space.
7. **Queenstown / Commonwealth**. Established HDB estate one or two stops from Buona Vista and Dover. Wide range of unit sizes and prices.
8. **Ghim Moh**. A smaller HDB pocket next to Buona Vista, walking distance to the NUS shuttle stop, with a well-known hawker centre.
9. **Bukit Timah**. Home ground for NUS Law and the Lee Kuan Yew School of Public Policy. Mostly landed housing and some condos, generally the priciest area on this list.
10. **Jurong East**. A regional hub with its own mall and MRT interchange, reachable to NTU via Boon Lay and to NUS by bus. More affordable than the east side of the belt.
11. **Pioneer / Boon Lay**. The two stations that actually sit next to NTU, connected by the campus shuttle. HDB-heavy, budget-friendly, and the shortest possible commute if NTU is home base.

## Myth: Off Campus Is Always More Expensive

Not true, though the cheapest single option is genuinely on campus if you can get a place. The mistake is comparing the cheapest hall room to the priciest private listing and calling that the whole picture.

A shared HDB room in West Coast or Jurong East can cost less than some pricier hall room types, and it comes without a lottery for a spot. Co-living rooms sit in a similar band once furnishing, wifi, and utilities are bundled in, so the all-in monthly number often lands closer to a hall fee than expected, without the application deadline.

Where off campus does get more expensive is a whole private apartment in Bukit Timah or Holland Village, rented solo or with one flatmate. That's a lifestyle choice, not the default price of living off campus.

## Off Campus Housing: A Student Starter Guide

If this is your first time renting in Singapore, a few terms are worth knowing before you look at a single listing.

**Licence agreement vs tenancy agreement.** Renting a room in a shared flat is usually a licence agreement, which covers a room and shared common areas. Renting a whole unit is a tenancy agreement. The difference affects your rights if something goes wrong, so read whichever one you're signing rather than skimming it.

**The 3 month rule.** Under URA rules, private residential property in Singapore, HDB flats, condos, and landed houses alike, cannot legally be rented out for less than three consecutive months. This has applied since 2017 and hasn't changed for 2026. It applies to every operator in the private market, co-living included, not just individual landlords.

**Deposit.** Expect one to two months' rent as a security deposit, refundable at the end of the stay minus any legitimate deductions for damage beyond normal wear and tear.

**Utilities and wifi.** Some listings bundle these into the rent, others bill separately. Confirm which before comparing prices, since a higher all-in rent can still beat a lower rent plus separate bills.

**Application process.** Usually a viewing (in person or video call), an offer, a deposit, and a signed agreement. HDB rooms have extra eligibility rules around who a homeowner can rent to, which a co-living operator or agent should already have sorted on their end.

## Mistakes: Signing a Twelve Month Lease for a Ten Month Course

This is the single most common housing mistake international and exchange students make here. A typical NUS or NTU semester runs 17 to 18 weeks, so a full academic year comes to roughly eight or nine months of actual term time. A one-semester exchange is closer to four or five months. Neither matches the 12-month lease that most private landlords default to offering.

The URA rule sets a floor of three months, not a ceiling of twelve. There's no legal reason a lease has to run a full year. Landlords push for 12 months for fewer turnovers, not because the law requires it.

The fix is to look for terms that actually match your course length before you sign anything. Some private landlords will do a 4 to 6 month lease if you ask directly. Co-living operators with published, room-by-room availability, Lazybee among them, tend to make this easier to check up front, since the term on the listing is the actual term on offer rather than a starting point for negotiation.

If you do end up on a longer lease than your course, understand the early termination terms before you sign, not after you need them. A subletting allowance or a break clause after a set number of months can save a lot of stress later.

## A Student Housing Checklist

- Confirm which campus you're actually commuting to, Kent Ridge, Bukit Timah, or Jurong, before picking an area
- Match the lease or licence length to your actual course length, not the landlord's default term
- Confirm the room, price, and included utilities in writing before paying any deposit
- Check the minimum stay is legally compliant, three months or more, for any private listing
- Ask what's furnished and what you'd need to bring or buy yourself
- Get the commute time to your actual timetable, not just to "the campus," since faculties can sit on different sides of a large site
- Confirm the deposit refund process and what counts as normal wear and tear
- Save every payment receipt and the signed agreement somewhere you can access after moving in

## Student Housing: The Questions Parents Ask

**Is it safe for my child to live off campus in Singapore?**
Singapore is a low-crime city generally, and the areas on this list are established residential neighbourhoods, not fringe ones. The bigger safety factor is usually who they're sharing with, not the neighbourhood.

**Can they rent for just one semester?**
Yes, as long as it's three months or longer, which most single semesters are. Anything shorter legally can't be a private rental, so a short summer programme needs on-campus or licensed short-stay accommodation instead.

**What if the course is shorter than three months?**
University-run accommodation isn't bound by the same private-market minimum, which is why it's usually the only lawful option for a stay that short. A private room or co-living listing can't legally go below that floor.

**How far is too far from campus?**
Past about 30 to 40 minutes door to door, the commute starts eating into study time and sleep more than the rent saved is usually worth.

**Do co-living rooms come with roommates picked for them?**
Depends on the operator and room type. Some are single rooms in a shared flat with existing housemates, others are twin or multi-share. Check the listing rather than assuming either way.

**Is a licence agreement as legally solid as a tenancy agreement?**
Both are enforceable contracts. The difference is what they cover, a room plus shared space versus a whole unit, not how seriously either one should be taken.
