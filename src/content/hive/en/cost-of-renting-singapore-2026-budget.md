The cost of renting in Singapore 2026 depends heavily on what you're renting. A room in a shared home typically runs from about S$600 for a small budget room up to S$2,500 for a private ensuite, with most common rooms landing between S$1,000 and S$1,600 a month. A studio or one-bedroom unit on your own usually starts around S$1,900 to S$2,900 and can pass S$3,500 in a central location. But rent is only part of the number. Once you add utilities, wifi, food, transport, and the upfront costs of moving in, a realistic all-in monthly budget for a newcomer renting a room sits somewhere between S$1,800 and S$2,800, and for someone renting a whole unit alone, closer to S$3,500 to S$5,500.

That's the headline. The rest of this piece breaks down where those numbers come from, what's actually included in a rental price, and the costs that catch people out because nobody mentions them until the first invoice lands.

## What renting in Singapore actually includes

"Rent" in a Singapore listing can mean very different things depending on what you're renting. A room in a co-living house or a shared flat usually comes furnished, and increasingly comes with utilities, wifi, and cleaning bundled into one price. A room rented directly from a landlord on a private tenancy is more likely to be furniture-only, with utilities and wifi billed separately and split between housemates.

A whole unit rented on its own, whether a studio or a full apartment, almost never includes utilities or wifi in the headline rent. You're setting those up yourself, paying the deposits, and carrying the full bill each month rather than splitting it.

Before you compare two listings on price alone, check what's actually bundled. A S$1,400 room that includes everything can be cheaper in practice than a S$1,100 room that doesn't.

## Singapore rental prices as they stand in 2026

Singapore's private residential rental index has been broadly flat to slightly up through the first half of 2026, after a couple of years of cooling from the sharp run-up in 2022 and 2023. Suburban condo rents have softened a little as new completions added supply, while central and landed rents have held up better. None of this moves room prices much, since rooms track the wider market more slowly than whole units do.

For rooms specifically, the current spread looks roughly like this:

- Budget or compact rooms: about S$600 to S$900 a month
- Standard common rooms: about S$1,000 to S$1,600 a month
- Master bedrooms or ensuites: about S$1,700 to S$2,500 a month
- Co-living rooms (furnished, often all-inclusive): roughly S$1,200 to S$2,500+, depending on operator and location

For whole units, the general bands are:

- Studios: typically S$1,900 to S$3,500, with a lot of listings clustering around S$2,400 to S$2,900
- One-bedroom apartments: roughly S$2,300 to S$5,000, with central locations sitting well above suburban ones

These are broad market ranges pulled together from current listing data, not a quote for any specific property. Always check the actual listing price for the unit you're looking at, since location, condition, and building age move the number a lot within each band.

## 9 costs that are not the rent

These are the costs that regularly get left out of a newcomer's mental budget, and the reason a "S$1,500 rent" plan can quietly become a S$2,200 month.

1. **Security deposit.** Standard practice is one month's rent for a lease of a year, two months for a two-year lease. This is paid upfront and separate from your first month's rent.
2. **Advance rent.** Most landlords want the first month paid in advance on top of the deposit, so your move-in cash need is usually two to three months' rent combined.
3. **Stamp duty.** For a signed tenancy agreement on a whole unit, stamp duty of 0.4% of the total rent over the lease term is customarily paid by the tenant, within 14 days of signing. Room licence agreements are sometimes structured differently, so confirm this applies to your specific contract before assuming either way.
4. **Agent commission.** If you use an agent, commission of roughly half a month's to a full month's rent is common, and who pays it depends on rent level and lease length. Room rentals and co-living bookings usually skip this entirely.
5. **Utilities.** Where not bundled into rent, expect roughly S$120 to S$300 a month depending on unit size and air-conditioning use, plus a utilities deposit when you open the account.
6. **Wifi and mobile.** Home broadband if not included, plus a local SIM. Modest but recurring, and easy to forget when budgeting from overseas.
7. **Groceries and food.** A hawker-heavy diet runs roughly S$400 to S$700 a month; mixing in cafes and restaurants pushes that to S$800 to S$1,200.
8. **Transport.** Public transport alone typically costs S$100 to S$150 a month for daily commuting. Add ride-hailing for late nights and it climbs from there.
9. **Move-in basics.** Bedding, a few kitchen items, a fan or extra storage if the room is unfurnished. A one-off cost, but real, and usually paid in week one when your budget is already stretched.

## Myth: Singapore is unaffordable for young renters

This gets repeated a lot, and it's overstated. It's true that a whole apartment in a central district is genuinely expensive, and headline cost-of-living figures for Singapore look intimidating when they're built around a full one-bedroom rental. But most newcomers, especially anyone arriving without a Singapore salary yet, are not renting a whole apartment on day one. They're renting a room.

A room, especially an all-inclusive one, brings the total monthly number down into a range that's genuinely comparable to other major Asian and Western cities. The affordability conversation changes completely depending on whether you're pricing a full condo or a common room, and most guides quoting scary headline numbers are pricing the former.

## Budgeting for Singapore before you have a salary here

If you're planning a move before you've landed a job or your first paycheck has cleared, the number that matters isn't your monthly budget. It's your landing cash: what you need in hand before you've earned a cent locally.

At minimum, plan for:

- Deposit plus advance rent for your room or unit (typically two to three months' rent combined)
- One to two months of full living costs as a buffer, since your first local paycheck may land four to six weeks after you start work
- A currency buffer if you're converting from a weaker or more volatile currency
- Visa and pass-related costs, which vary by pass type and are worth budgeting separately once you know which one applies to you

This is where an all-inclusive room genuinely simplifies the arithmetic. Instead of estimating five separate bills you've never paid in Singapore dollars before, you're budgeting one number. That's a real reduction in planning risk for someone budgeting from another country with no local reference point.

## Mistakes: budgeting the rent and forgetting the first month

The single most common mistake is treating the monthly rent as the whole plan, forgetting that move-in needs two to three months of rent in cash upfront, not one. Someone who's saved exactly one month's rent, thinking that covers them, is usually short once deposit and advance rent are both due.

The second is comparing rooms on rent alone without checking what's included. A cheaper room with no utilities, no wifi, and a shared unmetered aircon bill can end up costing more per month than a slightly pricier all-inclusive one, once the side bills are added up.

The third is misjudging food and transport. People from cities where eating out is expensive often assume Singapore will match that, miss how affordable hawker food actually is, and end up over budgeting unnecessarily, or the opposite: they never check hawker prices and get caught out assuming restaurant-level costs.

## A budget checklist for your first six months

- [ ] Confirm exactly what's included in the rent (utilities, wifi, cleaning) before comparing listings on price
- [ ] Set aside two to three months' rent in cash for deposit plus advance rent before you commit to a move-in date
- [ ] Check whether stamp duty or agent commission applies to your specific agreement, and who pays it
- [ ] Budget S$500 to S$1,000 a month for food and transport combined, adjusted for your actual habits
- [ ] Keep one month of full living costs as a buffer beyond your first payday, in case it lands later than expected
- [ ] Open a local bank account and get a local SIM in your first week to avoid overseas transaction fees eating into your budget
- [ ] Re-check your total monthly number against your actual income at the three-month mark, not just at move-in, since habits and prices both shift once you're settled

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it realistically cost to rent in Singapore in 2026?**
A room in a shared home typically runs S$600 to S$2,500 depending on room type, with most falling between S$1,000 and S$1,600. A studio or one-bedroom unit typically runs S$1,900 to S$5,000. Add utilities, food, and transport, and a realistic all-in monthly budget is roughly S$1,800 to S$2,800 for a room and S$3,500 to S$5,500 for a unit on your own.

**Is it cheaper to rent a room or a whole apartment in Singapore?**
A room is cheaper on almost every measure: lower rent, often bundled utilities, no agent commission, smaller deposit. A whole unit costs more but gives full privacy and no housemates.

**What upfront costs should I budget for before I move?**
A security deposit (one month's rent for a one-year lease, two months for two years) plus one month's rent in advance. That's typically two to three months' rent in cash before you get the keys, on top of stamp duty and agent commission where applicable.

**Does co-living actually save money compared to renting a room privately?**
It depends on the operator, but the real saving is often predictability rather than headline price. An all-inclusive co-living room bundles utilities, wifi, and cleaning into one number, removing several separate bills a private rental would leave you to set up and split yourself.

**How much should I budget for food and transport on top of rent?**
Public transport for a daily commute typically costs S$100 to S$150 a month. Food ranges from roughly S$400 to S$700 for a hawker-heavy diet up to S$800 to S$1,200 for a mixed dining lifestyle. Budget roughly S$500 to S$1,000 a month combined, depending on habits.
