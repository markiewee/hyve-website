Housing for Nepali nationals in Singapore comes down to one question: what pass are you on. Most working and studying Nepalis here hold an Employment Pass, S Pass, or Student Pass, and rent on the open private market exactly like any other foreign professional or student, co-living included. That's a different picture from some other nationalities in the region, since Nepal currently sits outside the main source-country lists Singapore uses for Work Permit hiring in construction, marine, process, manufacturing, and services, so the large-scale dormitory system built for those sectors mostly doesn't apply here. This guide walks through what actually applies to you, and where co-living fits.

## What Pass Are You On? This Decides Everything

Singapore's foreign workforce runs on a small set of passes, and each one comes with a different housing reality.

**Employment Pass (EP).** For professionals, managers, and executives, with a 2026 qualifying salary floor of S$5,600 a month for most sectors (S$6,200 in financial services), assessed alongside the COMPASS points framework. EP holders rent their own housing, and there's no nationality restriction on who can hold one.

**S Pass.** For mid-skilled roles, with a 2026 qualifying salary floor of S$3,300 a month for most sectors (S$3,800 in financial services), rising with age. Like the EP, the S Pass has no country-of-origin restriction, and S Pass holders rent their own housing too.

**Student Pass.** For those enrolled at a Singapore university, polytechnic, or approved private school. Student Pass holders rent privately, though many first-years start in campus hostels before moving off-campus.

**Work Permit.** This is where Nepal's picture differs from several neighbouring countries. Work Permits for the construction, marine shipyard, process, manufacturing, and services sectors are restricted to a defined list of source countries and regions. Singapore's Foreign Employee Dormitories Act regulates the purpose-built dormitories that house Work Permit holders in these sectors at scale, roughly 1,600 licensed sites and 439,000 beds nationwide, and that system exists because of hiring from the countries above. This isn't a judgement on Work Permit holders, whose housing is a regulated and legitimate system serving hundreds of thousands well. It's a factual difference in pathway, and it means most Nepali nationals working in Singapore are doing so on an EP or S Pass, and searching the open rental market rather than a dormitory allocation.

If you're not sure which pass applies to you, confirm it with your employer or school before you look at a single listing. It changes everything else in this guide.

## EP, S Pass, and Student Pass Holders: You Rent Like Everyone Else

If you're on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass, you rent on the open private market, the same as a Singaporean citizen or anyone else holding one of these passes. HDB rooms, condo rooms, whole-unit rentals, and co-living rooms are all fair game, and the same rules on minimum stay, deposits, and agreements apply to you as to anyone else.

The one hard rule across every pass: you need the pass itself issued and in hand, not just an offer letter or an In-Principle Approval, before you can sign a tenancy or licence agreement. If you're arriving while your pass is still processing, a hotel or short-stay serviced apartment bridges the gap. Some co-living operators will let you reserve a room against an offer letter, with the agreement finalised once the pass clears.

## Where Does Co-Living Fit In?

Co-living is built for exactly the EP, S Pass, and Student Pass segment, which, as covered above, is where most working and studying Nepalis in Singapore actually sit.

For a Nepali professional starting a new role, or a student a few weeks from term start, co-living solves a specific problem: a room fast, furnished, with no local guarantor, and no bare 12-month tenancy signed before you've seen the neighbourhood.

- **No guarantor chase.** Operators verify your pass and income directly instead of requiring a local guarantor, which some HDB and condo landlords ask first-time renters for.
- **Furnished from day one.** Bed, wardrobe, wifi, and usually a shared kitchen are already set up.
- **Shorter, more flexible terms.** Useful if your Student Pass renews annually, or your lease shouldn't outlast your pass validity.

## Nepali Community in Singapore: Food, Faith, and Everyday Support

Singapore's Nepali community is smaller and more dispersed than some other South Asian communities here, with roots going back decades through the Gurkha Contingent, a unit within the Singapore Police Force made up largely of Nepali nationals under a long-standing tripartite arrangement between Britain, India, and Nepal. Gurkha Contingent members and their families are housed through the Police Force rather than the private rental market, so this group sits outside the scope of this guide, but it's part of why a Nepali presence exists here at all.

Beyond that, Nepali students and professionals tend to find each other through community groups such as the Nepalese Society (Singapore), which runs cultural events, sports meetups, and festivals around Dashain and Tihar. Momo and other Himalayan food can be found in pockets around Little India and Tekka, and Little India remains a practical first stop for groceries, SIM cards, and remittance services regardless of which pass you're on.

## Settling In: The Practical Stuff

A few things worth sorting in your first weeks, separate from housing itself.

**Banking.** DBS, OCBC, and UOB open accounts for foreigners holding a valid pass and passport, usually in person once you have a local address on file.

**SIM and connectivity.** A local SIM from Singtel, StarHub, or M1 needs your passport and pass, usually sorted same-day at a mall outlet or airport kiosk.

**Getting around.** The MRT and bus network covers most of the island, and an EZ-Link or bank contactless card gets you on both.

## Before You Sign: What to Check

- Your pass is issued and in hand, not just an offer letter or IPA.
- The lease length doesn't outlast your pass validity, especially on a Student Pass.
- Deposit terms and refund process are written into the agreement, not just promised verbally.
- The room and floor area match what's actually advertised. Our guide on [is co-living legal in Singapore](/blog/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore) covers the minimum stay and occupancy rules every legitimate room should already meet.

Once those four are confirmed, you're taking on no more risk than any other renter here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can Nepali nationals get a Work Permit to work in Singapore's construction or manufacturing sectors?**
Nepal does not currently appear on MOM's published traditional or Non-Traditional Sources country lists for Work Permit hiring in the construction, marine, process, manufacturing, and services sectors. Most Nepali nationals working in Singapore do so on an Employment Pass or S Pass instead.

**Can I rent a co-living room in Singapore on an S Pass?**
Yes. S Pass holders rent on the open market like any other pass holder, and co-living is a common option for a first move since it's furnished and doesn't require a local guarantor.

**Do Nepali students need to live in a hostel while studying in Singapore?**
Not necessarily. Many first-years start in campus hostels, but Student Pass holders are free to rent privately, including co-living, once the pass is issued.

**Is there a Nepali community in Singapore?**
Yes, though smaller than some other South Asian communities here. It includes the historic Gurkha Contingent within the Singapore Police Force, plus a growing base of students and working professionals connected through groups like the Nepalese Society (Singapore) and food and community pockets around Little India and Tekka.

**How much does a co-living room cost in Singapore?**
Realistically S$600 to S$2,500+ a month depending on room type and location, plus a deposit of around one month's rent. Check current listings for exact pricing since it moves by room and by month.
