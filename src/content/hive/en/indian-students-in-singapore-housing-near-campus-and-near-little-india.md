Most Indian students moving to Singapore for NUS, NTU, or SMU end up choosing between two priorities: a short commute to campus, or easy access to Little India for groceries, temples, and a community that already knows the rhythms of home. You don't always have to pick one. Little India sits on two MRT lines that reach several student-friendly neighbourhoods without a transfer, so a handful of areas genuinely deliver both. This guide covers the commute math, the areas that pull off that balance, and what to actually check before signing anything.

## Is Little India Still the Community Hub It Used to Be?

Yes. Little India has been a recognised conservation area since 1989, centred on Serangoon Road, and it remains the cultural and commercial anchor for Singapore's Indian and South Asian community ([Roots.gov.sg](https://www.roots.gov.sg/stories-landing/stories/little-india-a-cultural-and-historical-precinct/story)). Sri Veeramakaliamman and Sri Srinivasa Perumal temples run regular services, Tekka Centre is the go-to wet market and hawker centre for South Asian groceries and food, and Mustafa Centre, a few streets over, is a 24-hour department store that's become a Singapore institution in its own right, selling everything from groceries to gold ([TripAdvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g294265-d2139541-Reviews-Mustafa_Centre-Singapore.html)). None of that has faded. If anything, the area's role as a first point of contact for new arrivals from India has stayed remarkably stable while the rest of Singapore's rental map has shifted around it.

That matters for a practical reason. A weekly grocery run, a temple visit, or just being able to get a proper meal from home without planning a special trip is a genuine quality-of-life factor, not a minor preference. It's worth weighing alongside your commute, not after it.

## How Far Is Little India From NUS, NTU, and SMU?

This is where most students get the planning wrong, because "near Little India" and "near campus" sound like opposite priorities on a map, but the actual MRT times tell a different story depending on which university you're headed to.

| From Little India MRT to | Approx. time | Route |
|---|---|---|
| **SMU** (Bras Basah / Bugis) | 4 to 9 minutes | Direct on the Downtown Line |
| **NUS** (Kent Ridge, via Buona Vista) | About 20 minutes | Downtown Line to Botanic Gardens, transfer to Circle Line |
| **NTU** (via Boon Lay / Pioneer, then campus shuttle bus) | 40 to 60 minutes | North East Line to Outram Park, transfer to East West Line, then bus |

SMU students have it easiest by far, since Little India sits almost next door on the same line. NUS is a genuinely workable 20-minute ride with one clean transfer. NTU is the outlier: it's on the far west of the island, and no combination of MRT and bus gets you there from Little India in under 40 minutes. If NTU is your campus, prioritise a home base near Boon Lay, Pioneer, or Jurong East first, and treat trips to Little India as a deliberate outing rather than a daily habit.

## Areas That Balance Both Well

A handful of neighbourhoods sit close enough to Little India to make it a short, casual trip, while still keeping a reasonable ride to campus.

- **Serangoon**, on the North East Line, is about 10 minutes from Little India with no transfer, and also connects to the Circle Line for onward journeys. It's a well-established residential estate with its own mall, NEX, at the MRT station.
- **Novena and Newton**, both on the Downtown Line a few stops from Little India, put you close to the community hub and give you a straightforward interchange-based ride toward NUS or SMU.
- **Farrer Park and Boon Keng**, the two North East Line stations flanking Little India itself, are close enough to walk to Serangoon Road on a regular evening.
- **Toa Payoh**, one stop further out on the North East Line, trades a few extra minutes to Little India for generally lower rent.

None of these are exclusively "Indian student" areas and shouldn't be treated as such. They're simply well-connected residential neighbourhoods that happen to sit a short, direct ride from Little India, which is the actual thing worth optimising for.

## Myth: Living Near Little India Means a Long Campus Commute

This assumption stops a lot of students from even checking the numbers. It's true for NTU, where the geography just doesn't cooperate. It's not true for SMU, which is essentially next door, and it's not really true for NUS either, where a 20-minute ride with a single transfer is a normal Singapore commute, not a long one.

The mistake is treating "near Little India" and "near campus" as one fixed trade-off across all three universities, when the honest answer depends entirely on which university you're at. Check your own route before ruling an area out.

## What Every Student Renter Needs to Know First

A few Singapore rental basics apply regardless of nationality or which campus you're headed to, and they're easy to miss if this is your first time renting here.

- **The three-month minimum stay is a legal floor, not optional.** Private rentals, HDB, condo, or landed, cannot legally run under three consecutive months in Singapore. It applies to every operator, co-living included.
- **A licence agreement covers a room in a shared home; a tenancy agreement covers a whole unit.** Know which one you're signing.
- **Confirm your pass type before you commit.** Student Pass, Dependant's Pass, or a work pass after graduation each have slightly different documentation expectations from a landlord or operator. Our [guide to renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](https://lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) covers this in full.
- **Match your lease length to your actual course length**, not the landlord's default 12-month offer. A one-semester exchange rarely needs a full year's commitment; our [student housing guide for NUS and NTU](https://lazybee.sg/blog/bukit-timah-clementi-student-housing) walks through this in more detail.
- **A shorter MRT ride doesn't always mean a shorter total commute.** Walking time and transfers matter as much as the train itself; our [commute-time guide](https://lazybee.sg/blog/where-to-live-by-commute-time-to-cbd-singapore) explains how to test a listing's real commute before you book it.

- [ ] Confirm your actual campus (Kent Ridge, Bukit Timah, Bras Basah, or the NTU main campus) before ruling any area in or out
- [ ] Test your specific route to Little India and to campus on Google Maps or Citymapper at a weekday evening or morning peak, not off-peak
- [ ] Check whether the route to either destination is direct or needs a transfer, and where
- [ ] Confirm the minimum stay is legally compliant (three months or more) for any private listing
- [ ] Match lease length to your actual course length rather than accepting a default 12-month term
- [ ] Ask what's included in rent, utilities and wifi bundled in saves separate bill admin
- [ ] Confirm your pass documentation requirements with the operator or landlord before paying a deposit

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Little India a safe and practical place to base myself as a student?**
Yes. It's an established, well-served residential and commercial district, not a fringe area, with regular MRT service on two lines and one of Singapore's busiest 24-hour retail hubs. Like anywhere in central Singapore, it can get crowded on festival weekends, which is worth knowing rather than a safety concern.

**Which university has the easiest commute from near Little India?**
SMU, by a wide margin. It's a few minutes away on the Downtown Line. NUS is a workable 20-minute ride with one transfer. NTU is the one campus where living near Little India means a genuinely long daily commute.

**Do I have to choose between campus and Little India?**
Not always. Serangoon, Novena, Newton, Farrer Park, and Boon Keng all sit close to Little India while keeping a reasonable ride to NUS or SMU. NTU is the exception where the trade-off is real.

**Can international students on a Student Pass rent co-living rooms in Singapore?**
Yes, subject to the same three-month legal minimum as any private rental, and subject to the operator confirming your pass documentation. Check requirements before paying any deposit.
