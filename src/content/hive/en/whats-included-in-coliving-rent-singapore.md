Most co-living rooms in Singapore bundle wifi, utilities, and cleaning into one monthly rent, so you're not opening a separate account or paying a separate bill for any of them. That's the short answer. The longer answer is that "included" isn't standardised across operators. One building's rent covers weekly cleaning and quarterly aircon servicing. Another's covers utilities up to a cap and nothing else. The word "included" doesn't tell you the scope, only that the scope exists somewhere in the fine print.

The gap between operators is where surprise costs hide. A small aircon servicing fee here, a utility overage charge there. They add up over a year, and they're the kind of thing nobody thinks to ask at a viewing because "all inclusive" sounds final.

## Is Wifi and Cleaning Included in My Rent?

Almost always wifi, and usually cleaning of common areas, yes. Nearly every operator includes wifi in the base rent, since a shared house without internet isn't a sellable product in 2026. Cleaning is close to universal too, though the scope differs: most operators clean shared spaces on a set schedule, while cleaning inside your own room is less consistently included.

What's less consistent is everything past those two. Utilities, aircon servicing, and toiletries for shared bathrooms vary a lot by operator. None of this is written in stone anywhere, it's a business decision each operator makes, which is exactly why you check the actual listing rather than assume.

## What "All Inclusive" Really Means

"All inclusive" is a marketing term, not a legal one. No regulator defines what it has to cover, so it means whatever the operator decides. In practice, across most well-run Singapore listings, it typically covers room rent, wifi, electricity and water up to a reasonable cap, common area cleaning on a set schedule, and furniture already in the room.

It doesn't automatically mean unlimited electricity, a private cleaner for your own room, or free replacement of anything you break. It describes the billing structure, one flat monthly number instead of several separate bills, not a promise that every conceivable cost is absorbed. Read it as "bundled," not "unlimited."

## Inclusion Standards Across Operators in 2026

The market has matured enough that most established operators now bundle the basics: wifi, utilities to some threshold, common area cleaning. That baseline is close to table stakes. Where operators still differ meaningfully is aircon servicing, in-room cleaning, and what happens once a utility cap is exceeded. Some build quarterly aircon servicing into the rent as standard, others bill it separately as a small recurring fee. Neither approach is wrong, but they produce a different actual cost over a year, and only one shows up as a number on the listing page.

## All Inclusive vs Rent Plus Utilities

| | All-inclusive rent | Rent plus utilities |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly bills | One number | Rent, plus separate utility and sometimes wifi bills |
| Budgeting | Predictable, fixed | Variable, depends on usage and season |
| Setup | No accounts to open | May need your own utilities or wifi account |
| Overage risk | Fair use cap, rarely exceeded by one person | No cap, but you only pay for what you use |
| Best for | One clean number to plan around | Genuinely low usage, willing to manage bills |

Neither structure is objectively better. All-inclusive is easier to budget and needs no account setup, which matters if you're arriving from overseas with no local utility history. Rent plus utilities can work out marginally cheaper for low usage, at the cost of opening accounts and absorbing the monthly swing.

## 10 Things That Should Be Included and Often Are Not

Ask about each before signing. None are exotic, and a well-run listing should answer all ten without hesitation.

1. Wifi, and whether it's shared bandwidth or a dedicated connection
2. Electricity, and whether there's a usage cap
3. Water
4. Common area cleaning, and how often
5. In-room cleaning, if any
6. Aircon servicing, not just the unit itself but the maintenance
7. Basic furniture: bed, wardrobe, desk
8. Kitchen essentials in shared spaces, not your personal groceries
9. Toiletries or consumables for shared bathrooms
10. Building access, key cards, or door codes at no extra charge

Most listings cover the first five without issue. Numbers six through ten are where gaps most often appear, because they're easy to leave off a marketing page and only surface once you're living there.

## Myth: All Inclusive Means Unlimited

It doesn't, and this trips people up more than any other line in a co-living agreement. Nearly every all-inclusive bundle comes with a fair use cap, a maximum electricity or water usage baked into the flat rent, past which an operator can reasonably charge extra. This isn't a loophole, it's how an operator protects the bundle from one tenant running the aircon around the clock and quietly raising costs for everyone else in the house.

Fair use caps are normal, not a sign of a bad operator. What matters is whether the cap is disclosed upfront and set at a level an ordinary tenant won't realistically hit. Ask what the cap is, and what happens if you go over it, before you sign.

## Understanding a Co-Living Rate Card

A rate card is the breakdown behind the headline rent, even if it's not labelled that on the page. Split the number into two buckets: what's fixed no matter what (room, furniture, wifi), and what's conditional or billed separately (utilities past a cap, aircon servicing if unbundled). If a listing only shows the headline rent and skips that second bucket, ask for it directly at the viewing.

## Mistake: Assuming Aircon Servicing Is Covered

This is the single most common gap people miss. Aircon servicing, the maintenance visit, gas top-up, and chemical cleaning, is easy to assume falls under "all inclusive" because it sounds like a utility. In plenty of listings it doesn't. Some operators bill it separately, sometimes as a small recurring fee, sometimes only when the unit is actually serviced.

Ask specifically about aircon servicing, by name, rather than assuming it falls under "utilities included." If it's not mentioned on the listing, don't assume it's covered.

## The Inclusion Checklist to Run on Any Listing

Before committing to any room, run through this and get answers in writing:

- [ ] Is wifi included, and is it shared or dedicated bandwidth
- [ ] What's the utility cap, and what happens past it
- [ ] Is common area cleaning included, and how often
- [ ] Is cleaning inside my own room included
- [ ] Is aircon servicing included, specifically
- [ ] What furniture comes with the room
- [ ] Are toiletries or consumables provided for shared bathrooms
- [ ] Is there any fee for access cards, door codes, or building entry
- [ ] Is there a fair use policy, and what triggers an extra charge
- [ ] Is any of this written into the agreement, or only said verbally

A listing that answers all ten clearly, before you sign, is a good sign about how the rest of your tenancy will go. One that dodges half of them is worth pressing again, or walking away from.

## FAQ: Inclusions, the Questions People Ask on Viewings

**Is wifi really included, or is that just marketing?**
For most established operators, yes. Worth asking whether it's a dedicated connection or shared bandwidth, since that affects speed at peak hours.

**What happens if I use more electricity than the fair use cap?**
The operator typically bills the overage, usually calculated per person or unit above the cap. Ask what the cap is and how overage is calculated before signing.

**Is aircon servicing always included in all-inclusive rent?**
No. It's one of the most commonly assumed inclusions that isn't universal. Some operators bundle it, some bill it separately. Ask by name rather than assuming it falls under "utilities."

**Does all-inclusive rent mean I never pay anything extra?**
Not necessarily. Fair use caps, damage beyond normal wear, and optional extras like a private cleaner are common exceptions.

**Should I get the inclusion list in writing?**
Yes, always. A verbal answer at a viewing is easy to forget. Get it confirmed in the agreement before you sign.

**Do different rooms in the same building have different inclusions?**
Usually not, most operators apply one standard across a property. Still worth confirming per room, especially if rooms vary in size or aircon setup.
