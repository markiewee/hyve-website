Moving to Singapore from Bangladesh, what you can rent and where you can live depends heavily on which pass you're on. Employment Pass, S Pass, and Student Pass holders rent privately the same way anyone else does, and co-living is usually the fastest way to get a furnished room lined up. Work Permit holders in construction, marine, and process work differently, since their housing is arranged by their employer and approved by MOM before they even land. Knowing which category you're in changes almost everything about how you plan your first weeks.

Bangladeshis are one of the largest foreign communities in Singapore, spanning every pass tier, from engineers and finance professionals on Employment Passes to technicians on S Passes, students at NUS and NTU, and the workers who build much of the city's infrastructure. This guide breaks down what each pass actually allows, what a room costs, and the practical stuff, from banking to finding good biryani, that makes the first month easier.

## Which Pass Are You Moving On?

The pass you hold decides whether you rent a room yourself or whether your employer arranges where you sleep.

**Work Permit.** This covers most Bangladeshi workers in Singapore, largely in construction, marine shipyard, process, and services roles. There's no fixed minimum salary for a Work Permit, MOM instead regulates it through sector quotas and a monthly levy paid by the employer. Since September 2023, employers in construction, marine, and process sectors must prove acceptable housing before a Work Permit holder can enter Singapore, and that housing is almost always a registered dormitory arranged and paid for by the employer, not something the worker rents on their own.

**S Pass.** For mid-skilled roles, technicians, supervisors, and similar. The 2026 qualifying salary is S$3,300 a month in most sectors (S$3,800 in financial services), rising with age, and set to increase to S$3,600 (S$4,000 in financial services) for new applications from 1 January 2027. S Pass holders generally rent their own room, the same as anyone with a professional pass.

**Employment Pass (EP).** For professionals, managers, and executives. The 2026 qualifying salary floor is S$5,600 a month in most sectors (S$6,200 in financial services), rising with age and assessed under the COMPASS points framework. It's due to rise to S$6,000 (S$6,600 in financial services) from 1 January 2027. Bangladeshi professionals hold EPs across engineering, IT, finance, and shipping, sectors where Singapore's economy and Bangladesh's growing skilled workforce overlap.

**Student Pass.** For students enrolled at Singapore's universities and polytechnics. Bangladeshi students study across NUS, NTU, SMU, and the polytechnics, mostly in engineering, business, and computing. A Student Pass renews with your enrolment, so plan your lease length around that, not around a standard 12-month term.

## If You're on a Work Permit, Can You Still Rent Privately?

Sometimes, but it's the exception, not the default. MOM does allow non-dormitory accommodation, including HDB flats and private rentals, for Work Permit holders, but the employer has to apply for it, submit a tenancy agreement or owner's consent letter, and get it approved, which takes around six weeks compared to about a week for dormitory housing. In practice, most Work Permit holders in construction, marine, and process work live in employer-arranged dormitories, since that's the faster and cheaper path for the employer. If your situation is genuinely different, that's a conversation to have with your employer, not something you can arrange independently the way an EP or S Pass holder can.

This is also why co-living rooms in Singapore are realistically built for S Pass, EP, and Student Pass holders who rent as individuals. If you're coming to Singapore as a professional, a mid-skilled worker on an S Pass, or a student, the rest of this guide is for you.

## What Does a Room Actually Cost?

Room prices vary by type, location, and how central the neighbourhood is. The honest range across the market in 2026 looks roughly like this:

| Room type | Typical monthly rent |
|---|---|
| Compact or budget room | S$600 to S$900 |
| Common room | S$1,000 to S$1,600 |
| Master bedroom (ensuite) | S$1,700 to S$2,500+ |

Co-living rent usually bundles in wifi, utilities, and furnishing, so the number you see is close to your real monthly cost, unlike a bare HDB or condo room where those get billed separately on top. ## Why Co-Living Works Well for a First Move

If you're arriving on an S Pass, EP, or Student Pass, renting solo in Singapore usually means chasing a local guarantor, signing a 12-month tenancy, and furnishing an empty flat from nothing. That's a rough ask if you've landed with two bags and a start date next week.

- **No guarantor chase.** Most co-living operators verify your pass and income directly, rather than requiring a local guarantor the way some private landlords do for first-time renters.
- **Furnished from day one.** Bed, wardrobe, wifi, and often a shared kitchen are already set up.
- **Shorter, more flexible lease terms.** Useful if your Student Pass or S Pass doesn't comfortably cover a standard 12-month tenancy.
- **Built-in community.** Landing in a new country is easier with housemates around, even on the weeks you keep to yourself.

## Settling In: The Practical Stuff

A few things worth sorting in your first month, separate from the housing question.

**Banking.** DBS, OCBC, and UOB all open accounts for foreigners holding a valid pass and passport, usually in person at a branch once you have a residential address. Having your room confirmed before your bank appointment saves a step, since some branches ask for an address on file.

**Groceries and Bangladeshi essentials.** Little India, around Serangoon Road, Tekka Centre, and Mustafa Centre, is the main hub, with Bangladeshi restaurants, grocery stalls, and Mustafa's 24-hour stock of spices and staples. Sundays are the busiest day, since it's the standard off day for many workers, and you'll find the area full of people catching up, sending remittances, and eating together.

**SIM and connectivity.** A local SIM from Singtel, StarHub, or M1 needs your passport and pass on hand, and can usually be sorted same-day at a mall outlet.

**Remittance and community.** Money transfer agents and banks around Little India and Farrer Park handle remittances home, and the area has long served as a gathering point for Bangladesh's community in Singapore. The High Commission of Bangladesh in Singapore handles passport renewals and consular matters if you need them while you're here.

## Before You Sign: What to Check

- Your pass is issued and in hand, not just an offer letter or in-principle approval.
- The lease length doesn't outlast your pass validity, especially on a Student Pass or S Pass.
- If you're on a Work Permit, confirm with your employer whether your housing is dormitory-based or has been separately approved for private accommodation. Don't assume you can independently sign a room lease.
- Deposit terms and the refund process are written into the agreement, not just promised verbally.
- The room count and floor area match what's actually advertised. Our guide on [is co-living legal in Singapore](/blog/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore) covers the minimum stay and occupancy rules every legitimate room should already meet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can Bangladeshi Work Permit holders rent a private room in Singapore?**
Usually not independently. Most Work Permit holders in construction, marine, and process sectors live in employer-arranged dormitories, which MOM approves faster than private housing. Non-dormitory housing is possible but has to be applied for and approved through the employer.

**What's the minimum salary for an S Pass or Employment Pass in 2026?**
S Pass is S$3,300 a month in most sectors (S$3,800 in financial services), rising with age. Employment Pass is S$5,600 a month in most sectors (S$6,200 in financial services), also rising with age and assessed under COMPASS.

**How much should I budget for a room in Singapore?**
Realistically S$600 to S$2,500+ a month depending on room type and location, plus a deposit of around one month's rent. Check current listings for exact pricing since it moves by room and by month.

**Is there a large Bangladeshi community in Singapore?**
Yes, spanning Work Permit workers, S Pass and EP professionals, and students. Little India and the Farrer Park area are the main community and remittance hub, especially busy on Sundays.

**Do I need a guarantor to rent as a Bangladeshi national on an S Pass or EP?**
Not usually with a co-living operator, since they verify your pass and income directly. Some standalone landlords still ask for one, particularly for first-time renters.
