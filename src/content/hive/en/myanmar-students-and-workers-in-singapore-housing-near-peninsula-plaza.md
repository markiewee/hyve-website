For Myanmar students and workers in Singapore, the single most useful landmark to plan a home around is Peninsula Plaza, the mall near City Hall MRT that's been the community's gathering point since the early 1990s. You don't have to live inside the building or even in the same district. What matters is a straightforward MRT ride, ideally without transfers, since a mall trip that takes 45 minutes with two changes stops being a weekly habit and turns into an occasional one. This guide covers what's actually at Peninsula Plaza today, how far it really is from different parts of Singapore, and which areas make the trip painless.

## What Is Peninsula Plaza, and Is It Still the Community Hub?

Yes, and it has been for over three decades. Peninsula Plaza is a mixed-use building at 111 North Bridge Road, a two-minute walk from City Hall MRT, and it became known as Singapore's "Little Myanmar" starting in the early 1990s when Myanmar residents began gathering there regularly ([The Smart Local](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/peninsula-plaza-singapore/)). That role hasn't faded. The basement through level 3 are still packed with Burmese restaurants and grocery shops, level 3 to 5 have hair salons that specialise in Myanmar-style cuts and treatments, and the mall is dotted with mobile phone shops, travel agencies, visa agents, and money changers ([The Smart Local](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/peninsula-plaza-singapore/); [Peninsula Plaza](https://www.peninsulaplaza.com.sg/2026/05/19/why-peninsula-plaza-is-known-as-little-myanmar-in-singapore/)).

Weekends are when it comes alive. That's when most of the community has a day off, and the mall fills with people shopping for groceries and clothing, eating food cooked by Myanmar chefs, sending money home through the remittance counters, or simply catching up with friends ([Johor Kaki](https://johorkaki.blogspot.com/2023/07/little-myanmar-peninsula-plaza-near.html)). If you're new to Singapore and haven't found your footing yet, it's a genuinely useful place to start.

## What You'll Actually Find Inside

A rough sense of the layout helps before your first visit, since the building is older and the signage isn't always intuitive:

- **Basement to Level 3**: Burmese restaurants (Mandalay Style Myanmar Restaurant, Inle Myanmar Restaurant, Maw Shan, and Ye Yint Cafe are regularly mentioned favourites) and grocery shops carrying imported snacks, seasonings, and pantry staples you won't find in an ordinary Singapore supermarket ([The Smart Local](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/peninsula-plaza-singapore/)).
- **Levels 3 to 5**: Hair and beauty salons, several run by Myanmar stylists.
- **Throughout the building**: Mobile phone and SIM shops, travel and visa agencies, money changers, and remittance counters for sending money home.
- **Weekends specifically**: The busiest window, with the widest range of stalls and the strongest sense of community atmosphere.

None of this requires you to live in the Peninsula Plaza building itself, which is mostly commercial and office space rather than residential. What you actually want is a home base with an easy ride to City Hall MRT.

## How Far Is Peninsula Plaza From Where You'll Actually Live?

This is the part most housing guides skip, and it's the part that decides whether a weekly grocery run stays a weekly grocery run or quietly stops happening. City Hall MRT sits on both the North-South and East-West lines, right in the middle of the network, which means a lot of areas reach it directly with no transfer at all.

| From | Nearest MRT | Line | To City Hall | Transfer? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jurong East area | Chinese Garden | East-West Line | Roughly 30 to 35 min | No, direct |
| Ang Mo Kio / Yio Chu Kang area | Yio Chu Kang | North-South Line | Roughly 35 to 40 min | No, direct |
| Serangoon area | Lorong Chuan (Circle Line) | Circle Line, then interchange | Roughly 30 to 35 min | Yes, one transfer |

The pattern worth noticing: anywhere on the East-West or North-South line reaches City Hall without changing trains, since City Hall is the interchange station for both. Areas that rely on the Circle, Downtown, Thomson-East Coast, or North East lines will usually need one transfer, which typically adds 5 to 10 minutes rather than derailing the trip entirely. One transfer is still a normal, workable commute for a regular visit. Two transfers is where it starts feeling like a special occasion rather than a routine.

## Do You Need to Live Right Next to Peninsula Plaza?

No, and trying to isn't usually worth it. Peninsula Plaza sits in the Downtown Core, a commercial and office district with very little residential housing nearby and higher rents where housing does exist. Most students and workers are better served by living in an established residential neighbourhood with a clean MRT connection, and treating Peninsula Plaza as a weekend or bi-weekly trip rather than a daily errand.

What actually matters for day-to-day life is a normal residential area with its own groceries, food options, and amenities close by, with Peninsula Plaza as a reachable extra rather than the anchor for every decision. That's a more sustainable way to plan a home base than chasing proximity to one mall.

## What to Check Before You Sign

A few practical points worth confirming regardless of where in Singapore you end up:

- **Your commute on paper versus in practice.** MRT time estimates don't include the walk to and from stations at either end. Add a few minutes on both sides before you treat a number as final.
- **Whether your pass type affects where you can rent.** Work permit, S Pass, Employment Pass, or Student Pass holders all face slightly different practical realities when renting, so confirm what your specific pass allows before committing to a lease.
- **What's actually included in the rent.** Utilities, wifi, cleaning, and furnishing vary a lot between listings that look similar on the surface. Ask directly rather than assuming.
- **How the deposit and notice period work.** Get this in writing before you hand over any money, not after.

**Is Peninsula Plaza still the main Myanmar community hub in Singapore?**
Yes. It's held that role since the early 1990s and continues to house Burmese restaurants, grocery shops, salons, and remittance services, with weekends being the busiest and most community-driven time to visit ([The Smart Local](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/peninsula-plaza-singapore/); [Johor Kaki](https://johorkaki.blogspot.com/2023/07/little-myanmar-peninsula-plaza-near.html)).

**Which MRT station is Peninsula Plaza closest to?**
City Hall MRT, an interchange for the North-South and East-West lines, about a two-minute walk away.

**Do I need to live in the Downtown Core to be near Peninsula Plaza?**
No. Areas on the North-South or East-West line reach City Hall directly with no transfer, which is usually a better trade-off than paying Downtown Core rents to live a few minutes closer.

**Can I send money home from Peninsula Plaza?**
Yes, the mall has remittance counters and money changers alongside its restaurants, grocery shops, and other services, though hours and specific providers are worth confirming directly since these can change.
