Moving to Singapore from China is straightforward once you have the right pass in hand, but the housing part trips a lot of people up because it depends on a document you might not have yet. A Chinese ordinary passport gets you 30 days visa-free entry, enough to fly in, view rooms, and open a bank account queue, but it does not let you sign a lease. That needs a Student Pass, Employment Pass, or S Pass actually issued, not just an offer letter or an in-principle approval.

For most students and early-career workers landing from China, the fastest, lowest-risk way to cover that gap is a furnished co-living room booked for the first three to six months, then a decision on where to settle once the pass is confirmed and the job or course has actually started.

## Do I Need a Visa Just to Enter Singapore From China?

No, not for a short visit. Since 9 February 2024, holders of ordinary passports issued by the People's Republic of China get visa-free entry to Singapore for stays of up to 30 days. That covers flying in for a university orientation, a job interview, or a room viewing before your pass is issued.

It does not cover actually living here. Entry visa-free and holding a valid immigration pass are two different things, and only the second one lets you sign a residential lease. If you're arriving on the 30-day visa-free entry to sort out housing before your Student Pass or work pass comes through, a short-term co-living booking bridges that gap far better than a hotel does on cost.

## Which Pass Do I Actually Need Before I Can Rent?

For students, it's a Student Pass, applied for by your school through ICA's SOLAR system once you have an offer. Processing typically takes four to eight weeks, and the pass fee is S$90. Since 2023, ICA issues it as a digital pass rather than a physical card.

For early-career workers, it's an Employment Pass or S Pass, sponsored by your employer. In 2026 the general Employment Pass salary floor is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), scaled up for older applicants. The S Pass floor for younger applicants sits at S$3,300 a month (S$3,800 in financial services).

None of these passes exist until they're actually issued. An offer letter, an In-Principle Approval, or a job contract is not the same thing, and no properly run operator will let you sign a lease on the strength of any of those alone. ## How Far Ahead Should I Book Housing?

Line your housing search up against your pass timeline, not your flight date. A Student Pass typically clears four to eight weeks after your school submits it. Employer-sponsored passes move on a similar timeline, sometimes faster if your employer's application track record is strong.

Book a short, flexible arrangement to land in, then commit to a longer lease once your pass is confirmed and you know your actual start date. This is also why co-living suits this stage better than a standard 12-month private tenancy: you're not locking into a long, rigid contract before you even know your pass issue date.

## What Does Housing Actually Cost?

Rooms in Singapore's shared housing market currently run from about S$600 for a basic budget room up to S$2,500 for a private ensuite, with most standard common rooms landing between S$1,000 and S$1,600 a month. Co-living rooms, furnished and often bundled with utilities and wifi, typically sit in the S$1,200 to S$2,500+ range depending on the operator and location.

Add food, transport, and a local SIM, and a realistic all-in monthly budget for a newcomer renting a room lands somewhere between S$1,800 and S$2,800. For upfront cash, plan for a security deposit (standard practice is one month's rent) plus your first month, so two months' rent in hand before you get the keys is the safe assumption. We've broken this all-inclusive-vs-separate-bills comparison down further in our guide to [security deposits in Singapore co-living](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

An all-inclusive co-living room removes a lot of the guesswork that's hardest to plan for from outside Singapore, since one number covers rent, utilities, and wifi instead of five separate bills you've never paid in Singapore dollars before.

## On-Campus Hall or Off-Campus Co-Living?

Singapore's universities attract more than 100,000 international students a year, and Chinese students make up roughly 30 percent of that number, one of the largest single cohorts on most campuses. Demand for on-campus housing has consistently outpaced supply in recent years, and several universities now openly tell incoming students, including exchange and postgraduate students, to plan for off-campus housing rather than assume a hall place is guaranteed.

Practically, that means most students arriving from China should treat off-campus housing as the default plan, not the backup. A co-living room near campus or on a direct MRT line gives you a furnished, ready-to-move-in setup without waiting on a hall allocation that may not come through.

## Opening a Bank Account and Getting Set Up

You'll need your issued pass, not just an offer letter, to open an account at most banks. DBS accepts applications online and will work from an ICA In-Principle Approval plus a school certification letter if your pass hasn't landed yet, while OCBC's acceptance of pre-pass documents varies by nationality and pass type, and UOB generally expects a branch visit with your pass already in hand. Have your passport and proof of local address (your co-living agreement or tenancy contract works for this) ready either way.

Day-to-day payments are easier than a lot of people expect. UnionPay is accepted at more than 10,000 merchants across Singapore, so you're not starting from zero on the payments side even before your local bank account clears.

## The Tuition Grant Bond, and Why It Matters for Housing

If you're a Chinese student on Singapore's Tuition Grant Scheme, worth knowing early: accepting the subsidised tuition option comes with a three-year bond requiring full-time employment at a Singapore-registered entity after you graduate. It's flexible across employers, but the clock starts the moment your studies end.

The practical housing implication is that a lot of Chinese students who start on a Student Pass end up staying in Singapore straight into their first job on an Employment Pass or S Pass, without a gap between the two. If that's your likely path, it's worth choosing housing with month-to-month flexibility from the start, rather than a rigid annual lease that fights against a status change you can already see coming.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The most common one is booking a rigid, long lease before your pass is actually issued, then scrambling when the pass date shifts by a few weeks, which happens often enough to plan around. A second is assuming the 30-day visa-free entry means you can settle into a normal lease right away; it gets you in the door, nothing more. A third is only starting the off-campus search after a hall application gets rejected, rather than running both in parallel from the start.

## A Quick Checklist Before You Book Anything

- [ ] Confirm your pass application is submitted and get a realistic issue-date estimate from your school or employer
- [ ] Budget two months' rent in cash for deposit plus first month, on top of flight and settling-in costs
- [ ] Check what's actually included in any room price (utilities, wifi, cleaning) before comparing two listings on rent alone
- [ ] Start your off-campus housing search in parallel with any on-campus application, not after it's rejected
- [ ] Have your passport and a local address ready for your bank account application the week you land
- [ ] If you're on the Tuition Grant Scheme, favour flexible, shorter-term housing over a rigid annual lease

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I rent a room in Singapore before my Student Pass is issued?**
No. You need the pass itself in hand to sign a standard lease or licence agreement. A short-term booking that doesn't require a long-term pass is the practical way to bridge the gap while your pass processes.

**Do I need a visa to fly to Singapore from China for a viewing?**
No, not for a short trip. Ordinary Chinese passport holders get 30 days visa-free entry, which is enough time to view rooms and sort paperwork, but not enough to count as a long-term stay.

**Is it hard for Chinese students to get on-campus housing in Singapore?**
Demand regularly outstrips supply, and Chinese students make up a large share of the international student population, so treating off-campus housing as your primary plan rather than a fallback is the safer approach.

**Can I open a Singapore bank account before my pass arrives?**
Some banks, DBS in particular, will start the process using an ICA In-Principle Approval and a school certification letter. Most still want the actual pass before the account is fully active.

**Does the Tuition Grant bond affect what kind of lease I should sign?**
Indirectly, yes. If you're likely to move straight from a Student Pass into your first job in Singapore, a flexible, shorter lease matches that transition better than a rigid 12-month tenancy signed while you're still studying.
