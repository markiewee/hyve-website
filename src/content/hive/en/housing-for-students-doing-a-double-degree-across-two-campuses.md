If your double degree splits your timetable across two campuses, the right room is the one that keeps both commutes reasonable for the years you're actually enrolled, not the one that's closest to whichever campus you spend the most hours at this semester. A double degree is a multi-year commitment, and your class mix between the two campuses will shift from year to year. Pick housing for the whole degree, not for this semester's timetable, and check the split before you lock in a lease that runs longer than the pattern holds.

That's the short version. The rest of this covers what a genuine double degree across two campuses actually looks like in Singapore, why it's a different housing problem from a single exchange semester, and how to weigh a room against a multi-year commute instead of a four-month one.

## What Actually Counts as a "Double Degree Across Two Campuses"

A double degree is two separate degrees, usually awarded by the end of a longer programme, not one degree with a minor or a study trip bolted on. The clearest local example is NUS's Double Degree Programme in Law and Business Administration, where Law modules run out of NUS Law's Bukit Timah campus and the Business modules run out of NUS Business School at Kent Ridge. NUS Law also runs double degrees pairing Law with Economics and with Life Sciences, all of which carry the same structure: core law teaching stays at Bukit Timah, the second discipline sits with its own faculty at Kent Ridge.

That's a genuinely different housing problem from a single exchange semester or a one-off elective at another school. If your split timetable is a one-off, our guide to [housing for students attending multiple campuses in one semester](https://lazybee.sg/blog/housing-for-students-attending-multiple-campuses-in-one-semester) covers that lighter version. A double degree is not that. You're not managing one unusual term. You're managing a multi-year pattern where the ratio of time at each campus changes by year, sometimes by semester, for as long as the double degree runs.

Some double degrees pair a Singapore campus with an overseas partner instead of a second local site; NTU's Nanyang Business School has run double degree partnerships with overseas universities including Waseda in Japan. Those usually work in blocks, a semester or a full year based abroad, rather than a weekly split between two Singapore addresses. That structure is a different housing question, what to do with your Singapore room while you're overseas, and it's not what this piece covers.

| Programme shape | Example | Housing question |
|---|---|---|
| Two Singapore campuses, weekly split, multi-year | NUS Double Degree in Law and Business Administration. Law modules at Bukit Timah, Business modules at Kent Ridge. NUS Law also pairs Law with Economics and with Life Sciences on the same structure | What this guide covers: a base that stays reasonable across a shifting multi-year split |
| One Singapore campus plus an overseas partner, in blocks | NTU Nanyang Business School double degrees with partners including Waseda in Japan | A different question: what to do with your Singapore room while you are abroad for a semester or year |
| A single exchange semester or one-off elective at another site | Any faculty | Optimise hard for that one term, and time the lease to it |

This piece is for the first kind: two campuses, both in Singapore, both in your weekly rotation for years at a stretch.

## Why This Is Different From "Multiple Campuses in One Semester"

A student on a one-semester exchange, or one taking a single elective at another school, is optimising for four or five months. Whatever room they pick, the commute pattern ends on a known date, and the lease can be timed to match it.

A double degree student is optimising for years. The room you pick in year one might still be your room in year three, by which point the balance of your timetable between the two campuses could have flipped. Law-Business double degree students, for instance, typically front-load more law modules in earlier years and shift toward business modules later, or vice versa depending on how the degree is sequenced. A room that was perfectly placed for year one's mix isn't automatically right for year three's.

That's the core reason to plan differently here than for a short cross-campus stint. A single semester rewards optimising hard for that semester's exact timetable. A multi-year double degree rewards a room that stays reasonable across a range of splits, since you can't be certain which split you'll be living with by your final year.

## The Two Ways to House a Two-Campus Timetable

There are really only two strategies, and they trade off against each other.

**Live centrally, accept a moderate commute to both.** Instead of optimising for whichever campus dominates this semester, pick a location that keeps both commutes inside a workable range for the whole degree. Neither trip is the shortest possible, but neither one becomes unworkable if your timetable balance shifts. We've written a full breakdown of that trade-off, including the actual commute-time math, in our [guide to living centrally versus near a single campus](https://lazybee.sg/blog/commute-comparison-living-centrally-vs-near-a-single-campus).

**Anchor near the campus you'll spend more years favouring, and accept the other commute is longer.** This works if your double degree's structure is lopsided by design, more years weighted toward one faculty than the other, and you can look at the actual module sequencing ahead of time rather than guessing. It's a bet on a known pattern, not a hedge against an unknown one.

| Strategy | How it works | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Live centrally, accept a moderate commute to both | Neither trip is the shortest possible, but neither becomes unworkable if your timetable balance shifts | You cannot see the year-by-year split in advance |
| Anchor near the campus you will favour over more years | A bet on a known pattern rather than a hedge against an unknown one | Your degree is lopsided by design and your programme office can show you the actual module sequencing |

Neither strategy is automatically right. What decides it is whether you can see your degree's actual year-by-year split before you commit to a lease. If your programme office or faculty advisor can tell you roughly how the module load shifts year to year, that's worth asking for before you sign anything, since it turns a guess into a plan.

## Matching Lease Length to a Multi-Year Programme

Most private housing conversations in Singapore assume a semester or a one-year lease. A double degree runs longer than either, which changes what you should be negotiating for.

Locking a multi-year rate isn't standard here, and most operators, co-living included, price on a rolling basis rather than a multi-year contract. What you can do is treat each lease renewal as a checkpoint, reassessing your commute needs against next year's actual module mix instead of assuming the room that worked in year one still fits by year three.

This also flips the usual minimum-stay question. Under Singapore's URA rules, private rentals, co-living included, cannot legally run under three months. For most students that floor is the binding constraint. For a double degree student planning several years in the same city, it barely matters. The real question is how often you're willing to move, not how short a stay you can get away with.

## What to Actually Check Before You Commit

A few things matter more for a multi-year, two-campus arrangement than they would for a shorter stay:

- **Get the real year-by-year module split**, not just this semester's timetable, from your faculty or programme coordinator before picking a location.
- **Test the commute to both campuses at actual class times**, not off-peak, since a double degree student is more likely to have back-to-back classes across both sites on the same day than a single-campus student is.
- **Check the shuttle bus situation at both ends.** NUS Kent Ridge and NUS Bukit Timah, for instance, are on different internal shuttle networks, and getting between the two isn't a five-minute internal hop.
- **Ask what happens at lease renewal**, not just at move-in, since you're planning for a programme that outlasts a single lease term.
- **Confirm the room comes furnished and utilities-inclusive** if you're the kind of student who'd rather not re-set-up a room every time you reassess location, since a fully furnished co-living room makes a location swap between years far less painful than moving furniture would.

| Check | Why it matters over years rather than months |
|---|---|
| The real year-by-year module split | This semester's timetable is not the whole degree. Ask your faculty or programme coordinator before picking a location |
| Both commutes at actual class times | A double degree student is more likely to have back-to-back classes across both sites on the same day |
| The shuttle bus situation at both ends | NUS Kent Ridge and NUS Bukit Timah sit on different internal shuttle networks. Getting between them is not a five-minute internal hop |
| What happens at lease renewal | You are planning for a programme that outlasts a single lease term |
| Furnished and utilities-inclusive | Makes a location swap between years far less painful than moving furniture |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a double degree across two campuses the same as a joint or dual degree with an overseas university?**
No. A local double degree like NUS Law and Business Administration keeps you in Singapore the whole time, splitting your week between two Singapore campuses. A joint degree with an overseas partner, such as NTU's business double degree arrangements with partner universities abroad, typically moves you overseas for a full semester or year at a stretch instead of a weekly split. The housing planning is different for each.

**Should I live near the campus I spend more time at this semester?**
Only if that split is representative of your whole degree, not just this semester's timetable. Check the year-by-year module sequencing with your faculty before deciding, since double degree programmes often shift their balance across years.

**Can I just move between campuses each year as my timetable shifts?**
Yes, and for a multi-year programme that's a reasonable approach, as long as you're choosing furnished, all-in-inclusive rooms that don't punish you for moving. Treat each lease renewal as a fresh decision point rather than committing to one location for the whole degree upfront.

**Does the three-month rental minimum affect double degree students differently?**
Not really, since a multi-year student is almost never planning a stay anywhere near that floor. The minimum matters more for exchange or short-course students. For a double degree, the real question is how often you're willing to relocate, not how short a lease you can find.
