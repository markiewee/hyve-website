If you've moved to Singapore on a Dependant's Pass because your partner got the job, the housing search is usually yours to run. Your partner is heads down in a new role during the exact weeks a lease decision needs making, and you're often doing it without a local network, without a local income yet, and without full certainty about which neighbourhood will actually work once you both know the commute and the routine. The practical fix is the same one that works for any decision made under uncertainty: don't lock in long before you've lived here.

That's the core of this piece. Not the Employment Pass holder's move, but yours, the one actually walking through the viewings.

## What Pass Are You On, and What Does It Let You Do?

Your partner's Employment Pass (EP) is what makes your stay possible. To sponsor you as a spouse on a Dependant's Pass (DP), they need to earn a minimum fixed monthly salary of S$6,000. That's their individual salary, not household income, and it's separate from the minimum salary needed to qualify for the EP itself. [Source: MOM, Dependant's Pass eligibility](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/dependants-pass/eligibility)

A DP covers a legally married spouse and unmarried children under 21. If you're an unmarried partner, DP isn't available to you, and you'd typically be looking at a Long-Term Visit Pass instead, which comes with its own separate eligibility path. Worth confirming directly with your partner's HR or an immigration adviser before you assume DP status.

**What the DP actually gives you:**

- The right to live in Singapore for as long as the pass is valid, tied to your partner's EP.
- No automatic right to work, that's a separate step (below).
- The right to sign leases, open bank accounts, and otherwise operate as a resident, once you have your DP in hand.

| | Dependant's Pass | Long-Term Visit Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Who it covers | A legally married spouse, and unmarried children under 21 | Typically an unmarried partner, on a separate eligibility path |
| Sponsor requirement | The EP holder must earn a minimum fixed monthly salary of S$6,000. That is their individual salary, not household income, and separate from the EP's own qualifying salary | Its own criteria |
| Right to live here | Yes, for as long as the pass is valid, tied to your partner's EP | Yes, per its own terms |
| Right to work | None automatically. That is a separate step | Separate again |
| Right to sign leases and open bank accounts | Yes, once the pass is in hand | Confirm with HR or an immigration adviser |

## Can You Work on a Dependant's Pass?

Yes, but the pathway depends on what kind of work you're doing, and this is where a lot of DP holders get confused because the terminology has shifted over the years.

**If you'll be employed by a company**, MOM assesses you the same way it assesses any work pass applicant. If you qualify on your own merits for an Employment Pass or S Pass, your employer applies for that directly, and your DP gets cancelled once the new pass is issued. If you don't meet EP or S Pass criteria, there's a separate DP-Work Permit (DP-WP) route: it has no minimum salary requirement, is open regardless of nationality, and its validity is tied to your DP. It does come with sector-specific quotas and levies your employer needs to factor in. [Source: MOM, working in Singapore on a Dependant's Pass](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/dependants-pass/working-in-singapore)

**If you want to set up or run your own business**, that's the Letter of Consent (LOC), a specific approval for DP holders operating as self-employed or business owners rather than employees. It's a separate application from the employment routes above. [Source: MOM, Letter of Consent for Dependant's Pass business owners](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/loc-for-dependants-pass-business-owners)

| Your situation | The route | Key detail |
|---|---|---|
| You qualify for an EP or S Pass on your own merits | Your employer applies for that pass directly | Your DP is cancelled once the new pass is issued |
| You will be employed but do not meet EP or S Pass criteria | DP-Work Permit (DP-WP) | No minimum salary, open regardless of nationality, validity tied to your DP. Comes with sector-specific quotas and levies your employer must factor in |
| You want to set up or run your own business | Letter of Consent (LOC) | A separate application, for DP holders operating as self-employed or business owners rather than employees |

The practical upshot: you almost certainly won't have work authorisation sorted on day one. Between landing, sorting your DP, and then sorting whichever work pathway applies to you, there's a real stretch of time where you have no local income and no local job to anchor your housing search around. That's normal. It's also exactly why a long lease is the wrong instrument for this period.

## Why the Housing Search Usually Falls to You

Three things are true for most trailing spouses and partners, and none of them are true for the EP holder who's already got a desk to go to.

**You're the one with the daytime hours.** Your partner is in onboarding meetings. You're the one who can actually go to viewings, meet agents, and walk a neighbourhood at 11am on a Tuesday to see what it's really like.

**You don't have a network to lean on yet.** No colleagues to ask "which area do you live in," no local friend to sanity-check a listing. You're often making a five-figure decision (deposit, first month, agent's fee) based on portals and reviews alone.

**You don't know your own routine yet.** If you're planning to look for work, volunteer, study, or just explore the city before deciding, you don't yet know which part of Singapore will actually suit your day. Committing to a specific address before you've lived here even a few weeks is a guess dressed up as a decision.

|---|---|
| You have the daytime hours | Your partner is in onboarding meetings. You are the one who can go to viewings and walk a neighbourhood at 11am on a Tuesday |
| You have no local network yet | No colleagues to ask which area they live in, no local friend to sanity-check a listing. Often a five-figure decision on deposit, first month and agent's fee made from portals alone |
| You do not know your own routine yet | Work, volunteering, study or just exploring. Committing to an address before you have lived here a few weeks is a guess dressed up as a decision |

None of this is a criticism of the process. It's just the structural reality of trailing on someone else's pass, and it's worth naming plainly instead of muddling through it.

## Why a Long Lease Is the Wrong First Move

A standard Singapore tenancy asks for a commitment of six months to two years, a deposit of one to two months' rent, and often an agent's fee, all signed and paid before you've had time to test whether the location actually works for your life here. If your partner's role changes, if you decide you want to be nearer a school, a gym, a friend you've since made, or simply nearer the MRT line you actually use, breaking that lease early usually means losing the deposit.

Co-living rooms are built for exactly this window. Shorter minimum stays, a lighter deposit, and a room that's already furnished with wifi and utilities sorted, so you're not making furnishing decisions in a country you've lived in for two weeks. You get a real base to operate from immediately, while you take the time to actually learn the city before committing to where you want to be longer term.

It also solves a coordination problem specific to trailing partners: you may be choosing housing before you know where your own work, if any, will end up being. A flexible room lets that resolve itself without forcing a premature address decision.

## FAQ

**Does my partner's Employment Pass automatically qualify me for a Dependant's Pass?**
Only if their fixed monthly salary meets the S$6,000 threshold MOM requires to sponsor a spouse. If their EP salary sits below that, you may not qualify for a DP even though they hold a valid EP, so it's worth confirming eligibility before assuming your pass status.

**Can I work in Singapore on a Dependant's Pass?**
Yes, but not automatically. If you qualify for an Employment Pass or S Pass on your own merits, your employer applies for that directly. If you don't, the DP-Work Permit route applies, subject to sector quotas and levies your employer arranges. Running your own business instead requires a separate Letter of Consent.

**Should I sign a full one-year lease before I've settled in?**
Not until you actually know the city. Most trailing spouses and partners are making the housing decision with the least local knowledge in the household, so a shorter, flexible commitment that lets you relocate once you understand the commute and the neighbourhood beats locking in on a guess.

**Who typically handles the housing search, the EP holder or the accompanying partner?**
In most households it lands on the accompanying partner, simply because they have the daytime hours the working partner doesn't during the first weeks of a new job. It's worth deciding this explicitly as a couple rather than defaulting into it.
