If you're a second or third year student at NUS or NTU wondering why your hall application didn't come through, it's not you. Both universities only guarantee a hall place for a student's first two years of study. After that, staying on campus depends on CCA points and a competitive ballot, and plenty of students who want to stay simply don't get back in. This guide is the checklist for what to do next: when to start looking, what to budget, and what actually changes once you're off campus.

## Why you're being moved out now, not in year one

This isn't a punishment or a paperwork error. NUS states its housing guarantee covers a student's first two years, after which continued accommodation depends on participation in hall and co-curricular activities. NTU runs the same structure: a guaranteed two year hall stay for new students, with seniors needing to accumulate CCA points to be considered for the following year.

| | NUS | NTU |
|---|---|---|
| Guaranteed hall period | First two years | First two years, for eligible new students |
| After the guarantee | Continued accommodation depends on participation in hall and co-curricular activities, allocated by ballot | Seniors need CCA points, generally in the higher single digits, to be considered |
| When it bites | Year 3, the first year the guarantee has fully expired for most cohorts | Same |

In practice, that means everyone who didn't rack up enough points, or simply didn't want to spend two more years chasing them, is pushed into the open rental market at roughly the same time. Year 3 is when this hits hardest, since it's the first year the guarantee has fully expired for most cohorts.

Knowing this in advance is the whole advantage. Students who get blindsided by a failed ballot in July are hunting for a room with three weeks to semester start. Students who plan for it in March have their pick of the market.

## The moving-out checklist

Work through this roughly in order. Items near the top need the longest lead time.

**1. Check your actual hall status early.** Don't wait for the ballot result to start thinking about this. If your CCA points are borderline, or you already know you're not reapplying, treat yourself as moving out the moment you know, not the moment the offer letter confirms it.

**2. Set a real budget, not a hopeful one.** Off campus rent in Singapore runs well above hall fees. A shared room in an HDB flat typically lands in the SGD 700 to 1,200 a month range, while managed studios or co-living rooms tend to sit higher, generally SGD 1,200 to 1,800, against on-campus fees that are usually SGD 500 to 1,000. Build your number around what you can sustain for a full semester, not what looks fine for the first month.

**3. Add setup costs on top of monthly rent.** A security deposit (commonly one to two months' rent) and possibly an advance month upfront are normal. Furnished co-living rooms cut this down since you're not separately buying a bed, desk, or wardrobe, but budget for it as a lump sum you need before you sign anything, not something you can pay in instalments.

| Option | Typical monthly cost | What is bundled |
|---|---|---|
| On-campus hall | SGD 500 to 1,000 | Wi-Fi, cleaning of common areas, laundry facilities, usually some meal plan or canteen access |
| Shared room in an HDB flat | SGD 700 to 1,200 | Usually nothing. Furniture, utilities and Wi-Fi are yours to arrange |
| Managed studio or co-living room | SGD 1,200 to 1,800 | Typically furnished, with utilities, Wi-Fi and cleaning in the rate |

On top of monthly rent, budget a security deposit of commonly one to two months' rent, and possibly an advance month, as a lump sum needed before you sign. A furnished co-living room cuts this down since you are not separately buying a bed, desk or wardrobe.

**4. Decide what you actually need vs what hall gave you for free.** Hall bundled in Wi-Fi, cleaning of common areas, laundry facilities, and usually some meal plan or canteen access. Off campus, each of those is either something you arrange yourself or something that's bundled into a co-living rate. Know which one you're picking before you compare prices, since an unfurnished room that looks cheaper on paper can end up costing more once you've bought furniture and set up your own utilities and Wi-Fi accounts.

**5. Start searching 6 to 8 weeks before you need to move.** Rooms near campus, especially anything walkable to NUS or NTU, get taken fast in the weeks before each semester starts. Waiting until the ballot result confirms you're out means you're searching in the same window as everyone else in your position. Starting earlier gives you first pick and more negotiating room.

**6. Shortlist by commute, not just price.** Your commute is now a daily cost in time and transport fare that hall never charged you. A room that's SGD 100 cheaper but adds 40 minutes each way to your commute is rarely the better deal once you factor in the semester.

**7. Check what's actually included before you compare two rooms.** "SGD X a month" means different things depending on the operator. Ask specifically whether utilities, Wi-Fi, and cleaning are in the rate or billed separately, since that gap is where a lot of student budgets get blown. Our guide on [what's actually included in co-living rent](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/whats-included-in-coliving-rent-singapore) breaks down what to check line by line.

**8. Read the lease term before you sign.** Hall was a semester or academic year commitment with a fixed end date. Off campus tenancy or licence terms vary a lot: some ask for six or twelve months, others (particularly co-living operators) offer shorter, more flexible terms that better match a student's actual timeline. If your graduation or exchange plans are still uncertain, a long, rigid lease is the wrong fit even if the price looks attractive.

**9. Understand the deposit and exit terms.** Know exactly what triggers a deduction and how much notice you owe if you need to leave early. This matters more for students than most other renters, since semester breaks, exchange programmes, and internships abroad can all shift your actual move-out date. Our piece on [security deposits in Singapore co-living](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore) covers what's standard.

**10. Line up your move for the gap between semesters, not mid-term.** Moving during a busy academic week is its own kind of stress on top of exams and coursework. If your ballot result or hall notice gives you any flexibility on timing, use the semester break to actually move, not the first week back.

## What changes day to day once you're off campus

Hall life has a built-in social structure: your floor, your CCA, people two doors down at 11pm before a deadline. Moving out doesn't remove that need, it just means you have to build it deliberately instead of getting it by default.

This is one reason co-living tends to suit students moving out of halls better than a solo rental. A shared house with a handful of other tenants, common areas, and someone else handling maintenance and cleaning keeps a version of that structure intact, without the CCA points requirement or the annual ballot uncertainty that pushed you out of hall in the first place.

The other shift is admin. Hall handled Wi-Fi setup, utility billing, and basic maintenance requests through one office. Off campus, that's either on you or bundled into your rent depending on what you picked in step 4 above. Co-living rooms that fold utilities, Wi-Fi, and cleaning into one monthly rate are worth weighing against the DIY version once you've actually priced out setting all of that up yourself.

| | In hall | Off campus |
|---|---|---|
| Social structure | Built in: your floor, your CCA, people two doors down at 11pm before a deadline | Built deliberately. A shared co-living house keeps a version of it without the CCA points or annual ballot |
| Wi-Fi, utilities, maintenance | Handled through one office | Either on you, or bundled into your rent depending on what you picked |
| Commitment | A semester or academic year with a fixed end date | Six or twelve months from many landlords, shorter and more flexible from many co-living operators |
| Commute | None | A daily cost in time and fare that hall never charged you |

## FAQ

**How long does NUS guarantee hall accommodation for?**
NUS guarantees housing for a student's first two years. After that, continued accommodation depends on CCA points and participation in hall activities, allocated through balloting.

**How long does NTU guarantee hall accommodation for?**
NTU guarantees a hall place for the first two years for eligible new students. Seniors need CCA points, generally in the higher single digits, to be considered for a place in later years.

**Why did I lose my hall place in year 2 or 3?**
Most likely your CCA points weren't high enough for the ballot, or you're past the two-year guarantee window that both universities apply to new students.

**Is off-campus rent much more expensive than hall?**
Yes, typically. Hall fees generally run SGD 500 to 1,000 a month, while shared off-campus rooms run higher, and managed studios or co-living rooms higher again, though co-living rates usually bundle in utilities and Wi-Fi that hall fees also covered.

**When should I start looking for a room after failing the hall ballot?**
As soon as you know, ideally 6 to 8 weeks before you need to move. Rooms near campus fill up fastest in the weeks right before each semester starts.

**Is co-living a good fit for students moving out of halls?**
It's a common choice for exactly this transition, since it keeps a shared-house social structure similar to hall, comes furnished, and usually offers shorter lease terms than a standard private tenancy.
