Moving to Singapore from South Korea is easier than most relocations, since Korean passport holders already get 90 days visa-free entry, more than enough time to fly in, view rooms, and open a bank account queue. The part that actually matters is housing, and it depends on a document you might not have yet. Entering visa-free doesn't let you sign a lease. You need a Student Pass, Employment Pass, or S Pass actually issued, not just an offer letter or an in-principle approval.

For most people arriving from South Korea, whether for a corporate posting, a startup role, or a graduate programme, the practical fix is a furnished co-living room booked for the first three to six months, then a longer-term decision once the pass is confirmed and work or study has actually started.

## Do I Need a Visa Just to Enter Singapore From South Korea?

No, not for a short visit. South Korean ordinary passport holders get 90 days of visa-free entry to Singapore, one of the longer windows Singapore grants any nationality. That covers a job interview, a relocation briefing, or a full week of room viewings before your pass is issued, with room to spare.

It does not cover actually living here. Entering visa-free and holding a valid immigration pass are two different things, and only the second lets you sign a residential lease. If you're using the 90-day window to sort housing before your pass clears, a short-term co-living booking bridges that gap far more cheaply than a hotel does over several weeks.

## Which Pass Do I Actually Need Before I Can Rent?

For students, it's a Student Pass, applied for by your school through ICA's SOLAR system once you have an offer. Processing typically takes four to eight weeks, the pass fee is S$90, and ICA issues it as a digital pass rather than a physical card.

For working professionals, it's an Employment Pass or S Pass, sponsored by your employer. As of 2026, the Employment Pass salary floor is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), scaled up for older applicants and assessed against the COMPASS points framework, rising again from 1 January 2027. The S Pass floor for younger applicants sits at S$3,300 a month (S$3,800 in financial services).

None of these passes exist until they're actually issued. A posting letter from head office, an offer letter, or an In-Principle Approval is not the same thing, and no properly run operator will let you sign a lease on the strength of any of those alone. ## How Far Ahead Should I Book Housing?

Line your housing search up against your pass timeline, not your flight date. A Student Pass typically clears four to eight weeks after your school submits it, and an Employment Pass or S Pass moves on a broadly similar timeline, sometimes faster with a strong corporate HR team behind it.

Book a short, flexible arrangement to land in, then commit to a longer lease once your pass is confirmed and your start date is set. This is also why co-living suits the first stretch better than a standard 12-month tenancy: you're not locking into a rigid contract before you know your pass issue date.

## What Does Housing Actually Cost?

Rooms in Singapore's shared housing market run from about S$600 for a basic budget room up to S$2,500 for a private ensuite, with most standard common rooms landing between S$1,000 and S$1,600 a month. Co-living rooms, furnished and often bundled with utilities and wifi, typically sit in the S$1,200 to S$2,500+ range depending on operator and location.

Add food, transport, and a local SIM, and a realistic all-in monthly budget for a newcomer renting a room lands somewhere between S$1,800 and S$2,800. For upfront cash, budget a security deposit (standard practice is one month's rent) plus your first month, so two months' rent in hand before you get the keys is the safe assumption. We've broken deposit terms down further in our guide to [security deposits in Singapore co-living](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

An all-inclusive co-living room removes a lot of the guesswork that's hardest to plan for from Seoul, since one number covers rent, utilities, and wifi instead of several separate bills you've never paid in Singapore dollars before.

## Where Do Korean Expats and Students Actually Live?

There isn't a single Korean neighbourhood in Singapore the way there is in some other cities, but Tanjong Pagar has grown into what locals often call Singapore's Little Korea. It has a real concentration of Korean restaurants, cafes, and grocery stores, and it sits close to the CBD on the East-West and North-South MRT lines, which makes it a practical base for anyone working nearby, not just a cultural draw.

South Korea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs put the Korean community in Singapore at just over 21,000 people as of 2023, one of the larger Korean communities in Southeast Asia. Roughly 40 to 45 percent are corporate expatriates, about 30 percent are self-employed or run their own businesses, and about 20 percent are students. That spread covers both company-sponsored housing budgets and tighter student budgets, and co-living tends to suit the second group better, since it doesn't need the long lease a relocation package sometimes assumes.

Families with school-age children often anchor around Singapore Korean International School (SKIS) in Bukit Timah, which runs kindergarten through senior high on a Korean national curriculum blended with international elements, plus a Saturday Hangul School for language upkeep.

## Opening a Bank Account and Getting Set Up

You'll need your issued pass, not just an offer letter, to open an account at most banks. DBS accepts applications online and will work from an ICA In-Principle Approval plus an employer or school letter if your pass hasn't landed yet, while UOB generally expects a branch visit with your pass already in hand. Have your passport and proof of local address (your co-living agreement works for this) ready either way.

Once your card lands, day-to-day spending is easy. The bigger adjustment is usually setting up a local number and a PayNow-linked account, since a lot of everyday payments here, from splitting a bill to paying a landlord, run through PayNow rather than a card.

## Working for a Korean Company's Singapore Office

Singapore is a common regional base for Korean conglomerates and mid-sized firms covering Southeast Asia, so a chunk of Korean Employment Pass holders arrive on an internal transfer rather than a fresh local hire. The pass process itself is the same either way, since MOM doesn't run a separate track by employer nationality, though the timeline can move faster when a company's HR team has done the paperwork before.

If you're arriving on a transfer with a housing allowance attached, check what it actually covers before signing a 12-month lease sight unseen. A short co-living booking for the first month keeps you flexible until you've seen the commute and neighbourhood in person.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The most common one is booking a rigid, long lease before your pass is actually issued, then scrambling when the issue date shifts by a couple of weeks, which happens often enough to plan around. A second is assuming the 90-day visa-free window means you can settle into a normal lease right away; it gets you in the door for viewings and paperwork, nothing more. A third is treating Tanjong Pagar as the only option, when a room on a direct MRT line to your office or campus can beat proximity to Korean restaurants alone.

## A Quick Checklist Before You Book Anything

- [ ] Confirm your pass application is submitted and get a realistic issue-date estimate from your school or employer
- [ ] Budget two months' rent in cash for deposit plus first month, on top of flight and settling-in costs
- [ ] Check what's actually included in any room price (utilities, wifi, cleaning) before comparing two listings on rent alone
- [ ] Set up PayNow once your bank account is active, since a lot of local payments assume it
- [ ] Have your passport and a local address ready for your bank account application the week you land
- [ ] If you're on a company relocation package, confirm what the housing allowance covers before signing anything long-term

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I rent a room in Singapore before my Employment Pass is issued?**
No. You need the pass itself in hand to sign a standard lease or licence agreement. A short-term booking that doesn't require a long-term pass is the practical way to bridge the gap while your pass processes.

**Do I need a visa to fly to Singapore from South Korea for a viewing?**
No. Korean ordinary passport holders get 90 days visa-free entry, which is enough time to view rooms and sort paperwork, but it doesn't count as a long-term stay or let you sign a lease.

**Is Tanjong Pagar the only area with a Korean community in Singapore?**
No, it's just the area with the highest concentration of Korean restaurants and shops. Korean residents live across many districts, and choosing a room near your workplace or school usually matters more day to day than proximity to Korean food.

**Can I open a Singapore bank account before my pass arrives?**
Some banks, DBS in particular, will start the process using an ICA In-Principle Approval and an employer or school letter. Most still want the actual pass before the account is fully active.

**Does a company relocation package change what kind of lease I should sign?**
Not automatically. Confirm exactly what the housing allowance covers before committing to a long lease, and consider a short, flexible booking for your first weeks while those details get finalised.
