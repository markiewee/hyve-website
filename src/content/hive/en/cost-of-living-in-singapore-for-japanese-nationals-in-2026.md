The cost of living in Singapore for Japanese nationals runs well above Tokyo, mostly because of rent. A single professional on an Employment Pass renting a room in a shared home should budget roughly S$1,900 to S$2,900 a month all in: rent, food, transport, and phone. A family renting a full unit and sending one child to a Japanese-curriculum school can expect somewhere in the region of S$6,500 to S$9,000 a month, plus one-time school entrance costs that land separately from the monthly number.

The rest of this piece breaks down where those numbers come from, plus the specifics a move from Japan runs into that a generic "moving to Singapore" budget skips: Japanese grocery pricing, JPY to SGD remittance, and what The Japanese School Singapore actually charges.

## How much more expensive is Singapore than Tokyo?

Estimates for the overall gap vary a lot by source, from Singapore being roughly 15% cheaper than Tokyo on one index to as much as 69% to 85% more expensive on others. What's consistent is the driver: rent. Numbeo's city-centre one-bedroom comparison puts Tokyo at roughly US$1,360 a month against Singapore's roughly US$2,700, close to double.

Day-to-day costs narrow that gap. Hawker food, public transport, and mobile data in Singapore land much closer to Tokyo prices than the rent comparison suggests. Housing is where the real jump happens, not groceries or getting around.

## What does rent actually cost, and why a room usually makes more sense first

A room in a shared home in Singapore typically runs S$600 to S$1,200 for a basic or HDB room, up to S$1,700 to S$2,500 for a master bedroom or ensuite. A studio or one-bedroom unit on your own usually starts around S$1,900 and can pass S$3,500 in a central location. Before you have a Singapore salary history or a settled view on which neighbourhood suits you, a room is the faster, lower-risk option.

There's a practical reason too. Many landlords doing a private tenancy want a local guarantor or a Singapore bank statement history, which a first-time arrival doesn't have yet. A co-living licence agreement or a master-lease operator isn't underwriting you as an individual the same way, so that friction mostly disappears.

For the fuller breakdown of deposits, stamp duty, and what's included in rent versus billed separately, see [cost of renting in Singapore 2026](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/cost-of-renting-singapore-2026-budget). That piece covers the general newcomer math in more depth than we'll repeat here.

## Food, Japanese groceries, and daily spending

A hawker-heavy diet runs roughly S$500 to S$1,000 a month, a genuine bargain by Singapore standards and close enough to Tokyo's own convenience-store and teishoku-lunch pricing that it rarely feels like a shock. Groceries alone, cooking mostly local ingredients, land closer to S$200 to S$400 a month for one person.

Japanese groceries push that number up. Singapore has a well-established network of Japanese supermarkets, including Meidi-Ya, one of the earliest Japanese grocers on the island, Isetan Scotts' supermarket floor, and Don Don Donki, which has expanded to multiple outlets since arriving in 2017. All three stock fresh Japanese produce, seasonings, and ready meals, but imported pricing runs above what the same items cost at home. Someone eating Japanese food at home several times a week should budget closer to S$700 to S$1,200 a month for food overall.

## Transport costs in Singapore

Public transport for a daily commute typically costs S$100 to S$150 a month on the MRT and buses, using a contactless card or SimplyGo. That's genuinely cheap, and it compares reasonably well to Tokyo's own train system, even if the network is smaller. Add regular Grab rides, especially for late nights or grocery runs with heavy bags, and transport can climb to S$300 to S$400 a month.

## Phone, data, and staying connected

A local SIM matters from week one, both for practical reasons (banking OTPs, delivery apps, work logins) and because Japanese carrier roaming plans get expensive fast past the first few days. Prepaid SIM-only plans start from around S$7 to S$15 a month for a moderate data allowance, with heavier data plans (500GB to 1TB) running S$14 to S$25. Setting this up on arrival, alongside opening a local bank account, is worth doing early; both usually need your pass or entry stamp and a local address.

## Sending money home: JPY to SGD remittance

This is a cost line that catches people out, since Japan's domestic transfer apps don't reach Singapore. Bank wires between the two typically carry a flat fee plus an exchange rate margin, and the combined cost usually runs above what a dedicated transfer service charges.

Digital providers are meaningfully cheaper. Wise, for example, charges a small fixed fee plus a variable fee of roughly 0.41% to 0.45% of the transfer amount at the mid-market rate for JPY to SGD transfers. As of early August 2026, the mid-market rate sat at roughly 1 SGD to 123 JPY. The yen has stayed on the weaker side against most major currencies for an extended stretch, worth factoring in if deciding whether to hold savings in JPY or convert early.

## Employment Pass and other pass costs

Pass eligibility and fees apply the same way regardless of nationality; there's no separate fee schedule for Japanese applicants. As of 2026, the standard Employment Pass costs S$105 for the application and a further S$225 if approved, so budget roughly S$330 total per pass, on top of whatever an employment agent or corporate services firm charges. Minimum qualifying salary for an EP starts around S$5,600 a month in most sectors, higher in financial services and for older applicants. Some nationalities face an additional Multiple Journey Visa fee of around S$30; confirm with your employer's HR or immigration agent whether this applies.

## Flights home and visiting during holidays

One-way flights from Singapore to Tokyo Narita start from around S$184 to S$200 on a good fare, with Haneda routes typically running higher, from roughly S$300 upward depending on the airline and how far ahead you book. Fares around Golden Week, Obon, or year-end holidays cost more, so booking a few months ahead for major holiday windows pays off.

## If you're bringing family: school costs are the real budget swing

For a single professional, school fees don't apply. For a family moving with a child, school choice is the biggest additional cost and swings the total more than any other line.

The Japanese School Singapore, with campuses at Changi and West Coast, charges annual tuition of roughly S$6,660 to S$7,500 for 2026/27, noticeably lower than many other international school options in Singapore. On top of tuition, expect one-time enrolment costs: an entrance fee (from roughly S$1,090 for JA membership holders up to around S$3,924 without corporate membership), a facility surcharge of around S$2,725, and a personal donation, typically S$3,000 plus GST, if there's no corporate donor behind the enrolment. These land as one-off costs at enrolment, not monthly ones.

Broader international schools in Singapore run at meaningfully higher price points, so anyone weighing a non-Japanese-curriculum option should price that out separately.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Singapore more expensive than Tokyo for daily living?**
On rent, yes, clearly. Numbeo's city-centre one-bedroom comparison puts Tokyo at roughly half of Singapore's rent. Overall index comparisons vary widely by source, so treat any single headline percentage as directional. Day-to-day costs like hawker food, transport, and mobile data are much closer between the two cities than the rent gap suggests.

**Can I find Japanese groceries in Singapore easily?**
Yes. Meidi-Ya, Isetan Scotts, and Don Don Donki all stock Japanese produce and snacks across multiple locations. Prices sit above local grocery staples due to import costs, so a Japanese-heavy diet costs more than eating mostly hawker or local supermarket food.

**What does an Employment Pass cost in 2026?**
S$105 for the application and S$225 if approved, roughly S$330 total, plus any agent fees. Minimum qualifying salary starts around S$5,600 a month in most sectors, and fees are the same regardless of nationality.

**Is it cheaper to rent a room or a whole apartment when relocating to Singapore?**
A room is significantly cheaper and easier for a first move, since it usually doesn't require a local guarantor or a Singapore rental history. See [cost of renting in Singapore 2026](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/cost-of-renting-singapore-2026-budget) for the full deposit and stamp duty breakdown.

**What's the cheapest way to send money between Singapore and Japan?**
Dedicated transfer services like Wise are typically cheaper than a bank wire, since banks bundle in a wider exchange rate margin on top of a flat fee. Compare live quotes before sending, since fees and rates shift often.

**How much does The Japanese School Singapore cost?**
Annual tuition runs roughly S$6,660 to S$7,500 for 2026/27, plus one-time enrolment costs (entrance fee, facility surcharge, and a donation where there's no corporate sponsor) that add several thousand dollars separately from the annual tuition. Confirm current figures directly with the school.
