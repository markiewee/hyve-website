Housing for legal professionals relocating to Singapore comes down to one fact: the firms that hire you are almost all in the same few CBD blocks, around Marina Bay and Raffles Place, so a room near that cluster cuts your commute regardless of which firm you land at. Most foreign lawyers move here on a standard Employment Pass, then separately work out how they're authorised to practise, whether that's foreign law only, or Singapore law in a defined set of areas through the Foreign Practitioner route. This guide covers where the firms actually sit, how the pass and practice rules work, and where to live once you've got both sorted.

## Where Are Singapore's Law Firms Actually Located?

Singapore's major law firms cluster tightly in the Downtown Core, mostly within a ten-minute walk of Raffles Place MRT.

**Allen & Gledhill** runs out of One Marina Boulevard. **WongPartnership** is at Marina Bay Financial Centre Tower 3. **Rajah & Tann** is in Marina One West Tower. **Drew & Napier** is at Ocean Financial Centre on Collyer Quay. Singapore's four largest local firms sit within a few minutes' walk of each other, between Marina Bay and Raffles Place.

International firms are in the same postcode. Latham & Watkins, for one, is at Republic Plaza on Raffles Place. Most other US, UK, and pan-Asian firms with a Singapore office, magic circle names included, sit somewhere in this same Marina Bay to Raffles Place to Collyer Quay stretch, because that's where the work, the clients, and the courts are.

Dispute resolution adds one more address worth knowing: the Singapore International Arbitration Centre operates out of Maxwell Chambers at 32 Maxwell Road, in Tanjong Pagar, a short walk south of the main cluster and its own MRT stop on the same lines.

## Why Does Singapore's Legal Sector Cluster So Tightly?

Singapore has spent decades building itself into the region's legal and dispute resolution hub, and the geography follows the strategy. SIAC, founded in 1991, is consistently ranked among the most used arbitral institutions in Asia-Pacific and globally, pulling cross-border disputes, and the firms that handle them, into Singapore rather than a dozen other Asian capitals.

That regional-hub role is also why the Big Four and the international firms overlap so heavily on the same few streets. Cross-border M&A, banking and finance work, and arbitration draw on the same pool of clients and courts, so there's little advantage to a firm setting up outside the core. For a relocating lawyer, that's good news: the job market is all in one compact stretch of the city.

## How Do Foreign Lawyers Actually Get Authorised to Work Here?

Two separate questions get bundled together when people say "moving to Singapore for law," and it's worth pulling them apart before you house-hunt.

**The immigration question** is usually the more familiar one. Most foreign lawyers relocating for a firm role come in on a general Employment Pass, the same pass used across most professional sectors. Unlike financial services, which the Ministry of Manpower holds to its own, higher qualifying salary line, legal roles don't have a separate published sector threshold. The general Employment Pass qualifying salary currently starts at S$5,600 a month at age 23 and under, rising with age to S$10,700 at 45 and above, with a further increase to S$6,000 and S$11,500 respectively scheduled for 1 January 2027 ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)). Most associate and partner-track salaries at the Big Four and international firms clear this comfortably, so for most relocating lawyers the pass itself isn't the hard part.

**The practice question** is separate, and specific to law. Holding an Employment Pass lets you work in Singapore. It doesn't automatically let you practise Singapore law. Foreign-qualified lawyers who want to handle Singapore law matters, rather than only advise on their home jurisdiction's law from a Singapore office, generally go through a Foreign Practitioner route, including the Foreign Practitioner Examinations administered by the Singapore Institute of Legal Education, built for lawyers already authorised to practise elsewhere. Firms structured as Qualifying Foreign Law Practices or Joint Law Ventures give foreign lawyers a licensed way to work across a mix of foreign and Singapore law without full local requalification. The exact scope depends on the firm's licence and your own qualification, so confirm it with your employer rather than assume.

Neither route changes where you should live. Both land you working out of the same CBD cluster either way.

## How Is This Different From Housing for Law Students?

If you're searching for this because you're studying law in Singapore rather than working, you want a different guide. Law students weigh campus commute against internship and moot access, on a semester-length or degree-length timeline, and their pass situation is a student pass, not an Employment Pass. That's covered separately in [housing for law students near the CBD](/blog/housing-for-law-students-near-the-cbd-in-singapore).

This guide is for the other case: a qualified lawyer relocating for a job, not a course. You're not timing a lease around semesters or internship rotations. You're settling in for a role that could run years, at a firm with a fixed CBD address from day one. Location still matters, but so does whether the room and lease terms support actually living here, not just getting through a placement.

## Where Should You Actually Live?

The commute math, MRT lines, and neighbourhood-by-neighbourhood breakdown for CBD access is covered properly in [where to live by commute time to the CBD](/blog/where-to-live-by-commute-time-to-cbd-singapore), so it won't be repeated here. The short version for law specifically: aim for a direct line into Raffles Place or Marina Bay with no transfer, since deal closings, hearing days, and late client calls don't pause for a train change.

Legal work runs long hours in bursts rather than a fixed clock, closer to how banking runs than a standard nine-to-six. Deal teams work to signing timelines, litigation and arbitration teams work to hearing and filing dates, and associates across the Big Four and international firms routinely stay well past close of business when a matter demands it. A short, direct commute matters more on those nights than on an easy week, and there are plenty of those nights in a law career.

This is also different from a short posting. If you're here on a brief secondment rather than settling into the role, lease flexibility matters more than long-term commute optimisation, and it's worth weighing that honestly before committing to a longer lease.

## Settling In, Not Just Landing

A career-track legal role in Singapore is a multi-year proposition for most people, not a rotation. A furnished, flexible-lease room removes a chunk of the setup work that eats into your first few weeks at a new firm, when you'd rather be learning the practice group than furnishing an apartment or negotiating a lease sight unseen from another country.

Co-living rooms near the CBD fit that stage well: furnished from day one, flexible on lease length, and close enough to Marina Bay or Raffles Place that the commute isn't something you're managing around your caseload.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do most Singapore law firms have their offices?**
Almost entirely within a short walk of Raffles Place MRT, across Marina Bay, Raffles Place, and Collyer Quay. Allen & Gledhill, WongPartnership, Rajah & Tann, and Drew & Napier are all in this stretch, along with most international firms with a Singapore office.

**Do I need a special Employment Pass for a legal role in Singapore?**
No. Unlike financial services, which has its own higher qualifying salary threshold, legal roles use the general Employment Pass framework, currently starting at S$5,600 a month at age 23 and under, rising with age ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)).

**Does an Employment Pass let me practise Singapore law?**
It lets you work in Singapore. Practising Singapore law specifically usually requires a separate route, such as the Foreign Practitioner Examinations administered by SILE, or working at a firm licensed as a Qualifying Foreign Law Practice or Joint Law Ventures. Confirm what your specific role and firm licence permit directly with your employer.

**Is this guide relevant to law students?**
Not primarily. Law students in Singapore weigh campus commute and a student pass rather than an Employment Pass and firm location. See the guide for [law students near the CBD](/blog/housing-for-law-students-near-the-cbd-in-singapore) instead.

**Why does Singapore have so many law firms in one small area?**
Singapore has built itself into the region's legal and arbitration hub, home to SIAC, one of the most used arbitral institutions in Asia-Pacific. That pulls cross-border legal work, and the firms handling it, into a compact CBD cluster rather than spreading across the city.
