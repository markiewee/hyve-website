If your semester timetable has you on two different campuses in the same week, don't pick housing near either one. Pick housing that keeps the two commutes roughly equal, ideally on a transport line that touches both, and take a lease that matches the semester rather than locking you into a full year. This is a shorter, lighter version of the double-degree housing problem, and it needs a different answer than "just live near campus."

## What Does a Multi-Campus Semester Actually Look Like?

Not every student who ends up on two campuses is doing a formal double degree. Plenty of single-degree students get a split timetable for one semester only, then it goes back to normal.

A cross-faculty elective can sit at a different site than your main faculty's building. A student whose home faculty runs mostly out of one part of campus might pick up a module taught in another building or a different campus zone entirely, for one semester, then never go back there again.

University-industry partnership components work the same way. Some programmes build in a stretch where part of the week is spent at a partner site or a satellite facility rather than the main lecture halls, without turning into a full placement year.

Polytechnic students see a version of this too. A semester with an internship, industry attachment, or Final Year Project hosted off the main campus often still has a day or two of classes back on campus in the same week, so the student is genuinely commuting to two places, not one.

| Situation | What it looks like | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Cross-faculty elective | A module taught in another building or campus zone entirely, outside your home faculty | One semester, then never again |
| University-industry partnership component | Part of the week at a partner site or satellite facility, without a full placement year | One semester |
| Polytechnic internship, attachment or Final Year Project | Hosted off the main campus, but with a day or two of classes still back on campus in the same week | One semester |
| Full double degree at two universities | Two campuses as permanent features of student life | Multiple years, a different problem entirely |

If any of this describes your semester, this guide is for you. If you're carrying two full degrees at two different universities for multiple years, the housing math is different and longer-term. See our [guide to housing for a double degree across two campuses](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-students-doing-a-double-degree-across-two-campuses) for that version instead.

## Why This Isn't the Same Problem as a Double Degree

A double degree is a structural, multi-year commitment. You know from day one that two campuses are permanent features of your student life, so it's worth optimising hard around that, potentially even choosing a room specifically because it splits the two commutes well over years, not months.

A single split semester is temporary by definition. You don't want to sign a year-long lease, uproot mid-degree, and pay a break fee just because one semester out of eight has an odd timetable. The smarter move is usually to ride it out in housing that's good enough for the semester, not perfect for the anomaly.

That difference changes what actually matters. For a double degree, minimising the two-way commute over years is worth real effort. For one semester, minimising hassle and avoiding a lease that doesn't fit the timeline usually beats squeezing out the last ten minutes of travel time.

| | Double degree | Single split semester |
|---|---|---|
| Commitment | Structural, multi-year | Temporary by definition |
| Worth optimising hard? | Yes, minimising the two-way commute over years is worth real effort | No. Minimising hassle and avoiding a lease that outlasts the timeline usually beats squeezing out the last ten minutes of travel |
| Right move | Potentially choose a room specifically because it splits the two commutes well | Ride it out in housing that is good enough for the semester, not perfect for the anomaly |

## The Core Housing Logic: Average, Don't Anchor

The instinct is to pick a room near whichever campus feels like "home base," then accept a long trek to the second site once or twice a week. That's usually the wrong call, especially if the second site's classes cluster on particular days.

The better approach is to treat both sites as real, and find a base that keeps the sum of both commutes reasonable rather than optimising one at the expense of the other. A location on a transport line that touches both sites, or sits roughly between them, beats a location that's excellent for one and brutal for the other.

This is exactly the tradeoff our [commute comparison guide on living centrally versus near a single campus](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/commute-comparison-living-centrally-vs-near-a-single-campus) walks through in more depth, and it applies directly here. A split-campus semester is a specific case of that same general question: is it worth trading proximity to one destination for a shorter combined trip across all your destinations?

Before you commit to a room, map both sites and your actual weekly schedule, not just theoretical straight-line distance. A room that's a 15-minute walk from Site A but a 50-minute multi-transfer ride to Site B on your two lecture days there isn't actually convenient, even though it looks great on paper for Site A alone.

## What to Check Before You Book

A short checklist matters more here than a long one, since you're solving for one semester, not four years.

- **Both commutes, not one.** Time the trip to each site at the actual hour your classes start, not off-peak.
- **The heaviest travel day.** If one day of the week has you moving between both sites, that's the day that should drive your decision, not the average week.
- **Lease length matches your semester.** Don't sign a 12-month contract for a housing need that expires in 13 to 16 weeks. Look for co-living or short-term options built around flexible terms rather than a full annual tenancy.
- **A quiet place to work between sites.** A split day often means a gap between classes on different campuses. A room with decent wifi and a desk saves you from burning that gap in a crowded library or a campus common area you don't have a card for.
- **How the second site's schedule might shift.** Confirm with your faculty or the partner site whether the split is fixed for the whole semester or could change after add/drop. A room chosen around a Week 1 timetable that gets revised in Week 3 is a frustrating problem to have signed a lease around.

## Where This Plays Out on the Ground in Singapore

Singapore's campuses are spread across the island in a way that makes this a real, recurring issue rather than a rare edge case. NUS alone runs multiple campus zones, including Kent Ridge as the main site and separate campuses for law and public policy and for the medical school, so an NUS student picking up one elective outside their usual faculty building can genuinely end up crossing the island mid-week. NTU has a similar shape once clinical or specialised components take students off the main Jurong West campus for part of a semester.

Polytechnics build this in more deliberately. A semester built around an internship, industry attachment, or a work-study component often still keeps a class or two running on the home campus in the same week, so the two-site problem shows up by design rather than by accident.

None of this needs a rare, named exchange programme to be true for you. If your own timetable has two site codes on it for one semester, the logic in this guide applies, regardless of which specific arrangement put you there.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need special housing if I'm only splitting campuses for one semester?**
Not special housing, but a shorter lease matters more than usual. Match the lease length to the semester rather than defaulting to a year-long tenancy for a situation that's temporary by definition.

**Should I pick housing near the campus where I spend more days?**
Not automatically. Weigh the heaviest single day of cross-campus travel, and the total weekly time on the road, rather than just counting which site gets more days.

**Is this the same as double-degree housing?**
No. A double degree is a multi-year structural commitment worth optimising hard around. A split semester is temporary, so the priority shifts toward flexibility and a lease that fits the timeline, not squeezing out the shortest possible commute.

**What if my split-campus schedule changes after add/drop period?**
Confirm the split is fixed before you sign anything long-term, and favour housing with flexible lease terms if there's any chance the timetable moves once the semester properly starts.
