The four things to sort first when you move to Singapore are your pass, a local bank account, a working SIM, and a registered address. They're tangled together more than people expect. Your address affects your bank account. Your pass affects your SIM. Nothing here is hard on its own, but the order you do them in matters, and most of it fits into your first two weeks, not your first weekend.

This checklist walks through what has to happen, in what order, and what genuinely can wait.

## What Do I Need to Sort Out First When I Arrive?

Start with your pass, because everything else depends on it. If you're on an Employment Pass, S Pass, or Work Permit, your employer usually handles the application and you collect the physical card after you land. If you're on a Long-Term Visit Pass or Student's Pass, that process runs through ICA instead.

Once your pass is in hand, three things unlock at roughly the same time: a bank account, a proper SIM registration, and a registered address. You don't need all three on day one. You need them inside your first two weeks, because several downstream tasks (salary crediting, phone-based two-factor authentication, government e-services) quietly assume you already have them.

## What Actually Has to Happen in Your First Fortnight

Strip away the nice-to-haves and here's what's actually mandatory or close to it in the first two weeks:

Your pass needs to be collected or activated. Your address needs to be registered, and the clock on that starts the day you move in, not the day you feel like dealing with it. Your bank account needs opening if your salary is being paid locally. Your SIM needs a real registration, because a passport-only SIM has a short shelf life.

Everything else, setting up CPF contributions if applicable, applying for a tax number, getting a local library card, is genuinely optional in week one. Don't let it compete for your attention.

## Do It Before You Fly vs Do It On Arrival

Some of this you can start from your home country. Most of it you can't.

| Task | Before you fly | On arrival |
|---|---|---|
| Pass application | Employer/school usually starts this remotely | You collect or activate the physical pass |
| Bank account | A few banks let you pre-open digitally | Most require you in a branch with your pass in hand |
| SIM card | Not really possible | Passport gets you a temporary SIM; pass gets you a permanent one |
| Address registration | Not possible, you need a real address | Must be done once you've moved in |
| Housing | Book or confirm remotely | Move in, get your tenancy agreement or booking confirmation |

The practical read: line up your pass and your housing before you land. Everything downstream of those two, bank account, SIM, address, has to happen once you're physically here.

## Needing an Address to Get a Bank Account (and Vice Versa)

This is the trap that catches almost everyone. Banks want proof of address to open an account. Some address registrations effectively want a settled arrangement to point to. If your housing is genuinely open-ended, or you're still deciding between three shortlisted units, you can end up stuck between two processes that each seem to need the other to move first.

The fix is simple: nail down your address before you touch the bank. Whatever you sign, whether it's a tenancy agreement, a co-living licence agreement, or a booking confirmation, that's your proof of address. Banks generally accept a tenancy agreement, a utility bill, or a letter from your employer or school, along with your pass and passport, and most want it dated within the last three months. This is where having a fixed address from day one actually helps, rather than moving somewhere short-term and switching again a few weeks later. Co-living setups, including Lazybee's rooms in Singapore, hand you a signed agreement on day one, which is exactly the kind of document a bank account application is looking for.

## You Can't Sort Everything Out in a Weekend

It's a nice idea. It's not how this works. Pass collection can take a queue and an appointment. Bank branches want you in person with original documents, and not every branch handles every account type same-day. SIM registration is quick, but only if you already have the right ID document with you.

Budget your first two weeks for this, not your first two days. Trying to compress it into a weekend usually just means redoing something, like registering a SIM on your passport and then having to walk it back in a month once that registration lapses.

## Relocating to Singapore: The Whole Sequence

If you want the dependency chain in one line: pass unlocks SIM and formal bank onboarding, address unlocks bank account and address registration, and housing unlocks address. So the real starting point isn't the bank or the SIM. It's locking down where you're actually living.

Once housing is settled, the rest falls into a fairly predictable order across the first two weeks: collect or activate your pass, register your address, open your bank account, register your permanent SIM, then circle back for the smaller admin (tax registration if relevant, transport card, any workplace-specific onboarding).

## Relocation Admin in 2026

Most of this sequence now runs through online portals rather than physical queues. MOM's pass services, ICA's e-Services for address updates, and most major banks' account applications can all be started or in some cases completed digitally. That doesn't mean instant. Processing windows vary by bank, by pass type, and by how complete your documents are, so treat any specific timeframe you read online as a guide rather than a guarantee, and check current processing times directly with the relevant agency or bank if a deadline matters to you. exact current processing windows with MOM, ICA, or your chosen bank before relying on a specific number of days.

## The 14 Tasks for Your First Two Weeks

1. Collect or activate your work pass, LTVP, or Student's Pass.
2. Confirm your address (tenancy agreement, licence agreement, or booking confirmation).
3. Register your address with ICA if you hold an ICA-administered pass, or update it through MOM's portal if your pass is MOM-issued.
4. Get a temporary SIM on your passport if you don't have one from the airport.
5. Open a local bank account with your pass, passport, and proof of address.
6. Re-register your SIM on your pass once it's in hand, before the passport-based registration lapses.
7. Set up online banking and mobile app access.
8. Update your employer or school with your bank account details for salary or stipend crediting.
9. Get a transport card or set up contactless payment for public transport.
10. Check whether you need to register for a local tax number, depending on your pass and income.
11. Save the key admin contacts: your bank's hotline, MOM or ICA case reference if you have one, your landlord or property manager.
12. Set up mobile banking two-factor authentication now that your SIM is stable.
13. Sort out health insurance or confirm what your employer already provides.
14. Do a final check that your address, phone number, and bank details all match across every system you've touched, since mismatches here are what cause delays later.

## Arrival Admin: The Questions Nobody Thinks to Ask

**Can I open a bank account before my pass arrives?**
Sometimes, with an In-Principle Approval letter or similar, depending on the bank. Most want the actual pass in hand. Check with your specific bank before assuming either way.

**What counts as proof of address if I'm renting a room, not a whole unit?**
A signed tenancy agreement or licence agreement for the room is generally accepted, the same as it would be for a full unit. What matters is that it's dated, names you, and states the address.

**Do I need a local address before I can even book a bank appointment?**
Practically, yes. You'll be asked for it on the application, and most banks want documentation to match.

**What happens if my passport-registered SIM lapses before I've sorted my pass?**
It gets suspended without much warning. Keep an eye on the date you registered it and re-register on your pass well before that window closes.

**Do I need to redo any of this if I move apartments a few months later?**
Yes. A change of address means updating it with ICA or MOM again, and usually notifying your bank too, since address mismatches can flag an account for review.

**Is it worth doing all of this myself, or should I use a relocation agent?**
Most of it is straightforward enough to DIY, especially the bank account and SIM. Where people get stuck is usually housing and the address side of things, which is worth locking down early and properly rather than as an afterthought.
