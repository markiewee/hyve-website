Moving to Singapore from Indonesia is one of the shortest relocations in the region and, for most Indonesians, one of the easiest to get legally right. You don't need a visa to enter. Your pass (Student Pass, S Pass, or Employment Pass) determines how long you can stay and what you can rent, and none of the three blocks you from renting a private room or unit. The part that actually trips people up is housing: committing to a 12-month lease before you know your neighbourhood, your commute, or whether your pass will even still be valid that far out.

This guide covers what changes and what doesn't when you move from Indonesia specifically, what a realistic first-year budget looks like, and why co-living has become a common landing spot for Indonesians arriving on a pass with limited runway.

## Do Indonesians Need a Visa to Enter Singapore?

No. Indonesian passport holders can enter Singapore visa-free for social or business visits of up to 30 days, provided the passport is valid for at least six months and an SG Arrival Card is submitted online before arrival. That covers a scouting trip, a viewing, or the flight in once your pass is approved. It does not cover living or working here past 30 days. For that you need the pass tied to your purpose: Student Pass for full-time study, Work Permit or S Pass for employment, or Employment Pass for professional roles.

## Which Pass Do You Actually Need?

**Student Pass.** If you're enrolled full-time at an approved Singapore institution, your school applies for your Student Pass through ICA's SOLAR system, usually before your course starts. The pass fee is S$90 and processing typically runs 4 to 8 weeks, so this needs starting well before you fly, not on arrival.

**Employment Pass (EP).** For professional, managerial, and executive roles. As of 2026 the minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), rising further from January 2027. EPs are usually issued for up to two years on first grant, with renewals up to three.

**S Pass.** For mid-skilled roles. The 2026 minimum is S$3,300 a month in most sectors (S$3,800 in financial services), and the bar rises with age. Validity mirrors the EP structure.

**Work Permit.** Common for roles in construction, manufacturing, and domestic work, and Indonesia has long been one of the main source countries here. Work Permit holders are usually housed by their employer rather than renting independently, so most of this guide is aimed more at Student Pass, S Pass, and EP holders arranging their own room.

## What Changes for Indonesians Specifically, and What Doesn't

Nothing about the tenancy rules changes based on nationality. Once you hold a valid long-term pass, an Indonesian renter has exactly the same rights and paperwork requirements as a renter from any other country. What genuinely differs comes down to logistics, not law:

- **Flight time and cost.** Jakarta to Singapore is under two hours, and Batam or Bintan is a ferry ride away. That makes a scouting trip before you commit far more realistic than it is for someone relocating from further afield, and it's worth doing if your timeline allows it.
- **Time zone.** Jakarta and Singapore differ by only one hour, so coordinating viewings, paperwork, and calls with an employer or agent back home is easier than most cross-border moves.
- **Halal food access.** Singapore has a large and well-established halal food scene, with certified options across most neighbourhoods, hawker centres, and supermarkets. Not something you need to plan around; it's already there.
- **Family and community.** Indonesians make up one of Singapore's larger foreign communities, so you're not arriving into total unfamiliarity. That matters less for the paperwork and more for how quickly the first few months feel normal.
- **Currency and remittance.** Budget for the IDR-SGD exchange rate when you're converting savings or sending money home, and set up a local bank account early since salary crediting, GIRO payments, and most rent collection assume one. ## What Does Renting in Singapore Actually Cost?

Rent is only ever part of the number. A realistic all-in monthly budget for someone renting a room in Singapore, once you add utilities, wifi, food, and transport, typically lands between roughly S$1,800 and S$2,800. Renting a whole unit alone runs closer to S$3,500 to S$5,500 once everything is counted. We break this down room by room and cost by cost in [cost of renting in Singapore 2026](/articles/cost-of-renting-singapore-2026-budget), including the first-month costs (deposit, agent fee if you use one, moving costs) that tend to surprise people who've only budgeted for the monthly rent line.

Two things worth knowing before you sign anything: stamp duty applies to most tenancy agreements and has a 14-day filing window most tenants don't know exists (see [stamp duty on a room rental](/articles/stamp-duty-room-rental-singapore)), and security deposits in Singapore follow patterns that aren't always what a first-time renter expects (see [security deposits in co-living](/articles/security-deposit-coliving-singapore)).

## Why Co-Living Fits a First Year in Singapore

The single biggest housing mistake newcomers make, Indonesian or otherwise, is signing a 12-month lease before they know the pass timeline, the commute, or the neighbourhood. A Student Pass typically renews annually alongside enrolment. An S Pass renewal depends on your employer and your role staying within quota. Locking into a rigid year-long private tenancy on a pass with less certainty than that is a bet, not a plan.

Co-living rooms generally run on more flexible terms than a standard whole-unit tenancy, the room usually comes furnished with utilities and wifi bundled in, and an operator handles the paperwork rather than leaving you to split bills with strangers you found on a listing site. That combination is exactly what makes the first few months easier to plan around when you're still working out how long you're staying and where.

It also removes a step that trips up a lot of newcomers: furnishing a room from scratch. If you're weighing whether to ship or buy furniture versus renting a place that already has it, we've laid out that trade-off in [shipping furniture vs renting furnished](/articles/shipping-furniture-vs-renting-furnished-singapore).

## A Practical First-Month Order of Operations

1. Confirm your pass type and its validity window before you start looking at leases. This decides how long a commitment makes sense.
2. Book a scouting trip if your timeline allows it. Jakarta to Singapore is short enough that this is realistic for many Indonesian movers in a way it isn't for most other nationalities.
3. Line up your bank account and SIM registration early, since salary crediting and most digital verification steps assume both are in place.
4. Compare room listings against what's actually included (utilities, wifi, cleaning) rather than headline rent alone.
5. Match your lease length to your pass, not to the landlord's preferred 12-month default.

## Moving From Indonesia: Questions We're Asked Often

**Do I need a visa to visit Singapore before I move?**
No. Indonesian passport holders get visa-free entry for up to 30 days for social or business visits, which is enough time for a scouting trip or a viewing, but not enough to live or work here.

**Can I rent a room in Singapore on a Student Pass?**
Yes. A Student Pass is a valid long-term pass, and holders 18 and above can sign their own tenancy or licence agreement. The same minimum-stay and documentation rules apply as for any other pass holder.

**Is it cheaper to rent alone or through co-living?**
It depends what's included. A room advertised at a lower headline rent that doesn't bundle utilities and wifi can end up costing more than a slightly higher all-inclusive co-living room once you add up the separate bills.

**How early should I start looking for housing?**
As soon as your pass application is in motion. Co-living rooms and shorter leases give you more room to move than a standard 12-month private tenancy, but availability still varies by neighbourhood and by month.

**Does my Indonesian nationality affect what documents I need to rent?**
No. Once you hold a valid pass, the document checklist is the same regardless of nationality: passport, pass or in-principle approval, and proof of income or enrolment. See [can foreigners rent in Singapore](/articles/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore) for the full list operators check.
