Two co-living rooms priced at S$1,400 a month can be completely different deals. One might be ensuite with utilities capped and included, the other a shared bathroom with electricity billed separately past a low threshold. To compare co-living listings properly, you need to normalise eight variables first: bathroom type, furnishing, room size, what's bundled, minimum stay and notice period, distance to MRT, building condition, and deposit terms. Only once those match, or you've adjusted for where they don't, does the headline price actually mean anything.

This isn't a complicated exercise. It's a checklist you run on any two listings in about ten minutes, including a Lazybee room against a competitor's.

## Why Price Per Room Alone Misleads You

A room price is one number standing in for a bundle of decisions the operator already made for you. Room size, bathroom type, what's included, how long you're locked in. Two listings can quote the same rent and differ on every one of those.

The market backs this up. Common rooms in Singapore co-living typically run S$1,000 to S$1,600 a month, and master bedrooms with ensuite bathrooms run S$1,700 to S$2,500, a gap wide enough that "cheaper" and "better value" are frequently not the same room. Room sizes vary just as much, with rentable rooms in Singapore generally falling anywhere from about 110 to 320 square feet depending on the unit and room type. A "cheap" 110 sqft shared room and a "pricier" 250 sqft ensuite room can work out to a near-identical cost once you divide by space.

None of this means the market is being dishonest. It means the headline number was never designed to be compared on its own, and comparing it that way is where people get a worse deal without noticing.

## The 8 Variables That Have to Match Before You Compare

Run through these for both listings before you look at price again. If a variable doesn't match, note it, you're not disqualifying either listing, you're adjusting for the difference.

**1. Ensuite vs shared bathroom.** This alone can move the price by several hundred dollars a month. Comparing an ensuite room's rent against a shared-bathroom room's rent without accounting for that is comparing two different products.

**2. Furnished level.** "Furnished" can mean a bed frame and nothing else, or a bed, wardrobe, desk, and curtains ready to move into the same day. Ask exactly what's in the room, not just whether the listing says furnished.

**3. Room size in square feet.** Get the actual number, not a description like "cosy" or "spacious." Once you have it, price per square foot is the single most useful normalising number in this whole framework.

**4. What's bundled into rent.** Utilities, wifi, and cleaning are the big three, and coverage differs by operator: some cap electricity and include it, others bill every kilowatt separately. This deserves a quick check on each listing rather than an assumption either way, since it moves the real monthly cost meaningfully.

**5. Minimum stay and notice period.** A 3-month minimum with 1 month's notice and a 6-month minimum with 2 months' notice are different levels of commitment, even at identical rent. If you might need to leave early, this variable can matter more than the price does.

**6. Distance to MRT.** Walking minutes, not "near MRT." A 5-minute walk and an 18-minute walk both get called "near MRT" in listings. Check the actual distance on a map, and factor in whether it's a covered or exposed walk, since that matters in Singapore's weather.

**7. Building age and condition.** A newer condo and an older walk-up can carry very different maintenance realities, even at the same rent, same room size, and same MRT distance. Ask when the unit was last renovated and what the common areas actually look like in current photos, not stock photos from years ago.

**8. Deposit terms.** How many months' deposit, and how it's returned. A higher deposit with a clear, itemised return process is generally a better sign than a lower deposit with vague terms, since the number itself is a smaller factor than what happens when you move out.

## A Mini Framework to Score Any Two Listings

Once you've got all eight variables for both listings, here's how to turn that into an actual number you can compare.

**Step 1: Build the true monthly cost.** Start with the headline rent. Add back anything genuinely excluded, your best estimate of utilities if they're billed separately, a cleaning fee if it's not bundled. This gets you closer to what you'd actually pay in a typical month, not just what's advertised.

**Step 2: Divide by room size.** True monthly cost ÷ square footage gives you a dollar-per-square-foot figure. This is the number that actually lets you compare a small ensuite against a larger shared-bathroom room on equal footing, since it strips out the size difference that a flat rent number hides.

**Step 3: Score the non-price variables.** For MRT distance, building condition, minimum stay, and deposit terms, there's no single formula, so just tick which listing wins on each. You're not trying to reduce everything to one number, you're making the tradeoffs visible so you can weigh them by what matters to you.

**Step 4: Decide on what actually matters to you.** If flexibility matters most, weight minimum stay and notice period heavily even if it costs a bit more per square foot. If your commute is fixed, weight MRT distance. The framework doesn't decide for you, it just makes sure you're weighing real, comparable numbers instead of comparing two headline rents that were never measuring the same thing.

## Worked Example: Comparing Two Listings

| | Listing A | Listing B |
|---|---|---|
| Headline rent | S$1,500/mo | S$1,350/mo |
| Bathroom | Ensuite | Shared (3 tenants) |
| Room size | 140 sqft | 110 sqft |
| Bundled | Utilities, wifi, cleaning, aircon servicing, all included | Wifi only; utilities billed separately past a cap |
| Est. true monthly cost | S$1,500 | ~S$1,470 (rent + estimated utility overage) |
| Cost per sqft | ~S$10.70 | ~S$13.36 |
| Minimum stay | 3 months | 6 months |
| MRT distance | 6 min walk | 12 min walk |

On headline rent alone, Listing B looks S$150 cheaper. Once utilities, room size, and bathroom type are normalised, Listing A is actually the better cost per square foot, has a shorter minimum stay, and is closer to the MRT. Listing B might still be the right call for someone who genuinely doesn't mind a shared bathroom and wants to lock in six months, but that's now a decision based on real tradeoffs instead of one misleading headline number.

## The 10-Minute Checklist

Run this on any two listings before you decide:

- [ ] Bathroom type confirmed for both (ensuite vs shared, and shared with how many people)
- [ ] Furnished level confirmed, not just the word "furnished"
- [ ] Actual room size in square feet for both
- [ ] Full list of what's bundled: utilities, wifi, cleaning, aircon servicing
- [ ] Minimum stay and notice period for both
- [ ] MRT walking distance checked on a map, not taken from the listing description
- [ ] Building age and current condition, from recent photos
- [ ] Deposit amount and return process for both
- [ ] True monthly cost calculated (rent plus anything genuinely excluded)
- [ ] Cost per square foot calculated for a real apples-to-apples number

If a listing can't answer most of these clearly before you book a viewing, that's worth noting on its own. A well-run operator should be able to give you all ten without hedging.

## FAQ

**Is price per room ever a fair way to compare listings?**
Only if room size, bathroom type, and what's bundled are already identical between the two. In practice that's rare, so price per room alone usually isn't enough on its own.

**What's the single most useful number if I only have time to check one thing?**
Cost per square foot, calculated after adding back anything genuinely excluded from the headline rent. It's the one number that adjusts for room size automatically.

**Should I trust listing photos for building condition?**
Ask when the photos were taken and, where possible, request a few current ones or a video call. Stock or old photos are the most common gap between what's advertised and what's actually there.

**Do all operators publish minimum stay and notice period upfront?**
Not always. Some only reveal it after you enquire. Treat a listing that won't state it plainly as a flag to ask more questions before you commit.
