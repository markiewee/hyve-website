If you're single and under 35 in Singapore, you cannot buy an HDB flat on your own under the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme or the Joint Singles Scheme. The minimum age for both is 35, confirmed on [HDB's own eligibility page](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/buying-a-flat/flat-grant-and-loan-eligibility/singles). A small set of exceptions (orphans, widowed persons, and single parents with children in their legal custody) can apply from age 21. Everyone else waits. That's the rule. It doesn't mean you have no housing options in the meantime, and this piece maps out what's actually open to you.

## The Singles Rule, Explained Without Spin

The Single Singapore Citizen Scheme lets one unmarried, divorced, or widowed Singapore Citizen buy a resale flat solo once they turn 35. The Joint Singles Scheme lets up to four singles buy together, though the CPF Housing Grant for Singles caps out at two applicants on that purchase.

On the resale market, eligible singles can buy almost any flat type except 3-generation flats, with no restriction on size beyond the usual Ethnic Integration Policy and SPR quota checks. On the new-flat side, singles are limited to 2-Room Flexi BTO units, and there's an income ceiling of $7,000 for that eligibility. Resale purchases carry no income ceiling. Eligible first-timer singles can also tap combined housing grants, reported up to $115,000, though grant quantums are revised periodically, so check HDB's site for the exact current figure before you plan around it.

## Why Can't You Buy Until 35?

The short answer is that public housing policy has historically prioritised married couples and families, on the reasoning that flats are a scarce, subsidised resource meant to support family formation. Singles were folded in later, and later still at a higher age floor than everyone else.

That reasoning is now openly being questioned in Parliament, which is the more interesting part of this story if you're the one waiting.

## The Singles Position in 2026

As of August 2026, the age is still 35. But it's under active review. National Development Minister Chee Hong Tat said in a July 2026 speech that the government is looking at lowering the eligibility age for singles, citing changed social norms and demographics, according to [Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2026/07/govt-considering-hdb-eligibility-age-singles/). He declined to give a timeline, pointing to supply constraints, per [The Online Citizen](https://theonlinecitizen.com/2026/03/04/chee-hong-tat-declines-timeline-on-lowering-singles-bto-age-amid-supply-concerns).

Workers' Party MP Pritam Singh has pushed for a review, and the party has floated 28 as the new floor, the age by which most Singaporeans are in the workforce. Nothing has been locked in. If you're mapping your own timeline, plan against the current rule (35), and treat any lower age as a possible upside, not something to bank on.

## Five Housing Routes Open Before You Turn 35

Not being able to buy an HDB flat is not the same as having no housing options. Here's what's actually available while you wait.

1. **Rent in the open market.** There's no age floor on renting an HDB room, a whole HDB flat, or a private unit. This is the most flexible option and the one most singles under 35 actually use.
2. **Buy private property instead.** Singapore Citizens face no minimum age to buy a condo or private apartment. You'll still need to clear the usual loan and down payment hurdles, and Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty applies if it's not your first property, but age isn't the blocker here.
3. **Stay with family for longer.** Not glamorous, but it's the lowest-cost way to bank savings for whichever path you take at 35.
4. **Co-living.** Furnished rooms, flexible leases, no renovation or furnishing outlay, and none of the multi-year commitment of a flat purchase. It's a genuinely useful bridge for singles building up savings or CPF ahead of eligibility, not a consolation prize. 5. **Check if you qualify for an early exception.** If you're widowed, orphaned, or a single parent with a child in your legal custody, you may be eligible under the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme from age 21, well ahead of the general rule.

## Myth: Singles Have No Options Until 35

This one gets repeated a lot, and it's false. What's true is narrower: you can't buy an HDB flat under the general singles scheme until 35. Renting, buying private property, and the narrow set of early exceptions above are all live options well before that. The confusion usually comes from conflating "can't buy public housing" with "has nowhere to live," which are two different problems.

## Public Housing Rules for Singles, Simplified

If none of this was familiar going in, here's the whole thing in plain terms:

- **Single Singapore Citizen Scheme**: buy solo, resale flats, from age 35 (or 21 if you qualify for an exception).
- **Joint Singles Scheme**: buy with up to three other singles, from age 35, grant support capped at two applicants.
- **BTO for singles**: 2-Room Flexi only, income ceiling $7,000.
- **Resale for singles**: most flat types except 3-generation flats, no income ceiling.
- **Before 35**: rent, buy private, or check the early-exception categories above.

## The Mistake of Waiting Passively for Your 35th Birthday

The most common misstep isn't picking the wrong housing option. It's doing nothing until the birthday arrives, then scrambling. A few specific versions of that mistake:

- Not building CPF Ordinary Account savings or cash savings during the wait, so 35 arrives with no runway to actually buy.
- Never checking whether you qualify for one of the age-21 exceptions, and assuming the general rule is the only rule.
- Assuming the eligibility age will drop before you're 35, and not planning against the current rule as the default.
- Renting somewhere that doesn't fit your life just because it's "temporary," when a few years of a bad living situation is a real cost too.

## Checklist: Map Your Options Against Your Age

Use this to figure out where you actually sit right now.

- **Under 21**: focus on savings and CPF, not housing purchase. Renting or staying with family covers the gap.
- **21 to 34, no exception category**: rent, or buy private if the numbers work. Start building your CPF and cash runway for 35 now, not later.
- **21 to 34, exception category (orphan, widowed, single parent with custody)**: check your eligibility under the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme directly with HDB. You may not need to wait at all.
- **35 and older**: you're eligible for the full range, resale, 2-Room Flexi BTO, and the Joint Singles Scheme if buying with others.
- **Anyone waiting on the 2026 review**: keep an eye on Parliament updates, but don't delay your own planning on the chance the age drops.

## Where This Leaves You

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can singles under 35 buy an HDB flat at all?

Not under the general rule. The Single Singapore Citizen Scheme and Joint Singles Scheme both set the minimum age at 35. The exception is a narrow set of categories, orphans, widowed persons, and single parents with children in their legal custody, who may qualify from age 21.

### Is the eligibility age definitely dropping to 28?

No. It's under review. The government has said it's looking at lowering the singles' eligibility age given changing social norms, and the Workers' Party has proposed 28 specifically, but as of August 2026 no timeline or confirmed change has been announced. Plan against the current rule of 35.

### Can I rent an HDB flat or room if I'm under 35?

Yes. Renting on the open market, an HDB room, a whole HDB unit, or a private apartment, has no age floor. The age-35 rule applies specifically to buying under the singles ownership schemes.

### Can singles under 35 buy private property instead?

Yes. Singapore Citizens face no minimum age to buy private property such as a condo. You'll still need to qualify for financing and factor in Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty if it isn't your first property, but age itself isn't a barrier.

### Does renting or co-living now affect my HDB eligibility later?

No. Renting anywhere, including a co-living room, doesn't affect your eligibility under the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme or Joint Singles Scheme once you turn 35 or otherwise qualify.

### What should I actually do while I wait?

Build savings and CPF Ordinary Account balance with the future purchase in mind, check whether you qualify for one of the early-exception categories, and choose a living situation now that doesn't cost you more than it needs to while you wait it out.
