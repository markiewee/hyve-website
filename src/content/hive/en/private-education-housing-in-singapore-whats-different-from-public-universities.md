If you're enrolling at a private institution like PSB Academy, Kaplan, MDIS, NAFA, Curtin Singapore, James Cook University Singapore, INSEAD or ESSEC, plan to rent your own room from day one. Almost none of Singapore's private education institutions (PEIs) run halls or dormitories the way NUS and NTU do. There's no ballot to wait on and no fallback if it doesn't come through, because there's usually nothing to apply for in the first place. On top of that, if you need a Student Pass, your school must be EduTrust-certified by the Committee for Private Education (CPE) for you to be eligible at all. Here's what that actually changes about how and when you find a place to live.

## Do Private Institutions in Singapore Have Halls or Dormitories?

Mostly no. PSB Academy, Kaplan, NAFA, LASALLE, Curtin Singapore, James Cook University Singapore, INSEAD and ESSEC don't operate student halls or dormitories. Students at these schools rent privately from the day they arrive, whether that's an HDB room, a condo, or a furnished co-living room.

MDIS is the one partial exception worth naming. It runs a small on-campus residence at its Stirling Road campus, but places are limited and priced as a package, so plenty of MDIS students end up looking at off-campus rooms too, either because the residence is full for their intake or because they want more flexibility. Full detail is in our [MDIS housing guide](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-mdis-students-in-singapore).

That puts almost every private-school student in Singapore in the same position SMU students are in, structurally, even though SMU is a public autonomous university. SMU's own on-campus capacity sits around 400 to 500 beds against a student population of more than 12,000, which is close enough to zero that our [SMU housing guide](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/smus-no-halls-policy-where-smu-students-actually-live) calls it "no halls" in practice. Private institutions run the same reality, just without even that small exception.

## On-Campus Housing at a Glance

| Institution type | On-campus housing | Guarantee for new students |
|---|---|---|
| NUS | ~11,000 beds across halls, residential colleges, student residences | No blanket guarantee, even for freshmen; selective Freshman Accommodation Exercise |
| NTU | Halls of residence across campus | Guaranteed for first two years, then competitive from year three |
| SMU | ~400 to 500 beds (Prinsep Street Residences plus leased partner beds) | No general guarantee; capacity mostly for postgrad and CIS students |
| Private institutions (PSB, Kaplan, NAFA, LASALLE, Curtin, JCU, INSEAD, ESSEC) | None, with MDIS a limited partial exception | Not applicable; private rental is the default from enrolment |

## Why Don't Private Institutions Run Their Own Housing?

Scale and business model. NUS and NTU are large land-holding campuses built around a residential college system, with thousands of beds baked into decades of campus planning. Private institutions are teaching businesses that lease space in commercial or purpose-built buildings, not sprawling grounds with room for hostel blocks.

PSB Academy's two campuses sit at Marina Square and The Cathay on Handy Road. Kaplan runs out of Odeon 333 on North Bridge Road. NAFA occupies towers along Bencoolen Street, one built directly over the MRT station. None of these are the kind of site that comes with a hostel wing attached, and building one from scratch isn't part of how a PEI operates.

Because of that, housing was never something these schools budgeted to provide at scale. It's built into the CPE's own guidance to prospective students to research accommodation independently, since PEIs are assessed on academic delivery, governance and student protection, not on-campus living arrangements.

## Does CPE and EduTrust Certification Affect Your Housing Timeline?

Indirectly, but it matters. The Committee for Private Education is the regulator for PEIs in Singapore, operating under SkillsFuture Singapore. EduTrust is its quality certification scheme, and it's the gate for whether a school can enrol foreign students on a Student Pass at all.

Per ICA, only EduTrust-certified PEIs can offer placements to foreign students who need a Student Pass. If a PEI isn't EduTrust-certified, it can't sponsor a Student Pass, and only foreign nationals who already hold a valid Dependant's Pass, Long-Term Visit Pass, or Immigration Exemption Order can study there instead.

That's a pass-eligibility rule, not a housing rule directly. But it shapes your timeline the same way: if you're an international student choosing between PEIs, EduTrust status is something to confirm before you accept an offer, at the same time you start planning where you'll actually live. Get the pass eligibility wrong and the housing question doesn't even arise.

## When Do Private-School Students Actually Need to Start Looking?

Now, or as close to your enrolment date as your budget allows. There's no hall ballot result to wait for, so there's no reason to delay the way an NUS or NTU freshman waiting on a Freshman Accommodation Exercise outcome might.

That's actually an advantage in one sense. NUS and NTU students who miss out on a hall place are often scrambling late, sometimes weeks before term starts, because they didn't know the ballot would go against them. Private-school students don't get blindsided the same way, because there was never a ballot to lose. The room search starts on day one, by default, for everyone.

The tradeoff is that private-school students carry the full cost and effort of finding a room from the very start, with no subsidised hall rate to fall back on even temporarily. Budgeting for a private rental or co-living room needs to happen before you commit to an intake date, not after.

## Why Are Private Campuses Usually More Central, and Does That Change the Commute?

Often, yes, though not universally. A number of private institutions sit inside or close to the city centre: PSB Academy at Marina Square and Handy Road, Kaplan at North Bridge Road, NAFA on Bencoolen Street, INSEAD and ESSEC both on Nepal Hill in the one-north business district. MDIS at Stirling Road in Queenstown and Curtin Singapore in Science Park II near Haw Par Villa sit a bit further out but are still well inside established MRT corridors. James Cook University Singapore, at Sims Drive between Kallang and Geylang, is the one that trades central for a quieter residential pocket near Aljunied.

Compare that to NUS, which sits out at Kent Ridge on the island's south-west edge, or NTU, further out again near Jurong West. Both effectively anchor students to a specific corridor, Clementi and the west side for NUS, Jurong and Boon Lay for NTU, if they want a short commute.

A city-centre PEI campus doesn't lock you into one corridor the same way. Since you're not chasing proximity to a single suburban gate, the practical question becomes which MRT line gets you into town cleanly, which opens up a much wider set of neighbourhoods across the island rather than just the streets immediately around campus.

## What This Actually Means When You're Room-Hunting

A few things fall out of everything above, in practical terms:

- **Budget for a private room from the start.** There's no hall rate to bank on, even provisionally, so build your housing cost into your total tuition-and-living budget before you commit to an intake.
- **Confirm EduTrust status if you're on a Student Pass.** This is a pass-eligibility question first, but it decides whether you can legally study and live in Singapore on that pass at all, so check it before signing anything.
- **Pick by MRT line, not by proximity to a gate**, for most PEIs. A central campus means your real filter is train time and price, not walking distance.
- **Check the specific school's guide** for your own campus. This piece stays general on purpose. For the details on any one institution, see our guides on [PSB Academy](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-psb-academy-students-in-singapore), [Kaplan](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-kaplan-singapore-students), [MDIS](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-mdis-students-in-singapore), [NAFA](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-nafa-students-in-singapore), [James Cook University Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-near-james-cook-university-singapore), [Curtin Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-near-curtin-singapore), [INSEAD](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-insead-singapore-students-a-short-program-guide), and [ESSEC](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-essec-asia-pacific-students-in-singapore).

## FAQ

**Do PSB Academy, Kaplan or NAFA students get school-provided housing?**
No. None of these three run halls or dormitories. Students arrange their own private rental or co-living room, typically before their course start date.

**What is EduTrust, and why does it matter for my Student Pass?**
EduTrust is the Committee for Private Education's quality certification scheme for PEIs. Per ICA, only EduTrust-certified schools can sponsor a Student Pass for foreign students. If a school isn't certified, foreign students without another valid pass can't enrol there at all.

**Is private education housing more expensive than a university hall?**
It depends on the room type and location, not the school type alone. A hall room at NUS or NTU can be cheaper than a private room in the same area, but it comes with a meal plan, curfews in some halls, and no guarantee of a place. A private or co-living room costs more predictably but with fewer restrictions and no ballot risk.

**Does every private institution in Singapore lack on-campus housing?**
Almost every one does. MDIS is the main exception, with a small on-campus residence at its Stirling Road campus, though capacity is limited and many students still end up renting privately.

**Do private-school campuses in Singapore tend to be more central than NUS or NTU?**
Many are, including PSB Academy, Kaplan, NAFA, INSEAD and ESSEC, which all sit in or near the city centre or one-north. A few, like Curtin Singapore and James Cook University Singapore, sit a bit further out, though still well served by MRT.
