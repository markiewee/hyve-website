If this is your first Employment Pass and your first time renting in Singapore, here's the short version. You need your EP card (or at minimum an In-Principle Approval letter) plus your passport and proof of employment to sign a lease. Private rentals have a legal three-month minimum. You'll want a Singapore bank account set up before or right around move-in, since most landlords and operators expect rent paid by local bank transfer or GIRO, not a foreign card. And the single most common first-timer mistake is signing a long lease before you've actually seen the neighbourhood in daylight.

That's the whole shape of it. The rest of this guide walks through each piece, because getting any one wrong costs you money or time you don't get back.

## Do I Even Qualify for an EP?

The Employment Pass is Singapore's work visa for professionals, managers, and executives, and it comes with a minimum salary requirement set by the Ministry of Employment and Workforce (MOM). As of this writing, the minimum qualifying salary is **S$5,600 a month for most sectors**, rising with age to S$10,700 for applicants 45 and above. **Financial services roles sit higher, at S$6,200 a month**, rising to S$11,800 at 45 and above.

These thresholds are scheduled to rise again. From 1 January 2027, the general-sector minimum moves to S$6,000 (S$11,500 at 45+) and financial services moves to S$6,600 (S$12,700 at 45+), applying to new applications from that date and renewals expiring on or after 1 January 2028.

| Sector | Minimum qualifying salary now | At age 45 and above | From 1 January 2027 | At 45+ from 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Most sectors | S$5,600/month | S$10,700 | S$6,000 | S$11,500 |
| Financial services | S$6,200/month | S$11,800 | S$6,600 | S$12,700 |

The 2027 figures apply to new applications from that date, and to renewals expiring on or after 1 January 2028.

Treat these as a snapshot, not a promise. MOM adjusts EP criteria periodically, so check [MOM's Employment Pass eligibility page](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/eligibility) directly before you rely on a number for anything that matters, like negotiating a job offer or budgeting a lease.

If you're coming in through a specific sector, tech, finance, engineering, or research, the salary floor is only part of the picture. Where you should actually live depends heavily on your industry's geography and hours. We've written separate guides for [finance and banking professionals](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-finance-and-banking-professionals-in-singapore) and [engineers on project-based contracts](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-engineers-on-project-based-contracts-in-singapore) that go deep on those specifics. This guide stays at the level that applies to every first-time EP holder, regardless of sector.

## What Documents Do I Actually Need to Rent?

Three things, at minimum, before any landlord or co-living operator will let you sign:

1. **Your EP card, or your In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter if the card hasn't been issued yet.** The IPA is the letter MOM sends once your application is approved, before the physical card is printed. Some operators will let you reserve a room off the strength of an IPA, but the tenancy itself only gets finalised once the actual pass exists and can be verified against MOM's system.
2. **Your passport.** Used to cross-check your identity against the pass details, and standard practice for any lease involving a foreigner.
3. **Proof of employment.** Usually your offer letter or employment contract, sometimes a recent payslip if you've already started. This confirms the job that the EP itself is tied to.

A properly run operator will verify your pass is currently active using MOM's own "Check a Work Pass" service, not just eyeball the printed expiry date, since a pass can be cancelled before its printed expiry if your employment ends early. For the fuller breakdown of what foreigners need to rent in Singapore across every pass type, see our guide on [can foreigners rent in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore).

| Document | Why it is needed | Accepted substitute |
|---|---|---|
| EP card | Proves the pass exists and is active | An In-Principle Approval letter can reserve a room with some operators, but not finalise a tenancy |
| Passport | Cross-checks your identity against the pass details | None |
| Proof of employment | Confirms the job the EP is tied to | Offer letter, employment contract, or a recent payslip if you have started |

A properly run operator will verify the pass against MOM's "Check a Work Pass" service rather than eyeballing the printed expiry, since a pass can be cancelled early if your employment ends.

One timing trap worth flagging: you cannot sign a standard residential lease on a tourist stamp while you wait for your EP to be processed. If you land before your pass is issued, you're looking at a hotel or a licensed short-stay stay for those first days or weeks, not a regular tenancy.

## Why Is There a Three-Month Minimum Lease?

This surprises a lot of first-timers coming from countries where month-to-month renting is normal. In Singapore, the Urban Redevelopment Authority (URA) sets a legal minimum stay of three consecutive months for private residential property. Anything shorter counts as short-term accommodation, which isn't allowed outside licensed hotels and serviced apartments.

This isn't a landlord preference you can negotiate away. It's a planning rule that applies across the board, to condos, apartments, landed houses, and co-living rooms alike. HDB flats run an even longer floor, a six-month minimum tenancy, and only landlords who already hold HDB's approval to sublet to non-citizens can rent to you at all.

Practically, this means your shortest legitimate option as a new EP holder is a three-month room, not a one-month trial. Co-living is usually where that three-month floor is easiest to find without also committing to a full year, since most standalone condo and HDB leases default to twelve months as standard.

| Property type | Legal minimum tenancy | Extra condition |
|---|---|---|
| Private residential (condo, apartment, landed, co-living room) | 3 consecutive months, set by URA | Anything shorter counts as short-term accommodation, allowed only in licensed hotels and serviced apartments |
| HDB flat | 6 months | The landlord must already hold HDB approval to sublet to non-citizens |
| Standalone condo or HDB lease in practice | 12 months is the market default | Co-living is usually where the three-month floor is actually available |

## Do I Need a Singapore Bank Account Before I Rent?

Not on day one, but soon after. Most landlords and co-living operators in Singapore expect rent paid by local bank transfer, PayNow, or GIRO. A foreign credit card or an international wire for every month's rent is possible but clunky, and some operators simply won't set it up that way for you.

To open one, you'll typically need your EP card (some banks accept the IPA plus employment letter, others insist on the physical card), your passport, and proof of a local address, a small chicken-and-egg problem if you haven't signed a lease yet. Most first-timers solve it by using a digital-first bank with lighter address requirements, or their employer's registered address as a placeholder while the account opens. Either way, get this sorted in your first week or two, since a first month's rent that hasn't cleared is not the impression you want to make as a new tenant.

## Where Should I Actually Look for Housing?

This is the question the salary threshold and the documents don't answer, and it's the one first-timers get wrong most often. A few starting principles that apply regardless of what you do for work:

- **Commute distance to your office matters more than it looks on a map.** Singapore's MRT lines make some journeys that look far genuinely fast, and some journeys that look close genuinely slow, depending on how many transfers you need.
- **Budget for the whole picture, not just headline rent.** Deposit (typically one to two months), utilities, and sometimes a small agent fee all sit on top of the number in the listing.
- **Co-living is usually the fastest legitimate way in.** It bundles the three-month minimum, furnished rooms, and utilities into one line, without the twelve-month default that most standalone leases carry.

## 7 Mistakes First-Time EP Holders Make When Renting

1. **Signing a twelve-month lease before seeing the neighbourhood in daylight, on a weekday.** Photos and a Saturday-afternoon viewing don't show you the 8am commute, the noise level at night, or how far the nearest supermarket actually is on foot.
2. **Not reading the fine print on utilities and deposit.** Ask explicitly: is wifi included? Is there a cap on electricity before you're billed extra for aircon use? What exactly does the deposit not cover, and how long after move-out does it take to get back?
3. **Committing to a lease longer than your pass validity supports.** A fresh two-year EP gives you plenty of runway for a standard twelve-month lease. A pass with less time left on it, or one tied to probation or a contract role, is a different calculation.
4. **Trying to sign before the EP is actually issued.** An offer letter and an IPA can get you a reservation with some operators, but not a signed, binding tenancy.
5. **Underestimating the deposit and setup cash needed upfront.** Between a security deposit, first month's rent, and possibly an agent fee, expect to have several months' worth of rent in liquid cash before you move in, not just next month's payment.
6. **Assuming every landlord will accept a foreign bank transfer indefinitely.** Get a local account moving in your first couple of weeks so paying rent doesn't become a monthly headache.
7. **Choosing based on the flat alone and ignoring the household.** In co-living especially, who else lives there and how the common spaces actually get used day to day matters as much as the room itself.

## FAQ

**What is the minimum salary to qualify for an Employment Pass in Singapore?**
S$5,600 a month for most sectors, S$6,200 for financial services, as of this writing. Both figures rise with age and are scheduled to increase again from 1 January 2027. Confirm the current number on [MOM's website](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass/eligibility) before relying on it.

**Can I rent in Singapore before my EP card is issued?**
Not a standard lease. You can sometimes reserve a room with an offer letter and IPA, but the tenancy itself is only finalised once the pass is issued and can be verified.

**What's the shortest lease I can legally sign in Singapore?**
Three months for private residential property, which includes most co-living arrangements. HDB rentals run a six-month minimum.

**Do I need a Singapore bank account to rent a room?**
Not strictly to sign, but almost every landlord and operator expects rent paid via local transfer or GIRO once you've moved in, so open one within your first couple of weeks.

**Is co-living a good option for a first-time EP holder?**
Often yes, since it bundles the three-month legal minimum, furnished rooms, and utilities into a single monthly rate, without the twelve-month default that comes with most standalone condo or HDB leases.
