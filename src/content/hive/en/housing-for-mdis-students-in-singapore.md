MDIS (Management Development Institute of Singapore) runs its main campus at 501 Stirling Road, in the Queenstown area, with Queenstown MRT station as the nearest stop and a free weekday shuttle bus connecting the two. Most incoming students start out in MDIS's own on-campus residence, but places there are limited and priced as a package, so a good number end up looking at off-campus rooms too, whether that's for a longer stay, more independence, or simply because the residence is full for their intake.

This guide covers where MDIS actually sits, who studies there, what the on-campus option looks like, and what to know if you're weighing a private room off campus instead.

## Where Is MDIS Located in Singapore?

MDIS's main campus is at 501 Stirling Road, Singapore 148951, sitting in the Queenstown planning area on the East-West MRT Line. Queenstown MRT station (Exit D) is the nearest stop, and MDIS runs a complimentary shuttle bus between the station and campus on weekdays, with no weekend or public holiday service ([MDIS](https://www.mdis.edu.sg/)).

Being on the East-West Line matters for anyone planning a commute, since it's a single, direct line running from Jurong in the west through to Tampines in the east, with no transfer needed to reach most of the island's main hubs.

**One thing worth knowing if you're planning further ahead:** MDIS announced in February 2025 that its Singapore campus will relocate to 190 Changi Road, targeted for the first half of 2027 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_Development_Institute_of_Singapore)). If your course runs past that date, factor the move into any longer-term housing decision. `` the exact relocation date and transport links closer to the time, since neither has been finalised in public MDIS materials as of this writing.

## Who Studies at MDIS?

MDIS was founded in 1956 and operates as a not-for-profit institution, one of Singapore's longer-running private education providers. It runs degree and diploma programmes across business management, engineering, fashion design, nursing, mass communications, psychology, and hospitality management, plus O-Level and A-Level preparatory courses through its MDIS College arm ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_Development_Institute_of_Singapore)).

MDIS has long positioned itself around a genuinely international student mix. It set up a dedicated International Affairs Department back in 1998 specifically to support students arriving from overseas, and the campus itself describes a "diverse student community" drawing on students from across Asia and beyond ([MDIS](https://www.mdis.edu.sg/)). The scale of its own on-campus residence, built to house roughly 1,700 students, is itself a signal of how large that non-local, needs-housing cohort is, since students who already live in Singapore with family typically don't need a hostel bed.

## Does MDIS Provide On-Campus Housing?

Yes. MDIS Residences @ Stirling is a purpose-built, 15-storey residence hall next to the main campus, with 782 air-conditioned rooms and 14 suites across roughly 1,700 beds. It holds a Green Mark GoldPlus sustainability rating and includes its own cafeteria and a 500-seat auditorium ([MDIS](https://www.mdis.edu.sg/)).

That's a genuinely large facility, and for many first-time international students it's the simplest option: zero commute, everything arranged before you land, and a built-in community of other students on the same course. It's worth applying for early, since places in any institution's own residence are finite and tend to go fastest in the run-up to each intake.

## What Happens When Places Run Out or the Term Runs Long?

On-campus residence works well for a first semester or two, but it isn't unlimited. Some students don't get a place at all in a given intake, some are only offered a residence contract for part of their course, and others simply want more space, a private bathroom, or a bit more independence than a hostel room offers once they've settled in.

That's the point where off-campus housing near an MRT on the East-West Line, rather than specifically next to Stirling Road, becomes the practical search. Since the line runs the length of the island without a transfer, "close to MDIS" for off-campus purposes really means "on the East-West Line, at a stop with a reasonable direct ride," not necessarily a walk from the campus gates.

## On-Campus Residence vs Off-Campus Co-Living

| | MDIS Residences @ Stirling | Off-campus co-living |
|---|---|---|
| Commute | None, next to campus | A direct MRT ride, no transfer needed if on the East-West Line |
| Room type | Single hostel-style room | Private bedroom, ensuite or shared bathroom depending on the room |
| Availability | Limited places per intake | Generally more flexible on move-in timing |
| Best for | First semester, first-time arrivals wanting zero admin | Longer stays, more space or privacy, or when residence places are full |
| Lease length | Tied to residence contract terms | Set by the individual room listing |

Neither option is objectively better. A first-semester student arriving with no local network usually does best starting in the residence, close to campus and other new students. Someone further into their course, or arriving after residence places have filled, is often better served by a private room off campus with more control over lease length and privacy.

## What Should International MDIS Students Check Before Booking a Room?

- Confirm the room's nearest MRT station and whether it sits on the East-West Line, the most direct route to Stirling Road without a transfer.
- Ask whether furniture, wifi, and utilities are included, since arriving from overseas with nothing set up locally makes a move-in-ready room a genuine time saver.
- Check the minimum stay period against your actual semester or course length before signing anything.
- If your course runs into 2027, ask how the planned Changi Road relocation might affect your daily commute and factor that into a longer lease.
- Verify who handles maintenance and day-to-day issues in the property, so you know who to contact once you've moved in.
