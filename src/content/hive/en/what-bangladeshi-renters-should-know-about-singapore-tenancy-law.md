There's no separate tenancy law for Bangladeshi nationals in Singapore. The rules on minimum stay, deposits, and documentation are the same for you as for any other foreign renter. What actually differs, and it differs a lot, is your pass type. A large share of Bangladeshi nationals in Singapore hold a Work Permit in the construction, marine shipyard, or process sectors, and Work Permit housing works under a different set of rules than the tenancy law that applies to an Employment Pass, S Pass, Student Pass, or Dependant's Pass holder. Knowing which situation you're in before you start looking for a room saves a lot of wasted viewings.

This piece pulls together what we've already written on Singapore tenancy law and adds the one piece that's specific to Bangladeshi renters: how Work Permit housing rules interact with ordinary tenancy law, and where the two paths diverge.

## If You're on a Work Permit: Housing Works Differently

Bangladesh is one of Singapore's largest source countries for migrant labour, concentrated heavily in the Construction, Marine shipyard and Process sectors, generally referred to as CMP. Bangladesh's own Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training recorded over 70,000 Bangladeshis taking up jobs in Singapore in 2025 ([BMET, via Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/world/singapore-offers-foreign-worker-levy-rebate-to-companies-facing-labour-shortage-983806)).

If that's your situation, housing isn't something you arrange independently the way an Employment Pass holder would. Since 19 September 2023, employers of non-Malaysian Work Permit holders in the CMP sectors must show proof of acceptable accommodation, a tenancy agreement, rental agreement, or accommodation provider contract, before the worker can even enter Singapore ([MOM, Employers Required to Provide Proof of Accommodation](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2023/0911-employers-required-to-provide-proof-of-accommodation-before-their-wph-entry-into-singapore)). Acceptable housing includes purpose-built dormitories, factory-converted dormitories, construction temporary quarters, and other approved residential housing ([MOM, Housing](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/housing)).

Practically, this means your employer holds the housing arrangement, not you personally, and any address change must be reported to MOM. If you're in a non-CMP role, pre-entry approval is lighter, but your employer still has to notify MOM once you've entered a tenancy or room rental agreement.

## Why HDB Isn't an Option for Most Bangladeshi Work Permit Holders

This is the one rule that trips people up most, so it's worth stating plainly: non-Malaysian Work Permit holders in the CMP sectors cannot rent a whole HDB flat or even a room in one. HDB's own rental eligibility rules restrict CMP-sector Work Permit rental to Malaysians only, on the basis of long-standing cross-border ties between the two countries ([HDB, Eligibility Conditions and Guidelines](https://www.hdb.gov.sg/managing-my-home/home-ownership/renting-out-a-flat-or-bedrooms/renting-out-a-flat/eligibility)). Manufacturing-sector Work Permit holders face the same restriction for renting a whole flat.

That leaves private residential housing (condos, apartments, landed property) as the realistic path for a room outside employer-provided dormitory housing, which is exactly where co-living sits.

## If You're on an EP, S Pass, Student Pass, or Dependant's Pass: Standard Rules Apply

Not every Bangladeshi renter in Singapore is on a Work Permit. If you're here on an Employment Pass, S Pass, Student Pass, or as a Dependant's Pass holder joining a spouse, you rent the same way anyone else does: sign once your pass is actually issued and in hand, not on a job offer or an In-Principle Approval letter. If you're specifically weighing how your pass type should shape your lease length, particularly matching lease term to your pass's remaining validity rather than a landlord's default 12-month offer, that comparison is covered in [Renting on an EP vs S Pass vs Student Pass in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore).

## The Minimum Stay and Occupancy Rules That Apply to Any Private Rental

Once you're renting privately, on your own arrangement or one your employer has documented, the same underlying rules apply as for any tenant. URA sets a minimum stay of three consecutive months for private residential property, plus an occupancy cap of up to eight unrelated occupants for private units of at least 90 square metres, a relaxation HDB and URA extended through the end of 2028 as of January 2026. Full mechanics, including what counts as a breach and who's liable, are in [Is Co-Living Legal in Singapore? The Rules, Explained](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore).

| Rule | What applies | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum stay, private residential | 3 consecutive months for any occupant | URA |
| Minimum stay, HDB | 6 months | HDB |
| Occupancy cap | 6 unrelated occupants as standard. Private units of at least 90 sqm and 4-room-plus HDB flats can house up to 8, a relaxation HDB and URA extended through the end of 2028 in their January 2026 announcement | URA and HDB |
| Security deposit | One month's rent is the 2026 norm for a co-living room, refundable in full absent real damage beyond fair wear and tear. No law sets the figure, so it is the clause in your agreement that governs the amount and the return conditions | Your contract |
| Agreement type | Renting a room rather than a whole unit almost always means a licence agreement, not a tenancy agreement. Standard across co-living, and not a red flag by itself | Your contract |
| Lease length | Match it to your pass validity, not the landlord's default term | You |

These apply to any private rental regardless of nationality. The one place nationality does bite is HDB eligibility for CMP-sector Work Permit holders, covered above.

## Licence Agreement or Tenancy Agreement: What You're Actually Signing

If you're renting a room rather than a whole unit, expect a licence agreement, not a full tenancy agreement. That's standard for co-living across Singapore and isn't a red flag by itself. The real legal difference, and the clause worth reading closely before you sign, is set out in [Licence Agreement vs Tenancy Agreement in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/licence-agreement-vs-tenancy-agreement-singapore).

## Security Deposit: What's Standard

One month's rent is the norm for a co-living room deposit in Singapore, refundable in full barring real damage beyond fair wear and tear. There's no government-set figure, it's a contractual term, so it's the clause in your agreement that governs the amount and the conditions for getting it back. What a deposit can and can't legally be deducted for, and the move-in photos that turn a refund into a formality rather than a dispute, is covered in [Security Deposit Co-Living Singapore: What's Standard](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

## Stamp Duty on Your Agreement

If your room is documented under a lease or tenancy agreement, IRAS charges stamp duty at 0.4% of the total rent for the lease period, due within 14 days of signing. Tenant pays it by convention, though it's negotiable, and there's a floor below which no duty applies at all. The full rates and deadlines are in [Do I Pay Stamp Duty on a Room Rental in Singapore?](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/stamp-duty-room-rental-singapore) If you don't have a Singapore bank account yet, IRAS's e-Stamping portal accepts card payment, so that isn't a blocker.

## Practical Points Worth Planning For

**Address changes need reporting.** If you're a Work Permit holder and you move, your employer must update MOM with your new address. Flag any move before it happens, not after.

**Remittance timing.** If you send money home regularly, check rent due dates against your salary and remittance schedule before committing to a lease.

**Document consistency.** A mismatch between the spelling on your passport, your pass, and your employment letter can slow down verification. Check all three line up before your first viewing.

**Group moves.** Relocating with colleagues and want several rooms in one unit? The occupancy cap still applies regardless of group size. Confirm the unit's actual cleared occupancy count first.

| What you hold | Lets you sign a lease |
|---|---|
| A visa-free entry stamp, or a Short-Term Visit Pass | No. It is an entry privilege, not a renting one |
| A job offer letter | No |
| An In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter | No. Some operators will reserve a room against it, but the agreement is signed only once the pass exists |
| An issued Employment Pass, S Pass, Work Permit, Student Pass, Dependant's Pass or Long-Term Visit Pass | Yes, once it is in hand and verifiable against MOM's or ICA's systems |

## Documents to Have Ready

- Passport, valid for your intended stay
- Issued pass card (Work Permit, EP, S Pass, Student Pass, or Dependant's Pass), not just an offer letter or IPA
- Employment or enrolment letter matching your pass details
- A recent payslip or bank statement
- A local Singapore phone number
- An emergency contact name and number

## Where This Leaves You

The law itself doesn't change based on where you're from. What changes for Bangladeshi renters specifically is that a large share arrive on a Work Permit, where housing runs through the employer and MOM rather than an independent search, and where HDB isn't an option in the CMP sectors. If you're renting on an EP, S Pass, Student Pass, or Dependant's Pass instead, the standard private-rental rules apply exactly as they would to anyone else.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can a Bangladeshi Work Permit holder rent an HDB flat in Singapore?**
No, not in the CMP sectors. HDB restricts CMP-sector Work Permit rental, whole flat or a room, to Malaysians only, and manufacturing-sector holders face the same restriction for a whole flat. Private residential housing remains an option.

**Do I need my employer's involvement to rent a room as a Work Permit holder?**
Yes, in practice. Employers of CMP-sector Work Permit holders must show MOM proof of acceptable accommodation before the worker enters Singapore, and any address change must be reported afterward.

**Does the same tenancy law apply to Bangladeshi renters on an Employment Pass or Student Pass?**
Yes. Once your EP, S Pass, Student Pass, or Dependant's Pass is issued, you rent under the same rules as any other foreign tenant: minimum stay, occupancy cap, deposit norms, and stamp duty all apply equally.

**How many Bangladeshi nationals are working in Singapore?**
Bangladesh's Bureau of Manpower, Employment and Training recorded over 70,000 Bangladeshis taking up jobs in Singapore in 2025, most concentrated in construction, marine shipyard, and process work.

**Can I sort stamp duty without a Singapore bank account?**
Yes. IRAS's e-Stamping portal accepts card payment, so a local bank account isn't a prerequisite.
