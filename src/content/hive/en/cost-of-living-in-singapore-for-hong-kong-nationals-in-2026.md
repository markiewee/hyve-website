For a Hong Konger moving to Singapore alone in 2026, a realistic monthly cost of living, once you're settled into a room rather than a whole unit, runs roughly S$2,000 to S$3,000. That covers rent, food, transport, phone and a basic health buffer. At the exchange rate as this is written, roughly 1 SGD to HK$6.13, that range converts to about HK$12,260 to HK$18,400 a month. The number worth paying attention to isn't the SGD figure alone though. It's how that figure moves once you factor in the exchange rate, Singapore's tax and CPF system versus Hong Kong's salaries tax and MPF, and what it costs to move money between the two.

Day-to-day costs like food and transport are the same math any newcomer to Singapore works through. The HKD conversion, the tax and retirement-fund comparison, and the remittance question are specific to a Hong Kong move, and that's what this piece focuses on.

## The SGD to HKD exchange rate is the first number to get right

As of early August 2026, 1 Hong Kong Dollar buys around S$0.163, which inverts to roughly 1 Singapore Dollar buying HK$6.13. Across 2026 the rate has held in a fairly tight band, with HKD/SGD trading between about 0.161 and 0.168 over the year, meaning 1 SGD has been worth somewhere between roughly HK$5.94 and HK$6.20.

One thing that makes this easier than some currency pairs: the Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the US dollar within a set band, so HKD/SGD movement is really driven by how the Singapore dollar moves against the US dollar, not by anything happening to the HKD itself. That makes the rate more predictable to plan around, though it's still worth checking the live rate before converting a real transfer rather than budgeting off a number from a headline months ago.

A S$2,500 monthly budget works out to roughly HK$15,325 at the middle of that 2026 range, but a move of just 0.1 in the HKD/SGD rate shifts that by several hundred HKD.

These are Singapore-wide market ranges, not a quote for any specific plan, and daily habits move them a lot.

## Income tax, CPF and MPF: what actually changes on your payslip

This is the comparison that catches Hong Kong movers off guard, because the two systems don't map onto each other cleanly.

Singapore's resident personal income tax is progressive, running from 0% up to 24% on chargeable income above S$1,000,000, with the first S$20,000 tax-free. Hong Kong's salaries tax is progressive from 2% to 17%, or capped at a 15% standard rate on income before personal allowances, whichever works out lower for the taxpayer. At typical professional income levels, Singapore's system tends to come out ahead for income under roughly S$300,000, while Hong Kong's 15% cap starts to win out at higher income bands.

The bigger structural difference is retirement contributions. In Hong Kong, MPF is mandatory: 5% from you and 5% from your employer, capped at HK$1,500 a month each side once income reaches HK$30,000. In Singapore, CPF only applies to citizens and permanent residents. As a foreigner on an Employment Pass or S Pass, no CPF is deducted from your salary, and your employer pays none either. That's one fewer mandatory deduction on your Singapore payslip, but it also means no local retirement savings are being built for you the way MPF does at home. Worth setting up your own savings discipline for that gap rather than assuming it's handled.

## What work pass applies to you

Hong Kong nationals go through the same Ministry of Manpower work pass system as any other foreign national moving to Singapore for work. There's no Hong Kong-specific pathway. Which pass applies depends on role and salary:

- **Employment Pass**, for professionals and managers, requires a qualifying salary of at least S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services) as of 2026, rising to S$6,000 and S$6,600 respectively from 1 January 2027.
- **S Pass**, for mid-skilled roles, requires a minimum qualifying salary of S$3,300 a month (S$3,800 in financial services), rising with age.
- **Work Permit**, for skilled and semi-skilled roles in specific sectors, applies to a narrower set of jobs and doesn't cover most professional relocations.

One practical point that does help a Hong Kong move: HKSAR passport holders don't need a visa for a business or social visit to Singapore of up to 30 days, which makes an in-person viewing trip or interview trip straightforward to plan without a separate visa application. That's entirely separate from needing a work pass to actually take up employment, which still applies regardless of passport.

## Sending money between Hong Kong and Singapore

If you're keeping savings in Hong Kong, supporting family there, or moving a lump sum over before you land, the transfer method affects how much actually arrives more than most people expect. Bank wire transfers typically mark up the exchange rate on top of a flat fee, which is easy to miss if you're only looking at the fee line. Dedicated transfer apps using a rate close to the mid-market rate, with one transparent fee shown upfront, generally land more HKD or SGD for the same transfer, and several now settle same-day. Compare the total amount that arrives, not just the advertised fee, since a low fee paired with a weak rate can cost more overall.

## Is Singapore actually more expensive than Hong Kong?

It depends on what you're comparing. General cost-of-living indices put the two cities within a roughly 15 percentage point band of each other, with the direction depending on source and methodology. Hong Kong tends to run higher on daily dining and eating out, Singapore tends to run higher on utilities and some services. Rent has enough nuance of its own, room sizes, deposit norms, lease lengths, that it deserves its own dedicated comparison rather than a paragraph here.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to live in Singapore as a Hong Konger in 2026?**
A realistic all-in monthly budget for someone renting a room rather than a whole unit is roughly S$2,000 to S$3,000, covering rent, food, transport, phone and a basic health buffer. That converts to roughly HK$12,260 to HK$18,400 at rates seen through 2026, though the exact HKD figure depends on the rate at the time you convert.

**Do Hong Kong nationals need a work pass to work in Singapore?**
Yes. There's no Hong Kong-specific exemption from Singapore's work pass system. Employment Pass, S Pass, or Work Permit all apply depending on role and salary, the same as for any other foreign national, though a 30-day visa-free visit is available for interview or viewing trips before you commit.

**Is CPF deducted from a Hong Konger's Singapore salary?**
No. CPF contributions apply only to Singapore citizens and permanent residents. Foreigners working on an Employment Pass or S Pass have no CPF deducted, and their employer contributes none either, unlike Hong Kong's mandatory MPF, which applies to almost all employees.

**What's the best way to send money between Hong Kong and Singapore?**
Dedicated transfer apps using a rate close to the mid-market exchange rate with one transparent fee generally beat traditional bank wires, which tend to mark up the rate as well as charge a fee. Compare the total amount received on the other end, not just the advertised fee.

**Is Singapore more expensive than Hong Kong overall?**
It's close, and depends heavily on category and lifestyle. General indices put the two cities within about 15 percentage points of each other either way. Hong Kong tends to run higher on dining out, Singapore tends to run higher on utilities and some services, and rent is enough of its own topic to need a separate, dedicated comparison.
