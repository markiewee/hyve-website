The cost of living in Singapore for a Bangladeshi professional or student in 2026 typically runs S$1,500 to S$1,900 a month for everyday costs, before rent. Add a room in a shared home, usually S$800 to S$1,800 depending on room type and location, and a realistic all-in monthly budget lands between S$2,300 and S$3,700. That figure covers food, transport, phone and wifi, and remittance costs, but not the rent for a whole unit, which runs considerably higher.

This piece is written for Bangladeshi nationals renting in the open market: professionals on an Employment Pass or S Pass, and students. It's worth saying upfront that this isn't the whole picture of Bangladeshi nationals in Singapore. A large share are Work Permit holders in construction, marine, and manufacturing, and under Ministry of Manpower rules their employer arranges dormitory or approved housing directly, so they aren't shopping the open rental market the way this article assumes. If that's your situation, the budget below won't apply the same way, since housing is handled outside your own monthly rent line.

## What does it cost to live in Singapore as a Bangladeshi national in 2026?

Singapore's general cost of living for a single person, excluding rent, runs roughly S$1,500 to S$1,900 a month, covering groceries, transport, phone, and everyday spending. That's a general baseline, not built around any one nationality.

For a Bangladeshi national specifically, two things move the number. Bengali staples and specific spices cost more here than at home, though far less than most newcomers expect once they find the right shops. Remittance home is also a real recurring cost that a generic cost-of-living figure leaves out entirely.

Bangladeshi nationals in Singapore span a wide range, from Work Permit holders in CMP sectors to a smaller, growing number of professionals and students on Employment Pass, S Pass, and student pass. Desker Road, nicknamed Little Bangladesh, and the wider Little India and Farrer Park area are where the community's grocery, food, and remittance infrastructure is concentrated, which keeps day-to-day costs manageable regardless of which pass you're on.

## Rent: the single biggest line item

Rent dominates the monthly number more than anything else. A room in a shared home typically runs S$800 to S$1,800 a month depending on room type, with all-inclusive co-living rooms bundling utilities and wifi into that one figure. A whole studio or one-bedroom unit costs considerably more and almost never includes utilities in the headline rent.

## Bengali food and groceries: what it actually costs

Cooking with Bengali staples in Singapore costs more than at home, but Desker Road and the shops around Mustafa Centre make it manageable. A monthly grocery habit built around rice, dal, mustard oil, and specific spices typically runs S$150 to S$300 a month for a single person cooking regularly.

Desker Road, at the intersection with Lembu Road, is the informal hub known locally as Little Bangladesh, dense with grocers, remittance counters, phone shops, and Bengali eateries. Fakruddin, near Mustafa Centre, is a commonly recommended stop for Bengali-style food, and Mustafa itself, open 24 hours, carries a wide halal grocery range.

Eating out doesn't have to be expensive. A hawker meal, including halal stalls in most hawker centres, runs S$5 to S$10. Mixing hawker meals with home cooking, a realistic monthly food budget is S$350 to S$600 for someone cooking often, or S$650 to S$950 for someone eating out more.

## Work passes: what they mean for the budget

Most Bangladeshi professionals moving to Singapore for skilled roles arrive on an Employment Pass or S Pass, and the pass type shapes the budget indirectly through the minimum qualifying salary tied to it. As of 2026, the Employment Pass requires a minimum qualifying salary of roughly S$5,600 a month for general sectors, and the S Pass requires roughly S$3,300, both rising with age.

These are floors set by MOM, not typical offer levels, so most incoming professionals land above them. Someone earning well above the EP minimum has meaningfully more room in the budget than someone closer to the S Pass floor.

## Sending money back to Bangladesh: remittance costs

Sending money home is a genuine recurring cost that generic cost-of-living guides skip entirely. The SGD to BDT corridor has a wide range of providers, and the gap between the cheapest and most expensive option is large enough to matter every single month.

Digital-first providers like Wise typically price close to the mid-market exchange rate with a transparent, low flat fee, often working out to under 1% of the transfer for a typical monthly remittance. Traditional bank wire transfers and some cash-pickup counters carry a much wider margin on the exchange rate, which is where most of the real cost hides rather than in the visible fee.

Desker Road and the Little India area have a dense cluster of remittance counters that Bangladeshi workers use on Sundays, their common rest day, alongside digital apps that let you send from your phone without queueing.

## Flights and staying connected to family

Round-trip flights between Singapore and Dhaka are frequent, with prices commonly ranging from roughly S$220 to S$460 depending on airline, season, and how far ahead you book. Fares tend to be cheapest outside major holiday periods, so book six to eight weeks ahead where possible around Eid.

Budgeting one or two trips home a year, plus an occasional emergency fare, is a realistic line item most generic Singapore cost-of-living guides leave out.

## Where Bangladeshi professionals and students tend to live

Little India, Farrer Park, and the area around Desker Road are the obvious first answer, since groceries, remittance services, mosques, and a built-in community make the early adjustment easier. For many working professionals, though, they're not the most practical base once commute to the CBD or a business park is factored in.

Co-living rooms across the island put you within reach of Desker Road and Mustafa Centre without requiring you to live there specifically, since MRT access makes a 15 to 20 minute trip routine. ## A realistic monthly budget for a Bangladeshi professional in Singapore

| Item | Typical monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Room rent (shared home, often all-inclusive) | S$800 to S$1,800 |
| Food, including Bengali groceries | S$350 to S$600 |
| Transport (public transport, daily commute) | S$100 to S$150 |
| Phone and wifi (where not bundled into rent) | S$30 to S$60 |
| Remittance to Bangladesh (fee and spread only, not the transfer amount itself) | S$10 to S$40 |
| Flights home (averaged monthly, 1 to 2 trips a year) | S$40 to S$80 |
| **Total, room + living costs** | **S$1,330 to S$2,730** |

This is a working professional's baseline, not a hard ceiling. Someone renting a whole unit alone instead of a room should add roughly S$1,000 to S$2,500 more to the rent line, since a full apartment costs considerably more and rarely includes utilities.

## Myth: Bengali groceries make Singapore unaffordable

This gets said a lot, and it's overstated. Yes, specific imported ingredients cost more here than at home. But once you know Desker Road and the shops around Mustafa Centre, that's one manageable line in a monthly budget, not the whole budget.

The bigger cost driver by far is rent, the same as it is for every nationality moving to Singapore. Anyone comparing headline "cost of living in Singapore" figures against Bangladesh should be comparing rent-adjusted numbers, not grocery receipts, since rent is where the real gap sits.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a realistic monthly budget for a Bangladeshi national living in Singapore in 2026?**
For a single professional renting a room, roughly S$2,300 to S$3,700 a month all-in, covering rent, food, transport, phone, remittance costs, and an averaged flight budget. Renting a whole unit alone pushes that meaningfully higher.

**Are Bengali groceries expensive in Singapore?**
Specific imported staples cost more than they do in Bangladesh, but a monthly grocery budget for one person, sourced from Desker Road or the shops around Mustafa Centre, typically runs S$150 to S$300.

**What's the cheapest way to send money from Singapore to Bangladesh?**
Digital-first remittance apps like Wise generally beat traditional bank wire transfers on both fee and exchange-rate spread for the SGD-BDT corridor. Compare live quotes across two or three providers before choosing, since rates and fees shift.

No. A large share are Work Permit holders in construction, marine, and manufacturing whose employer arranges dormitory or approved housing directly under MOM rules. This article, and the budget in it, is written for professionals and students renting a room or unit on the open market.

**Do I need an Employment Pass or S Pass to rent in Singapore?**
You need a valid pass to rent legally as a foreigner, and most landlords and co-living operators will ask to see it as part of document checks. The specifics by pass type are covered in [can foreigners rent in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore).
