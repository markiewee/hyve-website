The cost of living in Singapore for Myanmar nationals in 2026 typically runs S$1,300 to S$2,600 a month for a single person, covering food, transport, phone and wifi, and remittances home, before rent is added. Add rent for a room in a shared home, usually S$800 to S$1,800 depending on room type and location, and a realistic all-in monthly number lands between S$2,100 and S$4,400. Someone on a Work Permit and housed by an employer skips the rent line entirely; this guide is built for professionals, students, and Dependant's Pass holders who are renting privately.

Three things move that range more than anything else for a Myanmar national's budget specifically: which pass you're on, how much you send home each month, and the Kyat's exchange rate, which moves more than most currencies. This piece breaks all three down, with a full monthly budget at the end.

## What does it cost to live in Singapore as a Myanmar national in 2026?

Singapore's general cost of living for a single person, excluding rent, runs roughly S$1,500 a month on Numbeo's August 2026 data, covering groceries, transport, phone, and everyday spending. That baseline isn't built around any one nationality.

For a Myanmar national specifically, two things push costs in different directions. Groceries and eating out cost more than at home, but daily hawker food and public transport keep the baseline manageable. Remittances are a real recurring cost that generic cost-of-living figures skip, and the Kyat's exchange rate volatility makes that line harder to plan around than for most currencies.

Myanmar is one of Singapore's Non-Traditional Source (NTS) countries for Work Permit holders, alongside Bangladesh, India, Sri Lanka, and Thailand, meaning Myanmar workers are hired into specific sectors under MOM's NTS Occupation List. A growing number of Myanmar nationals are also here on Employment Pass or S Pass in professional roles, and on Student Pass. This guide is written for anyone renting privately, since Work Permit holders housed in employer dormitories have a different cost structure entirely.

## Rent: the single biggest line item

Rent dominates the monthly number more than any other cost, the same as it does for every nationality moving to Singapore. A room in a shared home typically runs S$800 to S$1,800 a month depending on room type, with all-inclusive co-living rooms bundling utilities and wifi into that figure. A whole studio or one-bedroom unit runs considerably more, and rarely includes utilities in the headline rent.

That piece isn't Myanmar-specific, but the rent math is identical regardless of where you're moving from. This article focuses on what changes on top of that baseline.

## Food and groceries: what changes for a Myanmar national

Cooking with Myanmar staples costs more than sourcing the same ingredients at home. A monthly grocery habit built around fish paste, tea leaf salad ingredients, and specific rice varieties typically runs S$150 to S$300 for a single person cooking regularly, sourced from Peninsula Plaza or a general wet market.

Peninsula Plaza, near City Hall MRT, is Singapore's best-known hub for the Myanmar community, often called "Little Myanmar." It holds Myanmar grocery stores, restaurants, remittance agents, and a Burmese-language library, all in one building.

A hawker meal runs S$5 to S$10, and Peninsula Plaza's food stalls serve Myanmar dishes at similar prices. Mixing hawker meals with home cooking, a realistic monthly food budget is S$350 to S$600 for someone cooking often, or S$600 to S$900 for someone eating out more.

## Work passes: what they mean for the budget

Most Myanmar nationals in Singapore are here on a Work Permit, since Myanmar sits on MOM's Non-Traditional Source list for the construction, marine, and process sectors. Work Permit holders are almost always housed by their employer, which removes the rent line from the budget entirely but also removes the choice of where to live.

A separate and growing group is here on Employment Pass or S Pass in professional roles. As of 2026, the Employment Pass requires a minimum qualifying salary of roughly S$5,600 a month in most sectors, and the S Pass requires roughly S$3,300, both rising with age. These are minimums, not typical offer levels, so most incoming professionals land above the floor.

Students on a Student Pass and family members on a Dependant's Pass are the other two groups renting privately in meaningful numbers. Document requirements by pass type are covered in [renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) and [can foreigners rent in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore).

## Sending money back to Myanmar: remittance costs and the Kyat problem

Sending money home is a real recurring cost, and for a Myanmar national it's more complicated than most corridors because the Kyat runs on a dual exchange rate system. The official rate sits far below the market rate that remittance providers and banks in Myanmar actually use, and the gap moves with conditions inside the country.

Because of that spread, the exchange rate you're actually given matters more than the headline fee. Licensed remittance operators, the kind found at Peninsula Plaza and through regulated apps, generally pass through a rate closer to the market rate than an unregulated channel, and Myanmar has pushed migrant workers toward officially licensed channels in recent years.

Budget the remittance as whatever amount you choose to send, and budget the fee and rate spread separately: a well-chosen licensed provider keeps that small, while a poor exchange rate can run several times higher. Compare a live quote from two or three providers before committing to one for regular transfers.

## Flights and staying connected to family

Round-trip flights between Singapore and Yangon are frequent, with Singapore Airlines, Myanmar Airways International, and SriLankan Airlines all flying the route. Off-peak round trips typically run S$260 to S$380. Prices rise around Thingyan (Myanmar New Year, mid-April) and year-end, so book six to eight weeks ahead for those dates.

Budgeting one or two trips home a year, plus an occasional emergency fare, is a line item most generic cost-of-living guides skip.

Co-living rooms across Singapore put you within an MRT ride of Peninsula Plaza without requiring you to base your search around it, which matters more for commute to work or campus. This is a working professional's or student's baseline, not a hard ceiling. Someone renting a whole unit alone instead of a room should add roughly S$1,000 to S$2,500 more to the rent line, since a full apartment costs considerably more and rarely includes utilities.

## Myth: cost of living in Singapore is the same for everyone from Myanmar

It isn't, and treating it as one number misses the point. A Work Permit holder is usually housed by their employer, with almost no rent line at all. A professional on an Employment Pass renting privately is closer to the budget table above, with rent as the dominant cost. Conflating the two gives a misleading number either way.

The other thing worth correcting: the Kyat's dual exchange rate makes remittance comparisons unreliable if they're not dated. A rate quoted a few months ago may already be stale, so always check a live quote rather than relying on a fixed figure, including the ones in this article.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a realistic monthly budget for a Myanmar national living in Singapore in 2026?**
For a single professional or student renting a room privately, roughly S$2,100 to S$4,400 a month all-in, covering rent, food, transport, phone, remittance costs, and an averaged flight budget. Work Permit holders housed by their employer have a very different, much lower cost structure since rent isn't part of their budget.

**Why is sending money from Singapore to Myanmar more complicated than other corridors?**
Myanmar runs a dual exchange rate system, with an official rate well below the market rate remittance providers and banks actually use. The gap shifts with conditions inside Myanmar, so a live quote from a licensed provider beats a fixed number.

**Is Peninsula Plaza the best place for a Myanmar national to live in Singapore?**
It's the best place to shop, eat, and connect with the community, but it's a commercial building, not a residential neighbourhood. Most Myanmar nationals renting privately live elsewhere and visit on weekends.

**Do I need a Work Permit, S Pass, or Employment Pass to rent in Singapore?**
You need a valid pass to rent legally as a foreigner, and most landlords and co-living operators will ask to see it during document checks. The specifics by pass type are covered in [can foreigners rent in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore).

**Are Myanmar groceries expensive in Singapore?**
Myanmar staples generally cost more here than at home, similar to the gap most nationalities face, but a monthly grocery budget for one person sourced from Peninsula Plaza typically runs S$150 to S$300, which is a manageable slice of the overall budget rather than the main driver. Rent remains the biggest line item by far.
