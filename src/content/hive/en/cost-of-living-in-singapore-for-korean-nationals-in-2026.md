The cost of living in Singapore for Korean nationals runs roughly two times higher than Seoul overall, driven almost entirely by rent. A single professional on an Employment Pass renting a room in a shared home should budget around S$1,900 to S$2,900 a month all in: rent, food, transport, and phone. A family renting a full unit and sending one child to a Korean-curriculum or international school can clear S$8,500 to S$11,500 a month once school fees are added.

The rest of this piece breaks down where those numbers come from, plus the costs a move from Korea specifically runs into that a generic "moving to Singapore" budget skips: Korean grocery pricing, KRW to SGD remittance, and flights home for Chuseok or Lunar New Year.

## How much more expensive is Singapore than Seoul?

Cost-of-living comparison data puts Seoul at roughly 58% to 61% cheaper than Singapore overall, once housing, groceries, and daily spending are weighed together. Average one-bedroom rent comparisons show Seoul sitting well under half of Singapore's, which is the single biggest driver of the gap.

Day-to-day costs narrow that gap a lot. Hawker food, public transport, and mobile data in Singapore land closer to Seoul prices than the headline "expensive country" reputation suggests. Housing is where the real jump happens, not groceries or getting around.

## What does rent actually cost, and why a room usually makes more sense first

A room in a shared home in Singapore typically runs S$600 to S$1,200 for a basic or HDB room, up to S$1,700 to S$2,500 for a master bedroom or ensuite. A studio or one-bedroom unit on your own usually starts around S$1,900 and can pass S$3,500 in a central location. For a first move, before you have a Singapore salary history or a fixed view on which neighbourhood suits you, a room is the faster and lower-risk option.

There's a practical reason beyond cost too. Many landlords doing a private tenancy want a local guarantor or a Singapore bank statement history, which a first-time arrival doesn't have yet. A co-living licence agreement or a master-lease operator isn't underwriting you as an individual the same way, so that friction mostly disappears.

For the fuller breakdown of deposits, stamp duty, and what's included in rent versus billed separately, see [cost of renting in Singapore 2026](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/cost-of-renting-singapore-2026-budget). That piece covers the general newcomer math in more depth than we'll repeat here.

## Food, Korean groceries, and daily spending

A hawker-heavy diet runs roughly S$500 to S$1,000 a month, a genuine bargain by Singapore standards. Groceries alone, if you cook mostly local ingredients, land closer to S$200 to S$400 a month for one person.

Korean groceries specifically push that number up. Singapore has a real network of Korean supermarkets, including Koryo Mart (K-Market) with outlets across the island at malls like Northpoint City, Parkway Parade, and Compass One, plus smaller stores like Sarangmart. Kimchi, Korean seasoning, snacks, and instant noodles are all available, but imported pricing runs noticeably above what the same items cost at home. Someone eating Korean at home several times a week should budget closer to S$700 to S$1,200 a month for food overall, factoring in the import premium and the occasional Korean restaurant meal.

## Transport costs in Singapore

Public transport for a daily commute typically costs S$100 to S$150 a month on the MRT and buses, using a contactless card or SimplyGo. That's genuinely cheap. Add regular Grab rides, especially for late nights or grocery runs with heavy bags, and transport can climb to S$300 to S$400 a month.

Compared to getting around Seoul, which also has a dense and cheap metro system, this is a rare category where the gap between the two cities barely shows up. Singapore's MRT covers most of the island reliably, so it's a genuine default rather than a backup plan.

## Phone, data, and staying connected

A local SIM matters from week one, both for practical reasons (banking OTPs, delivery apps, work logins) and because Korean roaming plans get expensive fast if relied on past the first few days. Prepaid SIM-only plans in Singapore start from around S$7 to S$15 a month for a moderate data allowance, with heavier data plans (500GB to 1TB) running S$14 to S$25.

Setting this up on arrival, alongside opening a local bank account, is worth doing early rather than putting off. Both usually need your pass or entry stamp and a local address, another reason having a room booked before landing helps.

## Sending money home: KRW to SGD remittance

This is a cost line that catches a lot of Korean nationals out, since Korea's own transfer apps don't cover Singapore the way they cover domestic transfers. Bank wires from Singapore to Korea typically carry a fee, plus an exchange rate margin, that together push the total cost well above what a dedicated transfer service charges.

Digital transfer providers are meaningfully cheaper. As of early August 2026, Wise quoted a fee of roughly S$7 to send S$1,000 to a Korean bank account at the mid-market rate, versus a reported average of around 8.8% of the transfer amount through traditional banks. The mid-market SGD to KRW rate has sat roughly between 1,100 and 1,175 won per Singapore dollar through mid-2026, so check the rate on the day rather than budgeting off a fixed number.

## Employment Pass and other pass costs

Pass eligibility and fees apply the same way regardless of nationality; there's no separate fee schedule for Korean applicants. As of 2026, the standard Employment Pass costs S$105 for the application and a further S$225 if approved, so budget roughly S$330 total per pass, on top of whatever an employment agent or corporate services firm charges if one's involved. Minimum qualifying salary for an EP starts around S$5,600 a month in most sectors, higher in financial services and for older applicants. Most nationalities, Korean passport holders included, don't face the additional Multiple Journey Visa surcharge that applies to some countries, but confirm this with your employer's HR or immigration agent before assuming either way.

## Flights home and visiting during holidays

One-way flights from Singapore to Seoul Incheon start from around S$179 on a good fare, with round trips from roughly S$268 on carriers like T'way Air. Round-trip fares around Chuseok or Lunar New Year cost more than off-peak dates, so booking a few months ahead for major holiday windows pays off. Budgeting one or two trips home a year, even at the higher end, is a manageable line item next to rent.

## If you're bringing family: school costs are the real budget swing

For a single professional, school fees don't apply and the numbers above hold. For a family moving with a child, school tuition is the single biggest additional cost. The Singapore Korean International School charges roughly S$13,400 a year for kindergarten, rising to around S$25,000 a year for high school, with an average annual fee across levels of roughly S$18,000. That's on top of a one-time registration or application fee and extras like school bus transport and uniforms.

Broader international schools in Singapore run a similar or wider range, so price this out early and separately from the household budget. It can easily double a family's total monthly spend on its own.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Singapore much more expensive than Seoul for daily living?**
Yes, on housing especially. Cost comparison data puts Seoul at roughly 58% to 61% cheaper than Singapore overall. Daily costs like hawker food, transport, and mobile data are closer between the two cities than the housing gap suggests.

**Can I find Korean groceries in Singapore easily?**
Yes. Korean supermarket chains like Koryo Mart (K-Market) and Sarangmart operate multiple outlets across Singapore, stocking kimchi, seasonings, and Korean snacks. Prices sit above local grocery staples due to import costs, so a Korean-heavy diet costs more than eating mostly hawker or local supermarket food.

**What does an Employment Pass cost in 2026?**
S$105 for the application and S$225 if approved, roughly S$330 total, plus any agent fees. The minimum qualifying salary starts around S$5,600 a month in most sectors. Fees and eligibility are the same regardless of nationality.

**Is it cheaper to rent a room or a whole apartment when relocating to Singapore?**
A room is significantly cheaper, and it's also the easier option for a first move since it usually doesn't require a local guarantor or a Singapore rental history. For the full deposit and stamp duty breakdown, see [cost of renting in Singapore 2026](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/cost-of-renting-singapore-2026-budget).

**What's the cheapest way to send money between Singapore and Korea?**
Dedicated transfer services like Wise are typically far cheaper than a bank wire, since banks tend to bundle in a wider exchange rate margin on top of a flat fee. Compare a couple of providers' live quotes before sending, since fees and rates shift often.
