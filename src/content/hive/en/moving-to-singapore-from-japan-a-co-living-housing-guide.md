Moving to Singapore from Japan is straightforward on the entry side, since Japanese passport holders already get 90 days visa-free, plenty of time to fly in, view rooms, and start the paperwork. The part that actually decides your housing is a document you won't have on day one: a Student Pass, Employment Pass, or S Pass has to be properly issued before you can sign a lease. An offer letter or an internal transfer memo from head office doesn't count.

For most people arriving from Japan, whether on a company posting, a new graduate role, or a university placement, the practical move is a furnished co-living room for the first few months, then a longer-term decision once the pass clears and the job or course has actually started.

## Do I Need a Visa Just to Enter Singapore From Japan?

No, not for a short visit. Japanese passport holders get 90 days of visa-free entry to Singapore, one of the longest windows Singapore grants any nationality, enough for a relocation trip, room viewings, and a first pass at the paperwork.

It doesn't cover actually living here. Entering visa-free and holding a valid immigration pass are two separate things, and only the pass lets you sign a residential lease or licence agreement. If you're using part of that window to sort housing before your pass is issued, a short-term co-living booking bridges the gap far more cheaply than weeks in a hotel.

## Which Pass Do I Actually Need Before I Can Rent?

For students, it's a Student Pass, applied for by your school through ICA's SOLAR system once you have an offer. Processing typically takes four to eight weeks, and the pass fee is S$90.

For working professionals, it's an Employment Pass or S Pass, sponsored by your employer. As of 2026, the Employment Pass qualifying salary starts at S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), scaling up with age, and rises again from 1 January 2027. Applicants also need to clear the COMPASS points framework, which scores an application on qualifications, salary relative to local peers, and workforce diversity. The S Pass floor sits at S$3,300 a month (S$3,800 in financial services) for younger applicants, moving up from 1 January 2027.

| Pass | Who applies | Threshold | Timeline and fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| Student Pass | Your school, through ICA's SOLAR system once you have an offer | Enrolment | Typically four to eight weeks, S$90 |
| Employment Pass | Your employer | From S$5,600/month, S$6,200 in financial services, scaling with age and rising again from 1 January 2027. Must also clear the COMPASS points framework, which scores qualifications, salary relative to local peers, and workforce diversity | Broadly similar |
| S Pass | Your employer | S$3,300/month, S$3,800 in financial services, for younger applicants, moving up from 1 January 2027 | Broadly similar |

A secondment letter, an offer letter, or an in-principle approval is not an issued pass. A properly run operator will not let you sign a lease on any of those alone.

## How Far Ahead Should I Book Housing?

Line your housing search up against your pass timeline, not your flight date. A Student Pass typically clears four to eight weeks after your school submits it, and an Employment Pass or S Pass moves on a broadly similar schedule.

Book something short and flexible to land in, then commit to a longer lease once your pass is confirmed and your start date is locked. This is why co-living tends to suit the first stretch better than a standard 12-month tenancy: you're not signing a rigid contract before you know your issue date.

## What Does Housing Actually Cost?

Rooms in Singapore's shared housing market run from about S$600 for a basic budget room up to S$2,500 for a private ensuite, with most standard common rooms landing between S$1,000 and S$1,600 a month. Co-living rooms, furnished and usually bundled with utilities and wifi, typically sit in the S$1,200 to S$2,500+ range.

Add food, transport, and a local SIM, and a realistic all-in monthly budget for a newcomer renting a room lands somewhere between S$1,800 and S$2,800. For upfront cash, plan for a security deposit (one month's rent is standard) plus your first month's rent, so two months' rent in hand before you get the keys is the safe assumption. We've broken deposit terms down further in our guide to [security deposits in Singapore co-living](https://lazybee.sg/blog/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

## Where Do Japanese Expats and Students Actually Live?

There's no single "Japanese district" in Singapore, but a few areas have a real concentration. West Coast and Clementi have grown into a hub for Japanese families, largely because The Japanese School Singapore runs its elementary campuses out of Clementi (95 Clementi Road, opposite NUS) and Changi, both tied to the Japanese Association of Singapore. If you have school-age kids, proximity to one of these campuses matters more than almost anything else on the list.

For food and community, the pocket of shops beside Cuppage Terrace, often called Little Japan by regulars, packs in izakayas, sushi counters, and late-night ramen spots aimed at Japanese residents rather than tourists, a short walk from Somerset MRT. Katong and the East Coast corridor have also become popular with newer arrivals for the food scene and calmer streets, though that's a broader expat draw rather than a specifically Japanese one.

A little over 31,000 Japanese nationals were registered as residing in Singapore at the most recent Japanese Ministry of Foreign Affairs count, one of the larger Japanese communities in Southeast Asia. The Japanese Association Singapore runs a clubhouse and a monthly newsletter, The Southern Cross, worth joining early.

## Opening a Bank Account and Getting Set Up

You'll generally need your issued pass, not just an offer letter, to open an account at most banks. DBS accepts online applications and will work from an ICA in-principle approval plus an employer or school letter if your pass hasn't landed yet, while UOB typically expects a branch visit with the pass already in hand. Bring your passport and proof of a local address; your co-living agreement works for this.

The bigger adjustment once the account's open is usually a local phone number and a PayNow-linked account, since a lot of everyday payments here, from splitting a bill to paying a landlord, run through PayNow rather than a card.

## Working for a Japanese Company's Singapore Office

Singapore is a common regional headquarters base for Japanese trading houses, manufacturers, and financial firms, so many Japanese Employment Pass holders arrive on an internal transfer rather than a fresh local hire. The pass process is identical either way, since MOM doesn't run a separate track by employer nationality.

If you're on a transfer with a housing allowance attached, check exactly what it covers before signing a 12-month lease you haven't seen in person. A short co-living booking for the first month keeps you flexible until you've walked the commute yourself.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The most common one is locking into a long lease before the pass is actually issued, then scrambling when the issue date shifts by a couple of weeks. A second is assuming the 90-day visa-free window means you can settle straight into a normal tenancy; it only covers viewings and paperwork. A third is anchoring the whole search around Little Japan or Clementi, when a room on a direct MRT line to your actual office or campus beats proximity to good ramen most days of the week.

## A Quick Checklist Before You Book Anything

- [ ] Confirm your pass application is submitted and get a realistic issue-date estimate from your school or employer
- [ ] Budget two months' rent in cash for deposit plus first month, on top of flights and settling-in costs
- [ ] Check what's actually included in a room's price (utilities, wifi, cleaning) before comparing two listings on rent alone
- [ ] Set up PayNow once your bank account is active, since a lot of local payments assume it
- [ ] Have your passport and a local address ready for your bank account application the week you land
- [ ] If you're on a company relocation package, confirm what the housing allowance actually covers before signing anything long-term

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I rent a room in Singapore before my Employment Pass is issued?**
No. You need the pass itself in hand to sign a standard lease or licence agreement. A short-term booking that doesn't require a long-term pass is the practical way to bridge the gap while your application processes.

**Do I need a visa to fly to Singapore from Japan for a viewing?**
No. Japanese passport holders get 90 days visa-free entry, enough time to view rooms and sort paperwork, but it doesn't count as a long-term stay and doesn't let you sign a lease.

**Is Clementi the only area with a Japanese community in Singapore?**
No. Clementi and West Coast are hubs because of the Japanese school campuses there, but Japanese residents live across many districts. The food and social hub near Cuppage Terrace, and Katong's growing expat food scene, both draw Japanese residents who don't live nearby.

**Can I open a Singapore bank account before my pass arrives?**
Some banks, DBS in particular, will start the process using an ICA in-principle approval plus an employer or school letter. Most still want the actual pass before the account is fully active.

**Does a company relocation package change what kind of lease I should sign?**
Not automatically. Confirm exactly what the housing allowance covers before committing to a long lease, and consider a short, flexible booking for your first weeks while those details get finalised.
