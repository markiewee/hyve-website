If you don't get into a hall at NUS or NTU, you're not shut out of student life, but you do need an off-campus plan on your own timeline. NUS has no blanket guarantee for anyone, freshman or senior, so a "no" is a real possibility every intake. NTU guarantees a hall place for your first two years, so a freshman miss there is usually about room type, not a room at all. From year three onward at both schools, the system runs on competition, not entitlement. Here's exactly how each one decides, what the odds actually look like, and what to do if the result doesn't go your way.

## How Does NUS Decide Who Gets a Hall Room?

NUS runs two separate systems depending on your year, and neither one is a lottery in the simple sense.

**Freshmen** are selected for hostel places through the Freshman Accommodation Exercise, based on pre-university CCA record and room availability, not a flat guarantee for every applicant. International freshmen get priority for their first year specifically, since NUS wants them settled before navigating a new country on top of a new degree. If you're not placed into your first-choice hostel, you're randomly allocated subject to what's left, not automatically housed somewhere.

**From year two**, most students go through the Residence Admission Scheme (RAS). This is nomination-based, meaning clubs, societies, and hall committees put forward members for housing rather than students applying as individuals into a general pool. If a participating group nominates more students than its allocated places, a ballot decides who gets in, and NUS caps some sub-categories (130 places is one published cap) with the remainder assigned by random ballot once each eligible group gets an even share.

There's also a pathway for returning residents, assessed on their contribution to hall life the year before, and a category for students without CCA activity who want to apply anyway. In practice, your odds at NUS from year two depend heavily on whether you're already inside a participating student group.

## How Does NTU Decide Who Gets a Hall Room?

NTU's system is structurally more forgiving for freshmen and structurally tougher for seniors than NUS's.

**Freshmen** are guaranteed a hall place for their first two years of study, based on year of admission. That's a real guarantee, not a priority ranking, though you still have to apply within the window (for AY2026/27, NTU's freshman application ran 2 to 22 June 2026, with results notified progressively from 8 July). Hall and room assignment is done by electronic ballot. You can indicate room type preferences, single or double, air-con or not, but the specific hall you land in is largely out of your hands unless you get onto a hall's own Freshmen Recommendation List through a senior contact.

**From year three**, NTU runs the Hall Admission Scheme (HAS), which sorts applicants into three tiers. Category A covers guaranteed cases (special needs, certain university programmes). Category B covers students recommended by faculty or a hall's own council. Category C is points-based, built on Campus Life Involvement (commonly called hall or CCA points) earned the previous academic year.

Points don't stack, only your highest-scoring activity counts, and they're tiered by the scale of the role: a Chair or Vice-Chair position in a Tier 1 or Tier 2 committee can be worth 7 to 9 points, lower tiers 3 to 5, and general participation or volunteering is worth 1 point. Students entering year three or four typically need somewhere around 7 to 9 points to have a realistic shot. If you spent your first two years in hall without joining any CCA or hall committee, your points balance is likely thin exactly when you need it most.

## NUS vs NTU: The Balloting Mechanics Side by Side

| | NUS | NTU |
|---|---|---|
| Freshman guarantee | None. Selected by pre-uni CCA + availability | Guaranteed for years 1 to 2, by year of admission |
| International student priority | Yes, first year only | No stated freshman priority beyond the general guarantee |
| Senior pathway | Residence Admission Scheme, nomination-based via CCA/hall groups | Hall Admission Scheme, points-based via Campus Life Involvement |
| What drives senior odds | Being an active, nominated member of a participating group | Accumulated hall/CCA points from the prior year |
| If oversubscribed | Random ballot, with caps and even-share rules in some categories | Points ranking, then ballot among ties |
| If unsuccessful | No published waitlist mechanism | Waitlist, but NTU's own guidance calls the odds low and the wait uncertain |

## So What Are the Real Odds?

Neither university publishes a clean, single success-rate number, and any source quoting you an exact percentage is guessing. The mechanics themselves tell you more.

At **NTU**, freshman odds are structurally good, since it's a guarantee tied to admission year, not a competition. The real risk window opens in year three, once the guarantee ends and you're competing on points you may not have been collecting.

At **NUS**, there's no equivalent freshman guarantee. Odds depend on your pre-university CCA record in year one, and from year two, on whether you're inside a nominating group under RAS. A strong first year spent outside CCA involvement can genuinely cost you a hall place in year two.

At both schools, the honest baseline holds: hall places are finite, demand exceeds supply most intakes, and both systems ration by rule (points, nomination, priority category) rather than by need. SMU sits at the extreme end of this pattern, with a few hundred university-managed beds against a student population in the tens of thousands, making off-campus housing the default there. ## What Actually Happens If You Miss Out

At NTU, a freshman miss is rare and usually means an undesired hall or room type, not nowhere to live, since the guarantee holds. A senior miss is different: you're placed on a waitlist NTU itself describes as low-odds and uncertain in timing, active until roughly end September for Semester 1 or end January for Semester 2.

At NUS, a miss at any year isn't automatically re-queued into anything, and there's no published fallback beyond checking whether a later round or category opens up.

Either way, the practical result is the same. You need an off-campus plan on a calendar that doesn't wait for a maybe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**If I don't get into NUS or NTU halls, is that the final answer?**
For NTU freshmen, no, since the two-year guarantee holds and any miss is about hall or room type, not a placement at all. For NUS at any year, or NTU from year three, a miss on your first application is usually final for that round, though checking for a later round is worth doing while you also start your off-campus search in parallel.

**Do international students get priority for halls?**
At NUS, yes, but only in their first year, to help with the adjustment to a new country. From year two, international students go through the same Residence Admission Scheme as everyone else. NTU's freshman guarantee applies broadly and isn't framed around nationality.

**Is there a points system at NUS like there is at NTU?**
Not in the same form. NUS's senior pathway (RAS) runs on nomination by clubs and hall groups rather than a published points ledger. NTU's Hall Admission Scheme uses an explicit tiered points system built on Campus Life Involvement from the prior year.

**Should I wait for a waitlist offer before looking elsewhere?**
No. Both universities' own guidance points the same direction: start your off-campus search immediately, rather than treating a waitlist as your plan. NTU is explicit that waitlist odds are low and timing uncertain.

**How fast can I actually move into off-campus housing if halls fall through?**
If you go the co-living route with a room that's genuinely vacant and furnished, a realistic timeline is a few days to about a week.
