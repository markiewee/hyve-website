Tanjong Pagar is Singapore's established Korean district, sometimes called "Little Korea," home to the Korean Association in Singapore, Korean marts like Emart and Lee Mart, and one of the densest clusters of Korean restaurants in the country along Tanjong Pagar Road and Amoy Street. No Lazybee house sits inside Tanjong Pagar itself, but all three, Ivory Heights, Thomson Grove, and Chiltern Park, are a manageable MRT ride away, which matters if you want to be close to campus without losing easy access to Korean food, groceries, and community.

This guide covers what's actually in Tanjong Pagar, how far it sits from NUS, NTU, and SMU, and which Lazybee house gets you there fastest depending on where you're studying.

## What Makes Tanjong Pagar Singapore's "Little Korea"

The nickname isn't marketing. As of 2023 there were just over 21,000 Koreans living in Singapore, the world's 18th-largest Korean diaspora community, and recent estimates put the number closer to 24,000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_Singapore)). A large share of that community, expatriates, business owners, and students together, has clustered around Tanjong Pagar for long enough that locals now call the area Koreatown.

71B Tanjong Pagar Road houses the Korean Association in Singapore and the Korean Chamber of Commerce, giving the area a genuine administrative anchor, not just restaurants. Korean marts including Emart and Lee Mart stock ingredients that are otherwise hard to find, and the strip along Tanjong Pagar Road and nearby Amoy Street has built up a dense run of Korean barbecue, fried chicken, and stew spots, among them Go! K-BBQ on Amoy Street, Todamgol, and Dal Bam ([Eatbook](https://eatbook.sg/korean-restaurants-tanjong-pagar/); [thinkerten.com](https://thinkerten.com/wordpress/2017/10/singapore-tanjong-pagar/)). Amoy Street Food Centre itself sits about a 4-minute walk from Tanjong Pagar MRT, so hawker food and Korean restaurants both sit within the same short stretch.

## How Far Is Tanjong Pagar From NUS, NTU, and SMU?

Tanjong Pagar itself sits right at the edge of the CBD, and general commute patterns into and around the CBD are already covered in our [guide to living by commute time to the CBD](https://lazybee.sg/blog/where-to-live-by-commute-time-to-cbd-singapore), so this section sticks to the specific distances that matter for this guide: how far Tanjong Pagar sits from each campus.

**To SMU**, Tanjong Pagar is close. The subway ride to Singapore Management University, changing at City Hall, takes around 12 minutes, and a direct SBS Transit bus from Tanjong Pagar Exit C covers the same distance in a similar time ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Tanjong-Pagar-MRT-Station/Singapore-Management-University)). Since SMU's campus sits in the city itself, this is the shortest of the three commutes by a wide margin.

**To NUS**, expect a transfer. Tanjong Pagar is on the East-West Line, and one-north MRT, the station nearest NUS's Kent Ridge campus, sits on the Circle Line, so the standard route changes trains at Buona Vista. Total travel time by subway runs around 25-30 minutes ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Tanjong-Pagar-Station/One-north-MRT-Station)).

**To NTU**, it's the longest of the three. Tanjong Pagar and Pioneer or Boon Lay, the two stations nearest campus, both sit on the East-West Line, so there's no train change, but it's a long ride across the island, followed by a shuttle bus (services 179 or 199) from either station into the middle of campus ([IAMCR](https://iamcr.org/singapore2025/getting-to-ntu)). Budget close to an hour door to door once the shuttle is factored in.

| Campus | Route from Tanjong Pagar | Time |
|---|---|---|
| SMU | Subway changing at City Hall, or a direct SBS Transit bus from Tanjong Pagar Exit C | Around 12 minutes, the shortest by a wide margin |
| NUS (Kent Ridge, via one-north MRT) | East-West Line to Buona Vista, then the Circle Line | 25 to 30 minutes |
| NTU (Pioneer or Boon Lay) | Straight along the East-West Line with no train change, then shuttle bus 179 or 199 into campus | Close to an hour door to door once the shuttle is counted |

## What Matters Beyond Commute

**Safety.** Singapore ranked second globally on the Safe Cities Index, behind only Tokyo, and first in Southeast Asia on the Global Residence Index's 2026 safety rankings, with a safety score of 0.90 ([kakapo.travel](https://kakapo.travel/blog/theme/safest-cities-world-2026); [Global Residence Index via seasiastats](https://seasiastats.com/statistic/safest-cities-in-southeast-asia-2026-7yh21)). All three Lazybee neighbourhoods, Serangoon, Jurong East, and the Lentor Hills area near Thomson Grove, are established residential estates with the same baseline of well-lit MRT walks and managed buildings that make Singapore rank this way in general.

**A room that's already set up.** Arriving from Korea with limited luggage and no local network is a different move from a local one. A furnished room with wifi and utilities already included removes a real source of first-week stress, before you've even worked out where the nearest Korean mart is.

**Verified housing before you commit.** Booking sight unseen from overseas, whether through an agent, a listing site, or a WeChat-equivalent group chat, carries the same risks anywhere: unrealistically low prices, deposit requests before a viewing, or listings copied from a real property. If you can't view in person, insist on a video walkthrough and confirm any agent's registration on the Council for Estate Agencies' website before sending anything. Our guide on [viewing a room remotely](https://lazybee.sg/blog/viewing-a-room-remotely-singapore) covers this in more depth.

## Matching Your Lease to Your Student Pass

Singapore's private rental market has one hard floor: no lease under three months, and that applies to every operator, co-living included. Past that floor, match your lease length to your Student Pass validity, not the landlord's default 12-month term, since your pass typically renews annually alongside your enrolment. Our guide on [renting on a Student Pass vs EP vs S Pass](https://lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) walks through this in detail.
