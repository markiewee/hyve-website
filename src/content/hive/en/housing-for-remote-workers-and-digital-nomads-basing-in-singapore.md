Singapore does not have a digital nomad visa. There is no equivalent to Bali's B211A, Portugal's D8, or Estonia's digital nomad visa, and no sign one is coming. If you're searching for housing as a "digital nomad in Singapore," the honest starting point isn't the room, it's your pass. Most people who end up living here long-term while working remotely fall into one of three categories, and only two of them are actually working legally while doing it. This guide walks through which is which, then covers the housing angle for the one that's genuinely common: an Employment Pass or S Pass holder with a real Singapore employer who happens to work remotely or hybrid.

## Does Singapore Have a Digital Nomad Visa?

No. Neither the Immigration and Checkpoints Authority (ICA) nor the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) issues a remote worker pass or freelancer visa. Every long-term pass Singapore does issue, Employment Pass, S Pass, Dependant's Pass, Student Pass, ties back to something concrete: a Singapore employer, a Singapore-registered course, or a family relationship to someone who holds one of those.

That's a deliberate policy choice, not an oversight. Countries that built dedicated nomad visas designed them around income earned elsewhere and spent locally. Singapore's whole pass framework runs the opposite way: work here requires a named, accountable Singapore-side sponsor.

## So How Do People Actually End Up "Working Remotely From Singapore"?

In practice, three groups get lumped under "remote workers in Singapore," and they're not the same thing legally.

**1. Employment Pass or S Pass holders with a real Singapore job.** They're sponsored by an actual Singapore employer, and their role happens to allow remote or hybrid work, either because their manager is overseas, their team is distributed, or their company just runs flexibly. This is fully legal and, by far, the most common setup behind the phrase "remote worker in Singapore."

**2. Dependant's Pass or Long-Term Visit Pass holders working under a Letter of Consent (LOC).** Spouses (and some other family members) of EP or S Pass holders can apply through MOM for an LOC, which permits work for one named Singapore employer. It is not a general permission to freelance or take remote assignments from anywhere.

**3. People entering repeatedly on tourist or social visit passes.** This group is the one most guides gloss over, and it's the one worth being blunt about.

## Can I Work Remotely for a Foreign Employer While on a Tourist Visa in Singapore?

No. A tourist or social visit pass does not permit any form of work in Singapore, and that includes remote work for a company based overseas. This is a common assumption people carry over from countries with genuine nomad visas, and it does not hold here.

Singapore law defines "work" broadly under the Employment of Foreign Manpower Act. It does not matter that your employer is in another country, that your salary lands in a foreign bank account, or that no Singapore company is involved. If you're physically in Singapore performing paid work, without a valid work pass, you're outside what your visit pass authorises.

In day-to-day practice, a laptop worker quietly answering emails from a co-living room isn't likely to get stopped on the street. But that's an enforcement gap, not a legal green light, and it's not something to build a housing or lease decision around. Two things do carry real risk: repeated short entries that read as someone living in Singapore on tourist status (which can draw scrutiny at the border, including refusal of entry or a shortened stay), and any arrangement that starts to look like ongoing local income or a fixed Singapore address tied to work. If that's your situation, the honest move is to get a read from an immigration lawyer or MOM directly before you sign anything longer than a short-stay booking.

## What About a Dependant's Pass or LTVP, Can I Work Remotely on That?

Only within the scope of the Letter of Consent, and an LOC is tied to a specific, named Singapore employer, not a blanket right to work. It's issued to cover local employment for that dependant, not freelance or remote work for a company outside Singapore.

If a DP or LTVP holder is doing genuine remote work for a foreign company with no LOC and no local employer involved, that sits in the same unclear territory as the tourist case above. It's not automatically covered just because the person holds a long-term pass for another reason (usually as a spouse or child). If this is your situation, it's worth checking directly with MOM rather than assuming a family-based pass extends to cover it. `` MOM's current LOC eligibility criteria and processing details directly with MOM before relying on specifics, since these move.

## The Setup Most "Remote-Flexible" People in Singapore Actually Have

Strip away the tourist-visa grey zone and the family-pass edge case, and what's left is the group this article is actually written for: EP or S Pass holders with a real Singapore-based job, where the role itself carries remote or hybrid flexibility.

This is common in tech, product, and some consulting and agency roles, where a company hires someone into a Singapore entity but doesn't require five days a week at a desk. It's also common for people relocating for a new Singapore role who negotiate a hybrid arrangement before they've committed to exactly where in Singapore they want to live.

That's a materially different starting point than a nomad hopping between countries every few months. The pass is real, the employer is real, and the only open question is housing, not legal status.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Singapore have a digital nomad visa?**
No. Singapore has no dedicated digital nomad or remote worker visa. Every long-term pass ties back to a Singapore employer, a Singapore-registered course, or a family relationship to someone who holds one.

**Can I legally work remotely for a foreign company while in Singapore on a tourist visa?**
No. A tourist or social visit pass does not permit any work in Singapore, including remote work for an employer based overseas. Repeated short entries used to sustain ongoing remote work can also draw immigration scrutiny.

**Can a Dependant's Pass or Long-Term Visit Pass holder work remotely?**
Only through a Letter of Consent (LOC) tied to a specific, named Singapore employer. An LOC does not cover freelance or remote work for a company outside Singapore.

**If I have an Employment Pass or S Pass, can my Singapore employer let me work remotely or hybrid?**
Yes. That's a matter between you and your employer, not an immigration restriction. Your pass is tied to that employer, not to a fixed number of office days.

**Is co-living a good option while I'm still deciding how long I'll stay in Singapore?**
Yes, particularly in the first few months of an EP or S Pass role. Shorter, flexible terms and furnished rooms let you test a neighbourhood before committing to a longer lease elsewhere.
