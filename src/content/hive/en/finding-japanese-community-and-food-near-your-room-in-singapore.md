If you're Japanese and settling into Singapore, or you just want proper ramen without a 45-minute production, the short answer is Cuppage Plaza. Tucked off Orchard Road, four minutes from Somerset MRT, it's been known as Singapore's "Little Tokyo" since the 1980s, a stack of izakayas and ramen counters that still run Japanese-only menus. Where you live changes how easily you get there on a normal weeknight, which most guides gloss over.

This piece covers what's really left of Japanese Singapore since Liang Court closed, where the food and grocery scene moved to, where community life happens through the Japanese Association, and how commute time from Lazybee's three neighbourhoods (Serangoon, Jurong East, Upper Thomson/Lentor) compares if this matters daily, not just occasionally.

## What Happened to Liang Court, Singapore's Original Little Tokyo

If you've read older guides, they probably point you to Liang Court on River Valley Road. That's out of date. Liang Court was Singapore's original Japanese-oriented mall for decades, but its tenants vacated by early 2020 and the building was demolished in 2021.

The site is now CanningHill Piers, a residential and retail redevelopment by CapitaLand and City Developments, with no meaningful Japanese retail presence. If a search result still points you there for Japanese groceries, ignore it. The businesses that anchored it scattered elsewhere, which means Japanese community life in Singapore today is more spread out than it used to be, not concentrated in one mall.

## Cuppage Plaza: Singapore's Little Tokyo

Cuppage Plaza is the closest thing Singapore has to a dedicated Japanese food district now. It's a low-rise commercial building a short walk from Orchard Central, and its upper floors are packed with small izakayas, ramen shops, and late-night bars that lean on authenticity over decor.

Rents there run lower than the malls around it, which is part of why Japanese chefs and entrepreneurs have kept opening there since the 1980s rather than in glossier locations. It's genuinely a bit hidden, the kind of place you find because someone told you about it, not because you walked past it.

It sits four minutes from Somerset MRT station on the North South Line, and it's also an easy walk from Orchard Central, where a Don Don Donki outlet gives you a grocery stop on the same trip.

## Robertson Quay's Japanese Dining Scene

A short ride from Cuppage Plaza, Robertson Quay has its own Japanese dining cluster, more polished than Cuppage Plaza's izakaya-and-ramen scene. Shunjuu, an authentic izakaya with riverside seating, has run there since 2003. Yumeya, nearby, is set up more like a Japanese teahouse than a restaurant.

If Cuppage Plaza is where you go for a quick bowl of ramen, Robertson Quay is where you'd take someone for a proper dinner. They're close enough to each other that a single evening out can cover both.

## Where to Buy Japanese Groceries

**Meidi-Ya** used to anchor Liang Court, and after that closure it consolidated into two locations: a two-storey flagship at Millenia Walk on Raffles Boulevard, with a food hall alongside the supermarket, and a smaller satellite outlet at Great World in River Valley. Both carry fresh produce, frozen goods, sauces, and a proper range of Japanese snacks and instant food.

**Don Don Donki** is the more accessible day-to-day option, with around 17 outlets across the island, including one by Orchard Central near Cuppage Plaza, plus branches at Clarke Quay Central, Suntec City, Tampines 1, Northpoint City, and elsewhere. It's not as specialised as Meidi-Ya, but it's close to wherever you already are, which matters more on a weeknight than having the single best selection.

## The Japanese Association Singapore: Community Beyond Food

The food scene is the visible part. The formal community anchor is the Japanese Association, Singapore, at 120 Adam Road, a walk from Botanic Gardens MRT station. First established in 1915 and reconstituted after the war in 1957, its current clubhouse, opened in 1998, includes a dedicated clinic running since 1985.

It runs cultural events, language classes, and general support for Japanese residents, expat or long-term. If you want something beyond restaurants, this is the place to start, and its shuttle bus to Botanic Gardens MRT makes it more reachable than the walking distance suggests. Families with school-age children may also know the Japanese School Singapore, which runs its own community touchpoints separate from JAS.

## Choosing Where to Live, Not Just Where to Eat

None of this means you need to live in the Orchard or River Valley area to stay connected. Central district rents run high, and plenty of people who eat at Cuppage Plaza regularly commute in for it rather than living on top of it. What matters more is whether the trip is actually easy enough that you'll make it on a normal week, not just when you're motivated.

Lazybee runs co-living rooms in three Singapore neighbourhoods, each with a different relationship to these Japanese food and community spots.

### Serangoon

Serangoon sits on both the North East Line and the Circle Line. Promenade station, a four-minute walk from Meidi-Ya at Millenia Walk, is also on the Circle Line, a direct ride with no transfer. Botanic Gardens, near the Japanese Association, is on the same line too. Cuppage Plaza takes one transfer, via Dhoby Ghaut onto the North South Line, then a couple of stops to Somerset.

### Jurong East

Jurong East is an interchange for the North South and East West Lines, and Somerset, the stop for Cuppage Plaza, sits on that same North South Line. A direct ride, no transfer, which is a real advantage if you want Little Tokyo to be a normal weeknight option rather than a planned outing. Reaching Meidi-Ya or the Japanese Association takes a transfer, typically via Buona Vista onto the Circle Line.

### Upper Thomson / Lentor

Both stations sit on the Thomson-East Coast Line, which runs directly to Great World, home to the Meidi-Ya satellite outlet, no transfer needed. It also passes through Orchard, a joint interchange with the North South Line, so Somerset and Cuppage Plaza are effectively one easy change away. The Japanese Association near Botanic Gardens takes more effort, since TEL doesn't serve that station.

## Making the Trip Worth It

- **Combine stops.** Cuppage Plaza and the Don Don Donki at Orchard Central sit close enough to hit on one trip, dinner and groceries together.
- **Weeknights over weekends.** Cuppage Plaza's small izakayas fill up fast on weekend nights. An early weeknight visit is the quieter version of the same meal.
- **Check JAS's calendar.** The Japanese Association runs periodic events beyond its core membership, worth a look even if you're not formally enrolled.
- **Don Don Donki covers the gaps.** With roughly 17 outlets islandwide, there's likely one closer to you on a given day, even without Meidi-Ya's full range.

## FAQ

**Is Cuppage Plaza the only place to find Japanese food in Singapore?**
No. Japanese restaurants have spread across the island as the cuisine's popularity has grown, but Cuppage Plaza, known as Little Tokyo, remains the densest cluster of authentic izakayas and ramen shops, with Robertson Quay close by for a more polished dining scene.

**Is Liang Court still Singapore's Japanese hub?**
No. Liang Court's tenants vacated in early 2020 and the building was demolished in 2021. It's now the CanningHill Piers residential and retail development, with no meaningful Japanese retail presence. Meidi-Ya, which used to anchor Liang Court, now operates from Millenia Walk and Great World instead.

**Which Lazybee neighbourhood has the most direct route to Japanese food and groceries?**
It depends what you're after. Jurong East has a direct North South Line ride to Somerset for Cuppage Plaza. Serangoon has a direct Circle Line ride to Promenade for Meidi-Ya at Millenia Walk, and to Botanic Gardens for the Japanese Association. Upper Thomson and Lentor have a direct Thomson-East Coast Line ride to Great World for the Meidi-Ya satellite outlet.

**Do I need to live near the Japanese Association to stay connected to the community?**
Not necessarily. It's reachable from most of the island by MRT and shuttle bus from Botanic Gardens station, and its event calendar is open beyond formal membership. Regular visits are more about convenience than a strict requirement.
