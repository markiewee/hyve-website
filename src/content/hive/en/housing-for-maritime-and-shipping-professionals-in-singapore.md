Most shore-based maritime and shipping professionals moving to Singapore should optimise their housing for the CBD, not for the port. That surprises people who assume a shipping job means living near the docks. In practice, the bulk of shipping line offices, ship management companies, chartering desks, marine insurers, and maritime law firms sit in or around the Central Business District, with only a smaller set of port-operations and terminal roles based out near Pasir Panjang, Tuas, or Jurong Port. This guide covers who actually needs housing here (shore-based staff, not seafarers), what work pass they hold, and where the office really is versus where you'd assume it is.

## Why Singapore Is a Major Maritime and Shipping Hub

Singapore has held the top spot on the Xinhua-Baltic International Shipping Centre Development Index for 13 consecutive years running into 2026, ahead of Shanghai in second place. That index scores port performance, maritime business services, and the broader business environment, not just cargo volume.

The Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore (MPA), a statutory board under the Ministry of Transport formed in 1996, regulates the sector and develops it as a global hub. Singapore is home to more than 200 international shipping groups, connected to over 600 ports worldwide, and its Registry of Ships ranks among the world's ten largest, with more than 4,400 vessels and 96 million gross tons under the flag.

On the port side, Singapore handled 44.66 million TEUs of container throughput in 2025 and moved 56.77 million tonnes of marine fuel, making it the world's largest bunkering port. PSA International, the operator behind most of that volume, runs terminals across more than 180 locations in 45 countries, with its own Singapore headquarters at PSA Horizons on Harbour Drive, near HarbourFront, not out at the terminals themselves.

## Shore-Based Professionals vs Seafarers: Who Actually Needs Housing Here

This distinction matters more than most guides make it out to be. A seafarer serving on board a vessel, whether Singapore-flagged or foreign-flagged, isn't a resident tenant looking for a room here. Crew live aboard, rotate on fixed contracts measured in months at sea, and fall under flag-state and international maritime labour rules rather than Singapore's residential or standard work pass framework. They don't need co-living housing the way an office-based hire does.

Shore-based maritime professionals are a different group: ship management staff (crewing, technical superintendents, marine engineers based ashore), chartering and broking desks, marine insurance and P&I club staff, maritime lawyers, classification society surveyors, port and terminal operations managers, bunker traders, and corporate shipping-line roles. These are the people this guide is for, since they live in Singapore full time, commute daily, and face the same housing decision as any other relocating professional.

## What Work Pass Shore-Based Maritime Professionals Hold

Most shore-based maritime professionals from overseas hold an Employment Pass or an S Pass, the same categories covering skilled professional and technical roles across other sectors. As of current Ministry of Manpower rules, the Employment Pass qualifying salary starts at $5,600 a month, on top of clearing the points-based COMPASS framework, with employers required to first advertise the role on MyCareersFuture. The S Pass sits at a lower salary band for mid-skilled roles. These thresholds move periodically, so check MOM's site directly for the current figure rather than relying on any article's number, including this one.

A first-time Employment Pass is valid for up to two years and renewable for up to three, comfortably covering a standard shore-based posting, unlike some project-based engineering contracts where the job itself sets the real end date. ## Where Shipping Companies Actually Have Their Offices

This is the part that trips people up before they've even landed. Container terminals, bunkering operations, and shipyards sit at the western and southwestern edges of the island, but the commercial and administrative side of shipping, the part that employs most EP and S Pass holders, is concentrated in the CBD.

Tanjong Pagar is the clearest example. It was Singapore's original working port district from the 1860s, and the old Tanjong Pagar Dock Company gave the area its maritime identity long before the container terminals moved further out. Today it sits inside the Downtown Core and Outram planning areas, at the southern edge of the CBD, and still carries a dense cluster of shipping and maritime-services offices even though the wharves themselves have relocated. Raffles Place, Robinson Road, Cecil Street, and Anson Road carry the rest of that CBD-based presence.

Port-operations roles are the exception. Pasir Panjang Terminal, PSA's main container hub, sits roughly 7km west of the old Keppel Harbour area and is scheduled to wind down by 2040 as volume shifts to the new Tuas Port, which opened its first berths in 2022 and is targeting 18 berths by 2027. Jurong Port, a separate operator handling general and multipurpose cargo for vessels up to 150,000 deadweight tonnes, sits further west again. If your job is terminal operations, marine engineering support for port equipment, or bunkering operations at one of these sites, your commute looks nothing like a CBD-based chartering or insurance role.

## Does It Matter Where You Live?

For most shore-based professionals, yes, but only in the way it applies to any CBD-based office job: living within a reasonable MRT ride of the Downtown Core, Tanjong Pagar, or Raffles Place keeps your commute short, since Singapore's rail network reaches most residential districts within 30 to 40 minutes of the centre.

It's a genuinely different calculation for the smaller group of port-operations and terminal staff. Tuas in particular sits well west of the main residential belt, is dominated by heavy industry, and has little housing built for professionals, since the area was deliberately zoned apart from residential Singapore. If your day-to-day work happens on-site at a terminal or bunkering facility, weighting your commute west matters more than the generic "live near the CBD" advice that fits most other maritime roles.

## What to Actually Look For in Housing

- **Fast access to the CBD for commercial or corporate roles.** Chartering, ship management, insurance, and legal roles are almost all based in or near Tanjong Pagar, Raffles Place, or the wider Downtown Core, so an MRT connection into that area matters more than proximity to any port.
- **An honest commute check for port-operations roles.** If your actual desk is at Pasir Panjang, Tuas, or Jurong Port, don't default to CBD housing just because that's the assumption for most maritime jobs.
- **Furnished, flexible-term housing if you're new to Singapore.** A first posting rarely comes with certainty about how long you'll stay in a given role, so a lease that doesn't lock you into a full year makes the early months easier.
- **Utilities, wifi, and basic setup bundled in.** Maritime hires often relocate on short notice, and furnishing a bare unit from scratch eats into time you don't have before you start.

## FAQ

**Do seafarers need housing in Singapore the way office-based maritime staff do?**
No. Seafarers on board a vessel live aboard and rotate on fixed sea-time contracts, governed by flag-state and international maritime labour rules rather than Singapore's residential rental market. This guide is for shore-based maritime and shipping professionals who live in Singapore full time, not crew at sea.

**What work pass do shore-based maritime professionals in Singapore usually hold?**
Most hold an Employment Pass or an S Pass, the same categories covering skilled professional and technical roles in other sectors. The Employment Pass currently requires a qualifying salary starting at $5,600 a month plus a passing score under the COMPASS framework, though thresholds change periodically, so confirm the current figure on MOM's site.

**Do shipping company offices sit near the port or in the CBD?**
Mostly the CBD. Chartering, ship management, marine insurance, maritime law, and corporate shipping roles cluster around Tanjong Pagar, Raffles Place, and the wider Downtown Core. Only port-operations and terminal-based roles, at sites like Pasir Panjang, Tuas Port, or Jurong Port, sit out near the water.

**Does it matter where I live if I work in maritime in Singapore?**
For most CBD-based roles, living within reasonable MRT reach of the Downtown Core is enough. It matters more if your actual work is on-site at a terminal or bunkering facility further west, where the commute is genuinely longer and less well served by rail.
