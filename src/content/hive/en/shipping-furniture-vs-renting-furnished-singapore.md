For most people moving to Singapore for two years or less, renting furnished works out cheaper and far less stressful than shipping furniture from home. Sea freight, storage while you wait for a lease, and the real chance that your sofa doesn't fit through the door or the lift add up fast, and Singapore's furnished rental supply is large enough that going furnished usually isn't the compromise people assume. Shipping only wins the maths when you're moving permanently, your furniture is genuinely worth more than freight plus risk, and you already know it'll fit.

That's the short answer. The rest of this is the actual working, because "it depends" isn't useful without numbers attached to it.

## What Furnished Really Means Here

In Singapore, "furnished" is not one standard. It ranges from a bed frame and a wardrobe, to a full setup with sofa, dining table, TV, washing machine, fridge, and a stocked kitchen. Co-living rooms and serviced apartments sit at the fuller end. Individual landlords listing a spare room can mean almost anything, so the word alone tells you very little.

Always ask for an itemised list or photos of what's actually in the room before you commit, not a listing that just says "furnished." A landlord's idea of furnished and yours can be two different rooms.

Co-living operators, Lazybee included, list rooms that come fully furnished, bed, storage, wifi, and shared kitchen appliances included, nothing left to source before move-in. Worth knowing before you start pricing shipping quotes against a room that might already include everything you'd have shipped.

## Shipping Costs and Furnished Supply in 2026

Sea freight is still the practical way to move furniture internationally, and pricing this year comes down to volume and whether you fill a shared or dedicated container. A shared container (LCL, where you pay only for the space your boxes take up) for a modest furniture load commonly runs from the low thousands to around ten thousand Singapore dollars, depending on origin and volume. A full 20-foot container to yourself costs more, quotes from several thousand up past fifteen thousand dollars aren't unusual before local delivery, insurance, and duty. These are general market ranges, not a quote for your specific move, so treat them as a planning baseline and get actual quotes before deciding.

Singapore Customs grants GST relief on used household articles and personal effects for people genuinely changing residence into Singapore, provided you've owned and used the items for at least three months and don't dispose of them within three months of arrival (customs.gov.sg). That removes one cost worry for a real relocation, but it doesn't cover freight itself, and it won't help if you're shipping newly bought furniture to dodge local prices.

Furnished supply hasn't shrunk either. Co-living, serviced apartments, and furnished condo units remain widely available, and roughly seven in ten tenants here rent furnished or partially furnished rather than bare. Not a niche option you settle for, closer to the default.

## Ship It, Store It, Sell It, or Rent Furnished

Four real options, trading off differently depending on how long you're staying.

| | Ship it | Store it back home | Sell it | Rent furnished |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | High (freight, insurance, customs paperwork) | Low, ongoing monthly fee | None, you gain cash | None beyond the rent premium if any |
| Best for | Permanent moves, high-value pieces | Short postings, uncertain length of stay | Cheap furniture, or moving for good | Any stay under 2 to 3 years |
| Risk | Doesn't fit the room, damage in transit, customs delay | Keeps costing you every month you're away | Can't undo it if plans change | Depends on operator quality |
| Time to move-in | Weeks, sometimes over a month | Immediate, but you arrive with nothing | Immediate | Immediate |
| Ongoing cost in Singapore | None once landed | Storage fee at home continues | None | Possibly a modest rent premium |

Storage at home is the option people default to without running the maths on it. A storage unit sized for a room's worth of furniture typically runs somewhere in the low hundreds of dollars a month, depending on city and unit size. Over a two-year posting, that alone can rival or beat what shipping would have cost, for furniture you still can't use.

## 8 Items Never Worth Shipping to Singapore

Some items lose the maths on their own, regardless of your situation.

1. **Large upholstered sofas.** Bulky to ship, expensive to insure, and Singapore's humidity is hard on fabric not chosen for it.
2. **Mattresses.** Heavy, awkward to ship cleanly, and cheap to buy new locally, which most people prefer anyway.
3. **Particleboard flat-pack furniture.** Rarely survives a sea crossing intact, and not worth the freight cost even when it does.
4. **Large area rugs.** Heavy for their value, and humidity plus dust mites make a shipped rug a maintenance headache here.
5. **Non-voltage-compatible appliances.** Singapore runs 230V. Anything built for 110V needs a permanent transformer, its own ongoing cost and fire-risk conversation.
6. **Oversized dining sets.** Local apartments and HDB flats run smaller than most Western or regional homes. A six-seat table from a bigger house often simply doesn't fit.
7. **Books and paper in bulk.** Heavy, cheap to replace or digitise, a poor use of container volume by weight.
8. **Anything you're not sure you'll keep long-term.** Real doubt about how long you're staying, or whether your taste will still suit it in two years, means it's not worth locking freight cost into it now.

## Myth: Furnished Rentals Are Always More Expensive

This one doesn't hold up once you run the full comparison. Yes, a furnished unit often lists at a premium over an identical unfurnished one, commonly a few hundred dollars a month more. Looked at as a single line item, furnished looks like the pricier choice.

But that comparison leaves out what unfurnished actually costs you to fill. Furnishing a bare home from scratch, even modestly, commonly runs into several thousand dollars, before delivery, assembly, or the furniture you'll have to sell or abandon when you leave. Renting furniture instead of buying is cheaper upfront but adds a recurring monthly line that erases much of the "savings" over anything but a long stay.

Run the actual total: furnished rent premium multiplied by your months here, versus buy-or-rent furniture cost plus disposal hassle at the end. For a stay under two to three years, furnished frequently comes out ahead once everything is counted, not just the headline rent.

## Furnishing Your First Singapore Home

First time renting here, starting from zero, the decision tree is simpler than it looks.

Under two years or unsure how long you're staying: go furnished. It removes the shipping decision entirely and skips the furniture-disposal stress at move-out.

Two to five years, with furniture you already own and love: get a real shipping quote, factor in the GST relief above, and compare it honestly against a furnished rent premium over the same period.

Permanent, or your existing furniture is genuinely high value: shipping starts to make sense, provided you've measured everything against the room it's going into, the single most common way this goes wrong.

Not sure yet: rent furnished for your first lease, get a feel for local unit sizes, and make the shipping or buying call later with real information instead of a guess from abroad.

## The Mistake That Ruins This: Shipping Furniture That Won't Fit the Room

The single most avoidable failure here is shipping a sofa, bed, or wardrobe sized for a Western or larger regional home into a Singapore room smaller than the one it came from. HDB flats and many condo units run noticeably smaller than equivalent homes in the US, UK, Australia, or parts of mainland China. A queen bed frame that fit comfortably in a previous bedroom can leave no walking space in a Singapore room half the size.

This happens because people measure the item, not the room. Get the actual room dimensions, not a general floor plan, and measure your furniture against them, including doorway and lift width for delivery. No firm room dimensions yet because you haven't signed a lease? That's a sign it's too early to lock in a shipping decision at all.

## A Ship or Sell Decision Checklist

Run through this before committing either way.

- Do you know your actual Singapore room dimensions, not just a floor plan
- Have you measured your furniture against them, including doorway and lift width
- Do you know how long you're actually staying, or is it open-ended
- Have you gotten a real freight quote, not just a general range
- Do you qualify for GST relief on used household items (three months' prior ownership and use)
- Have you priced what furnishing a bare unit would cost locally, buy or rent
- Have you compared that total against a furnished rental's premium over your expected stay
- Is anything on your shipping list from the never-ship list above

More than two or three "no" or "not sure" answers is your answer. Rent furnished for now, and revisit shipping once the picture is clearer.

## Shipping vs Furnished: The Questions Behind the Spreadsheet

**Should I ship my furniture to Singapore?**
Only if you're staying long-term or permanently, the furniture is worth more than the freight, and you've confirmed it fits the room. Shorter or uncertain stays almost always come out cheaper going furnished once shipping, storage, and disposal are counted.

**Is furnished rental really more expensive than unfurnished in Singapore?**
Not once you count the full cost. Furnished carries a rent premium, but furnishing a bare unit from scratch, buying or renting furniture, commonly costs more overall for a stay under two to three years.

**Do I get GST relief on shipped household items?**
Singapore Customs grants relief on used household articles and personal effects for genuine relocations, if you've owned and used the items for at least three months and won't dispose of them within three months of arrival. It doesn't cover newly bought items shipped to dodge local prices.

**What's the single biggest mistake people make shipping furniture here?**
Shipping items sized for a bigger home without measuring against actual Singapore room dimensions first. Local units, HDB flats especially, run smaller than many people expect.

**Is it cheaper to store furniture back home or ship it?**
Depends on posting length. Storage back home is a recurring monthly cost that adds up over a long stay, while shipping is a one-time cost carried upfront. Run both numbers against your expected time here before deciding.

**What if I'm not sure how long I'm staying?**
Rent furnished. It's the option that doesn't need you to guess right, and it keeps the shipping decision open until you actually know.
