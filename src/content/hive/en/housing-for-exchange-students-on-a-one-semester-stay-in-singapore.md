Most exchange students at NUS, NTU, and SMU cannot count on a hall room being guaranteed, since halls fill from degree students first and exchange places are shortlisted or balloted around whatever's left. A one-semester exchange runs roughly four to five months, comfortably above Singapore's three-month minimum stay for private rentals, which makes an off-campus room or a co-living room a real, legal option, not a fallback. The practical answer is to apply for a hall place anyway, because it costs nothing to try, but build a parallel off-campus plan from week one rather than waiting to see if the hall ballot comes through.

That's the short version. Here's what each university actually does with exchange housing, how the numbers work for a semester-length stay, and where to look if the hall doesn't come through.

## Why Exchange Students Get Squeezed on Hall Housing

On-campus housing at all three universities is built primarily for full-degree students, and exchange places get fitted in around that.

At NUS, roughly 30% of incoming exchange students are shortlisted for a Residential College place each semester, with priority given to filling as many exchange students into on-campus housing as possible before arrival. Most exchange students who do get in can rank their top three on-campus preferences, but oversubscribed options mean landing your first pick isn't guaranteed either.

At NTU, exchange students under the Office of Global Education and Mobility can apply for Halls of Residence, but allocation runs by electronic ballot against room availability, not first-come-first-served. General housing priority goes to final-year students and medical cases first, so an exchange applicant is competing from behind those groups.

SMU doesn't offer on-campus housing to exchange students at all. There's no dormitory on campus for anyone. SMU has arranged a limited number of beds with an external operator (CAMPUS, roughly 65 beds) open to its students, but beyond that, every SMU exchange student is finding a room in the open market from day one.

| University | On-campus option for exchange students | How places are allocated |
|---|---|---|
| NUS | Residential College place | Roughly 30% of incoming exchange students shortlisted each semester. Those who get in rank three preferences, but oversubscribed options mean the first pick is not guaranteed |
| NTU | Halls of Residence, via the Office of Global Education and Mobility | Electronic ballot against room availability. Priority goes to final-year students and medical cases first, so exchange applicants compete from behind |
| SMU | None on campus, for anyone | A limited number of beds arranged with an external operator, CAMPUS, roughly 65. Beyond that, the open market from day one |

## How Long Is a One-Semester Exchange, Really

A single semester at NUS or NTU runs from early August to early December for the fall term, or mid-January to early May for spring, which comes to about four months of term dates. SMU's terms run similarly, mid-August to late November, and mid-January to mid-April. Add a week or two on either end for arrival and settling in, or exam period and departure, and a realistic housing need lands closer to four and a half to five months.

| University | Fall term | Spring term | Realistic housing need |
|---|---|---|---|
| NUS and NTU | Early August to early December | Mid-January to early May | About 4 months of term, closer to 4.5 to 5 months with arrival and departure |
| SMU | Mid-August to late November | Mid-January to mid-April | Same, allowing for settling in and exams |

Singapore's minimum stay for a private rental is three months, whether it is an HDB flat, a condo, or a room inside either. A one-semester exchange clears that floor with room to spare. What it does not clear is the twelve-month default many individual landlords quote first, which suits their turnover rather than the law.

## What If the Semester Really Is Shorter Than Three Months

Some short summer exchange or block-teaching programmes run under three months. In that case, a private rental legally cannot be the answer, co-living included, since the three-month floor applies to every private-market operator, not just individual landlords. University-run short-stay housing or a licensed short-term stay is the only lawful route for a stay that short. If your programme is right on the edge, confirm the actual week count before assuming a private room is even an option.

## Off-Campus Options That Actually Fit a Semester

If the hall doesn't come through, or you'd rather not gamble a ballot against your only housing plan, the off-campus market has three broad lanes.

**A shared room in a private flat or HDB unit**, arranged through a property agent or directly with a landlord. This can work, but a lot of individual landlords default to offering a full year, and getting a shorter term takes asking directly and sometimes pushing back on the standard listing.

**A whole private apartment**, usually rented with other exchange or postgrad students splitting the cost. More space and more control, but also more admin: finding flatmates, splitting a deposit, and usually still negotiating a term shorter than the landlord's default year.

**A co-living room**, where the term, price, and what's included are already published rather than negotiated one listing at a time. This is the option that tends to fit a semester most cleanly, since the room is already furnished, wifi and utilities are typically bundled, and the listed term is the actual term on offer rather than a starting point for a longer conversation.

| Option | How it works | The catch |
|---|---|---|
| Shared room in a private flat or HDB unit | Through an agent or directly with a landlord | Many landlords default to a full year. A shorter term takes asking directly and sometimes pushing back |
| Whole private apartment | Split with other exchange or postgrad students | More space and control, but more admin: finding flatmates, splitting a deposit, still negotiating below the default year |
| Co-living room | Term, price and inclusions already published | Fits a semester most cleanly. Furnished, wifi and utilities usually bundled, and the listed term is the actual term on offer |

None of these is automatically the right call for every student. What matters is picking a term that matches your actual dates, not the landlord's preferred one.

## When to Start Looking

Hall ballots and shortlists typically resolve weeks before term starts, sometimes as late as a few weeks out, which leaves a narrow runway if the hall doesn't come through and you're starting your off-campus search from scratch after that. The safer approach is to start looking at off-campus options in parallel with the hall application, not after it's rejected.

## Mistakes That Cost Exchange Students Money or Time

**Waiting on the hall ballot before looking elsewhere.** By the time a rejection lands, the best off-campus rooms for your dates are often already taken by students who started looking earlier.

**Signing a twelve-month lease for a four-month stay.** It happens more than it should, usually because a landlord offers it as the default and the student doesn't think to ask for shorter. A semester doesn't need a year-long commitment behind it.

**Assuming SMU has a fallback dorm.** It doesn't. SMU exchange students who assume a hall is even an option lose real time confirming that before starting the actual search.

**Booking a room sight unseen from photos alone.** A video walkthrough or a documented viewing before paying a deposit is standard practice, not an extra step, especially when you're arranging it all from another country.

## A Quick Checklist for Exchange Student Housing

- Apply for a hall place if your university offers one to exchange students, but don't treat it as your only plan
- Confirm your actual semester dates, including arrival and exam period, before comparing any listing's term
- Rule out a private rental entirely if your programme genuinely runs under three months
- Ask directly for a lease term matching your semester if a private landlord's default listing is twelve months
- Compare a co-living room against a shared private flat on what's actually included, not just the monthly figure
- Start the off-campus search the moment your exchange place is confirmed, not after a hall rejection

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can exchange students get guaranteed housing at NUS, NTU, or SMU?**
No university guarantees it. NUS shortlists around 30% of exchange students for on-campus accommodation each semester. NTU allocates by ballot with priority to other student groups first. SMU offers no on-campus housing to exchange students at all.

**Is it legal to rent a private room for just one semester in Singapore?**
Yes, as long as the semester is three months or longer, which a standard NUS, NTU, or SMU semester comfortably is. See [is co-living legal in Singapore](/blog/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore) for the full minimum stay rule.

**What if my exchange programme is shorter than three months?**
A private rental, co-living included, cannot legally go under three months. University-run short-stay housing or a licensed short-term option is the only lawful route for a shorter stay.

**When should I start looking for off-campus housing as an exchange student?**
As soon as your exchange place is confirmed, in parallel with any hall application, not after you hear back from the ballot.

**Does SMU have any student housing at all?**
Not on campus, for anyone. SMU has arranged a small number of external beds through a third-party operator, but beyond that, all SMU students, exchange or otherwise, arrange their own housing off campus.
