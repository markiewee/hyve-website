Unlike some other communities in Singapore, Hong Kongers here don't cluster around one neighbourhood the way, say, Koreans cluster around Tanjong Pagar. Where to live instead comes down to what you're actually here for: if you're a student, it's about distance to NUS, NTU, or SMU; if you're a working professional, it's about distance to the CBD and the financial district around Raffles Place and Marina Bay. This guide covers both, plus where Lazybee's three houses fit on each commute.

## Why There's No Single "Hong Kong District" in Singapore

Singapore does have long-established, visible clusters for some nationalities, Tanjong Pagar for the Korean community, parts of Little India and Peninsula Plaza for South Asian and Myanmar communities. Hong Kong doesn't have an equivalent single postcode, and that's not a gap, it just reflects who's actually moving here.

Hong Kong professionals arriving for finance and tech roles tend to prioritise commute to the CBD over any specific ethnic cluster, since Singapore's Chinese-majority population already means Cantonese and Mandarin are widely spoken and Chinese food is everywhere, not confined to one district. Students, meanwhile, are distributed across whichever part of the island puts them closest to campus. The practical upshot: pick your area based on commute and lifestyle fit, not on chasing a neighbourhood that doesn't really exist here in the way it might elsewhere.

## If You're a Student: How Far Is Each Campus?

NUS, NTU, and SMU all admit Hong Kong applicants directly on HKDSE results, no separate Singapore entrance exam required, alongside an English test (NUS generally looks for around IELTS 6.0, NTU around IELTS 6.5 or TOEFL 90) ([study-abroad.org](https://www.study-abroad.org/blog/sg-application-guide/)). NUS currently ranks 8th globally on the QS World University Rankings, NTU 26th, both well inside the world's top 30.

Once you're enrolled, campus location becomes the real variable. NUS's Kent Ridge campus sits on the Circle Line near one-north MRT, in the island's southwest. NTU is furthest out, near Pioneer and Boon Lay MRT on the East-West Line, followed by a shuttle bus into campus. SMU sits right in the city, a short walk from Bras Basah and Bugis, which makes it the most centrally located of the three by a wide margin.

## If You're a Professional: How Far Is the CBD?

The shift of finance and technology roles from Hong Kong to Singapore has been one of the more visible workforce trends of the past few years, driven by Singapore's political stability, English-medium business environment, and comparatively lower personal tax rates ([SEA](https://singaporeemploymentagency.com/hong-kong-singapore-relocation-2026-guide/)). If you're coming over on an Employment Pass, the qualifying salary for 2026 sits at S$5,600 a month in most sectors and S$6,200 a month in financial services specifically, rising to S$6,000 and S$6,600 respectively from January 2027, with the exact figure for your age band confirmed only through MOM's Self-Assessment Tool ([various MOM-sourced guides, cross-checked Aug 2026](https://migaku.com/blog/language-fun/singapore-employment-pass-ep-and-pep-eligibility-in-2026)).

For most finance and professional-services roles, that means your day-to-day commute target is Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or the broader CBD stretching toward Tanjong Pagar. Tech roles are more spread out, with clusters around one-north (life sciences and biomedical) and the city fringe, so it's worth locking down your actual office address before picking a neighbourhood, rather than assuming "CBD" covers every job.

## Where Hong Kong Food and Culture Show Up

Even without a single enclave, there are real points of familiarity around the island. Chinatown, centred on People's Park Complex and Chinatown Complex Food Centre, has a genuine run of Hong Kong-style stalls, dim sum counters doing char siew bao and har gao, a hand-milled rice noodle roll stall run by an ex-Raffles Hotel dim sum chef, and congee variations styled the Hong Kong way ([eatbook.sg](https://eatbook.sg/chinatown-complex-food/); [DanielFoodDiary](https://danielfooddiary.com/2021/05/24/peoplesparkfoodcentre/)). Cantonese fine-dining rooms serving Hong Kong-style banquet food also show up across the city, from hotel restaurants to standalone spots.

Holland Village, meanwhile, is Singapore's longer-established international dining and social hub, more Western-leaning in its food mix but a reliable spot if you want a laid-back, café-heavy area with a mixed local-and-expat crowd rather than a purely Chinese-food-focused one ([Pacific Prime](https://www.pacificprime.com/blog/expats-guide-to-holland-village-singapore.html)). Neither of these is a place to live in the co-living sense so much as somewhere to eat and socialise regularly, which is a more realistic way to think about "Hong Kong" touchpoints here than expecting a residential cluster.

## Matching Your Lease to Your Pass

Private residential property in Singapore can't be let for under three months, a floor that applies to every operator, co-living included. Past that floor, match your lease length to your Student Pass or Employment Pass validity rather than defaulting to a standard 12-month term, since both pass types typically renew on a schedule tied to your enrolment or your employment contract. Our guide on [renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) walks through this in more detail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a Hong Kong neighbourhood in Singapore?**
Not in the concentrated sense that some other communities have. Hong Kongers in Singapore are spread across the island based on where they study or work, rather than clustered in one district. Chinatown and Holland Village are the closest things to reliable Hong Kong or Cantonese-food touchpoints.

**Where should Hong Kong professionals live if they work in finance?**
Prioritise commute to Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or the CBD. Thomson Grove's direct Thomson-East Coast Line run and Ivory Heights' East-West Line connection into Raffles Place are the two shortest options among Lazybee's houses.

**Where should Hong Kong students live if they're at NTU?**
Ivory Heights sits on the same East-West Line as NTU's nearest stations, Pioneer and Boon Lay, with no transfer required.

**Do Hong Kong students need a separate entrance exam for NUS or NTU?**
No. Both universities admit directly on HKDSE results alongside an English proficiency test, with no separate Singapore-specific entrance exam.

**How long is the minimum lease in Singapore?**
Three months, a floor that applies across private residential rentals including co-living. Past that, match your lease to your pass validity rather than a default 12-month term.
