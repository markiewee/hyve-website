If you're moving to Singapore as a research scientist for an A*STAR institute or a biotech/pharma company, your job is almost certainly based at Biopolis or Fusionopolis in one-north, and your work pass is almost certainly an Employment Pass. That combination narrows the housing question fast: you want something within easy reach of one-north, flexible enough to sign quickly on an EP, and settled enough that you're not living out of a suitcase for a role you expect to hold for years, not months.

This is a different brief from a postdoc's. If you're coming out of a PhD into a university-affiliated postdoc position, [our postdoc housing guide](/blog/housing-for-postdocs-and-early-career-researchers-in-singapore) covers that specifically. This piece is for the industry side: A*STAR research institutes and private biotech or pharma companies, where the pay, the contract structure, and the employer relationship all look different.

## Where do research scientist jobs in Singapore actually sit?

Biopolis is Singapore's biomedical research cluster, built at one-north and opened in the early 2000s. It houses A*STAR's biomedical research institutes, including groups working in genomics, bioinformatics, molecular and cell biology, and bioengineering, alongside private biotech and pharmaceutical companies that lease lab and office space in the same precinct. If your offer letter says A*STAR and mentions a life sciences or biomedical institute, Biopolis is very likely where you'll actually show up each day.

Fusionopolis sits right next to Biopolis, also within one-north. It's the tech-adjacent counterpart, hosting A*STAR institutes working in infocomm, engineering, and computing, plus companies in electronics, AI, and advanced manufacturing. Some research scientist roles, especially in materials science or computational research, land here instead of Biopolis.

Both sit inside one-north, a masterplanned research and innovation district developed by JTC in the Buona Vista/Queenstown part of Singapore, on the Circle Line. Whichever building your offer names, one-north is the anchor point for figuring out where to live. A room a 20-30 minute commute from anywhere else in Singapore isn't the same as a room a short ride from one-north.

## What work pass do research scientists actually get?

Almost all research scientist hires into A*STAR institutes or private biotech and pharma companies come in on an **Employment Pass (EP)**, sponsored by the hiring institute or company. As of 2026, the EP's minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600/month in most sectors, rising to S$6,000/month for new applications from January 2027, with a higher bar for older applicants and every application assessed under the COMPASS points framework.

Research scientist pay in biomedical and tech R&D roles generally clears the EP salary floor comfortably, particularly for anyone with a PhD and a few years of research or industry experience already behind them. That's worth naming plainly, because it changes the housing conversation: this isn't someone budgeting around a training stipend.

A Personalised Employment Pass (PEP) is a separate, higher category requiring a fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500, well above what most research scientist roles pay even at senior levels. Unless the offer is exceptional, assume EP.

## How is this different from a postdoc move?

The overlap is real. Both groups often hold EPs, both often arrive from overseas, and the individuals sometimes move between the two categories over a career. But the employer type and the career stage are genuinely different, and that changes what "the right housing" looks like.

A postdoc is typically on a university-affiliated, fixed-term academic appointment, often one to three years, sometimes renewed year to year, with pay that starts closer to entry level. A research scientist joining A*STAR or a biotech firm is usually an **industry hire**: a permanent or longer-contract role, often a mid-career move or a postdoc-to-industry transition, at a materially higher salary band. The job is less likely to end abruptly at a fixed contract date, and the person taking it is more likely to be settling in for the medium-to-long term rather than bridging to the next academic posting.

That difference matters for housing decisions. A postdoc reasonably avoids over-committing to a lease that might outlast a one-year contract. A research scientist moving into an industry role with more career stability can reasonably plan around living somewhere for a year or more without the same renewal anxiety, and is more likely to be furnishing a life, not just a room, around the move.

## What actually matters when you're looking for a place

A few things carry more weight for this specific move than for a generic Singapore relocation:

- **Distance to one-north over "central" in general.** A room near Orchard or the CBD can still mean a 40-minute commute to Biopolis or Fusionopolis at peak hours. Check the actual travel time to one-north, not just the district name.
- **A lease you can sign before you land.** Most EP holders start within weeks of accepting an offer. A room you can book and confirm remotely, without a viewing trip or a local guarantor, removes one of the biggest bottlenecks in a fast international move.
- **Furnished and utility-inclusive, especially for a solo mid-career move.** If you're moving without a partner or family, setting up an entire flat from scratch for one person is a poor use of time and money you don't need to spend twice if the role works out and you later look for something bigger.
- **Enough privacy and quiet to actually work from home occasionally.** Lab-based research scientists still write papers, prep grant applications, and take calls with collaborators in other time zones. A living situation where you have your own space to do that matters more here than it would for a short tourist-style stay.
- **Room to stay longer without renegotiating everything.** Given the more settled nature of this move compared to a postdoc contract, flexibility to extend month to month or move onto a longer lease once you're confident in the role is worth more than a rigid short-term-only setup.

## FAQ

**Do research scientists joining A*STAR need a company-sponsored work pass?**
Yes. Almost all research scientist hires into A*STAR institutes or private biotech and pharma companies come in on an Employment Pass sponsored by the hiring institute or company.

**Is housing for a research scientist different from housing for a postdoc?**
The pass type often overlaps, but the employer and career stage differ. A postdoc is usually on a university-affiliated academic contract with a shorter, renewal-dependent term. A research scientist joining A*STAR or a biotech firm is typically an industry hire, often a mid-career or postdoc-to-industry move, with more stable pay and a longer expected tenure, which supports a more settled housing choice.

**Where should I look for housing if I'm working at Biopolis or Fusionopolis?**
Both sit within one-north, on the Circle Line. Check actual commute time to one-north specifically rather than assuming any "central" location is close enough, since peak-hour travel across Singapore can add 30-40 minutes even from areas that look central on a map.

**How fast can I arrange housing before starting an A*STAR or biotech role?**
Most EP holders start within a few weeks of accepting an offer. A furnished room that can be booked and confirmed remotely, without a local guarantor or an in-person viewing, is usually the fastest way to have somewhere ready on day one.
