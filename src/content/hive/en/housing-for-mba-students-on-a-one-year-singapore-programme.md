If your Singapore MBA runs 10 to 14 months, don't sign a standard 12-month calendar-year lease. Your programme almost certainly doesn't start in January and end in December, so a lease built around a calendar year leaves you paying for weeks you don't need, or short by weeks you do. The fix is matching your lease to your actual programme dates, in a format that lets you move in and out on those exact dates. That's what this guide covers, for NUS, NTU, and briefly INSEAD, plus what to actually book.

## Why a Normal Lease Fights a One-Year MBA

Most private rentals in Singapore default to a 12-month term because that's what landlords are used to offering. It works fine for someone whose life runs on a calendar year. It works badly for an MBA student.

Singapore's full-time MBA programmes run roughly 10 to 14 months, but they start wherever the school's intake lands, usually August, not January. A standard 12-month lease signed on your August move-in date runs to the following August, well past graduation if your programme wraps in October or November. The mismatch gets worse if you add a semester abroad, an internship, or a job that starts the moment your programme ends. A fixed calendar-year lease has none of that slack built in.

## NUS MBA: The Duration Isn't Actually Fixed at 12

The NUS MBA runs a flexible duration for its full-time track, roughly 12 to 17 months depending on how many electives and how much of the optional international exposure component you take on, with the intake starting every August ([NUS MBA](https://mba.nus.edu.sg/)). Two students in the same cohort can end up with programme lengths several months apart depending on choices they make partway through, so "book housing for the MBA" isn't one fixed answer even within a single school's programme.

Locking in a lease the moment you get your offer letter, before you've settled your electives or exchange terms, is guesswork. It's worth holding off on your housing end date until your course plan for the year is closer to settled, and picking a format that can flex with it rather than a fixed term built around the shortest possible programme length.

## NTU: The Nanyang MBA and the Nanyang-Waseda Double MBA

NTU's Nanyang MBA runs a standard 12-month full-time track ([NTU Nanyang MBA](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/business/admissions/graduate-studies/nanyang-mba)). Twelve months sounds like it should line up cleanly with a normal lease, and it can, so long as your lease dates match your actual intake month rather than the calendar year. Nanyang MBA intakes don't run January to December, so a "12-month lease" only works if it starts on your move-in date, not on 1 January.

Where it gets more complicated is the double-degree options. NTU runs a Nanyang-Waseda Double MBA, earning two MBA degrees, one from Nanyang Business School and one from Waseda University, across roughly 14 months split between Singapore and Japan ([NTU Nanyang MBA Programme Overview](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/business/admissions/graduate-studies/nanyang-mba/programme-overview)). NBS also runs double-degree tracks with St Gallen and ESSEC over roughly 24 months. Any of these means your actual time physically in Singapore is shorter than the headline programme length, and it isn't evenly split, since the Singapore and overseas legs rarely land on neat calendar boundaries. Work out roughly how many months you're actually in Singapore before committing to any housing term, so you're not paying for rooms you won't use.

## The Real Fix: Match Move-In and Move-Out to Your Actual Dates, Not the Calendar

The common thread across NUS, NTU, and INSEAD is the same. None of these programmes run January to December, and none are exactly 12 months once you factor in electives, exchange terms, or a double-degree split. Treating "one-year MBA" as shorthand for "12-month calendar lease" is the mistake that costs the most money and hassle.

What works is a lease that starts on your real move-in date and ends on your real move-out date, rather than one anchored to 1 January or 1 July because that's what a standard tenancy defaults to. A room that lets you set both ends of the lease around your programme is worth more than a marginally cheaper room on a fixed term that doesn't fit.

One legal floor applies regardless of programme structure: Singapore's minimum stay for any private residential rental, condo or landed house, is three consecutive months under URA rules ([URA](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/property-and-business-owners/property/short-term-accommodation/)). A full-time MBA at 10 to 17 months clears that easily. It only matters if you're doing a single short module or an exchange leg under three months, in which case a private rental legally can't be the answer for that stretch.

## Cohort Life Changes What You Want From a Room Too

MBA programmes run hard on cohort relationships. Study groups, case competitions, networking events, and the informal stuff after class are a real part of what you're paying tuition for, not a side effect of it. That's a housing implication most students don't think about until they've moved in.

A room in a shared house, rather than a solo studio, tends to fit the rhythm of an MBA year better. You're choosing a base for a year built around meeting people and staying available for a study group that might convene at short notice, not a home to disappear into. Co-living rooms, where the common spaces and social layer come built in, line up with that better than a private condo that leaves you commuting to see anyone from your cohort.

## A Quick Checklist Before You Book

- Confirm your actual move-in and move-out dates from your offer letter or programme calendar, not an assumed 12 months
- If you're on NUS's flexible-duration track, hold off finalising your housing end date until your elective and exchange choices are closer to settled
- If you're on a double-degree track (Nanyang-Waseda, St Gallen, ESSEC), work out how many months you're actually in Singapore before committing to a lease length

- Weigh a shared, sociable setup against a solo studio given how much of the MBA year runs on cohort relationships

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do MBA programmes in Singapore run on a calendar year?**
No. NUS starts every August with a flexible 12 to 17 month duration. NTU's Nanyang MBA runs a standard 12-month track from its own intake date, not January. INSEAD splits a 10-month programme across two campuses. None of them line up with a 1 January to 31 December lease.

**How long is the NUS MBA full-time programme?**
Roughly 12 to 17 months, depending on electives and the optional international exposure component, starting every August.

**How long is the NTU Nanyang MBA?**
A standard 12-month full-time track. The Nanyang-Waseda Double MBA extends to around 14 months split between Singapore and Japan, and NBS also runs double-degree tracks with St Gallen and ESSEC over roughly 24 months.

**Can I get a lease shorter than three months for a short MBA module or exchange leg?**
Not as a private rental. Singapore's URA minimum stay is three consecutive months for any private residential property. A stretch shorter than that needs a hotel or short-term serviced apartment instead.

**Is co-living or a private condo better for an MBA year?**
For most MBA students, co-living fits better because of the shared social layer and flexible lease dates that match a non-calendar programme. A private condo can still make sense if you specifically want to live alone, but it usually means a standard 12-month term that doesn't match your actual dates as closely.
