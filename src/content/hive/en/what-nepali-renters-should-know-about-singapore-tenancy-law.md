Singapore doesn't run a separate tenancy law for Nepali renters. The rules that apply to you are the same ones that apply to any tenant here. Where Nepali nationals actually get tripped up is entry: Nepal passport holders can enter Singapore visa-free for up to 30 days ([ICA](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)), and that entry stamp alone does not let you sign a residential lease. You need an actual pass, issued and in hand, before any operator can legally let you sign.

One more thing worth flagging early, since it doesn't apply to every nationality the same way: some Nepali workers in Singapore are on Work Permits in construction, marine shipyard, or process work, sectors where your employer, not you, arranges your housing under a specific Ministry of Manpower rule. If that's your situation, the private co-living market isn't where you'll be looking. If you're on a Student Pass, Employment Pass, S Pass, or a Work Permit outside those three sectors, you're renting on the open market like anyone else, and everything below applies to you directly.

## Can I Rent in Singapore on My 30-Day Visa-Free Entry?

No. Nepal is not on Singapore's visa-required list, so you can enter without applying for a visa in advance, provided your passport has at least six months' validity and you complete an SG Arrival Card before arrival ([ICA](https://www.ica.gov.sg/enter-transit-depart/entering-singapore/visa_requirements)). That's an entry privilege, not a renting one.

To sign a lease, whether a full tenancy agreement or a co-living licence agreement, you need one of these actually issued and in hand: an Employment Pass, S Pass, Work Permit, Student Pass, Dependant's Pass, or Long-Term Visit Pass. A job offer, an In-Principle Approval (IPA) letter, or your visa-free entry stamp doesn't qualify you to sign, only the pass itself does. The full breakdown of which passes work and how a legitimate operator verifies them is in [Can Foreigners Rent in Singapore? Passes and Documents Operators Must Check](/blog/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore).

If you're arriving ahead of your pass being issued, plan on a hotel or a short-stay serviced apartment for the gap. Some operators, Lazybee included, will let you reserve a room ahead of that date once your offer letter and IPA are in, but the agreement itself only gets signed once the pass exists and can be verified against MOM or ICA's own systems.

## If You're on a Work Permit in Construction, Marine, or Process Work

This is the one place Nepali renters genuinely diverge from most other nationalities covered on this site, so it's worth being direct. Since 19 September 2023, employers hiring non-Malaysian Work Permit holders in the Construction, Marine Shipyard, and Process (CMP) sectors must get pre-entry housing approval before the worker arrives, with proof of acceptable accommodation such as a tenancy agreement or a dormitory contract ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/press-releases/2023/0911-employers-required-to-provide-proof-of-accommodation-before-their-wph-entry-into-singapore)). Dormitory housing gets approved within about a week; private residential housing takes roughly six weeks or longer due to extra checks.

Practically, that means if you're arriving on a CMP Work Permit, your employer is accountable for where you live, and you won't typically be house-hunting on the open co-living market yourself. If you later move to an Employment Pass, S Pass, or Student Pass, you become a standard renter and everything else here applies to you directly. Work Permit holders outside CMP, such as manufacturing or services, aren't covered by that specific rule, but should still confirm their housing situation with their employer before assuming they're free to rent independently.

## The Same Minimum Stay and Occupancy Rules Apply to You

Once you're eligible to rent as an individual, the underlying rules are identical for a Nepali tenant as for anyone else. Singapore's Urban Redevelopment Authority sets a minimum stay of three consecutive months for any occupant of private residential property, and HDB sets six months for flats. There's an occupancy cap too: private units of at least 90 square metres and four-room-plus HDB flats can house up to eight unrelated occupants, a relaxation that HDB and URA extended through the end of 2028 as of their January 2026 announcement.

None of that changes based on your nationality. What matters is checking it applies correctly to the specific room you're being shown, since the cap depends on the unit's actual floor area, not what a listing claims. The full explanation of both rules, including what actually counts as a breach, is in [Is Co-Living Legal in Singapore? The Rules, Explained](/blog/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore).

## Documents You'll Be Asked For, and Why That's Normal

A legitimate operator asks a Nepali renter for the same document pack it asks anyone else. Expect to be asked for:

- Your passport, valid for your intended stay
- The actual pass card (not the IPA letter), physical or digital
- Proof matching your pass: an employment letter or payslip for a work pass, an enrolment or matriculation letter for a Student Pass
- A local Singapore phone number and email
- Proof of income or funds, particularly if you don't have a local guarantor
- An emergency contact
- A passport photo

If you're on a Student Pass, many operators will also ask for a guarantor or a parent's contact, standard practice for younger or first-time renters generally, not something specific to Nepali students. The full document checklist, including the verification steps a compliant operator runs (MOM's Check a Work Pass service, ICA's e-services), is covered in the [foreigner renting guide](/blog/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore) linked above.

## What Happens With Your Security Deposit

Deposit practice doesn't differ by nationality either. One month's rent is the standard deposit for a co-living room in Singapore in 2026, refundable in full unless the landlord can show real damage beyond normal wear and tear. There's no law setting that figure, it's contractual, which is exactly why reading the deposit clause before you sign matters more than the number itself.

Some operators ask for more if your renting profile is harder to verify quickly, for example if this is your first tenancy in Singapore and you don't yet have a local guarantor. That's a judgment call individual operators make, not a rule tied to where you're from. The complete rundown of what's legal to deduct, what move-in photos protect you, and how the Small Claims Tribunal works if a deposit dispute happens is in [Security Deposits in Co-Living Singapore: What's Standard, What's Legal, How to Get It Back](/blog/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

## Practical Things Worth Sorting Early

A few things aren't strictly tenancy law but tend to matter in the first weeks after you land, and are worth handling before your document chase eats your first month:

**Bank account.** Most Singapore banks will open an account for Employment Pass, S Pass, or Student Pass holders once the pass is issued, some even before you land. A local account ready makes rent payment and deposit refunds smoother than routing everything through a Nepali account.

**Consular support if you need it.** Nepal does not maintain a resident embassy in Singapore; the Embassy of Nepal in Bangkok is the mission accredited to Singapore for major consular matters, alongside an Honorary Consulate in Singapore for routine services such as document authentication.

**Remittance and salary timing.** If part of your plan is sending money home, factor rent due dates against your salary and remittance schedule before you commit to a lease.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I sign a Singapore lease during my 30-day visa-free stay as a Nepali citizen?**
No. You need an actual work, study, or dependant pass issued and in hand, not just your entry stamp or a job offer. The visa-free period lets you be physically present to view rooms and prepare documents, not to sign a lease.

**Do Nepali renters face different deposit or minimum-stay rules than other foreign tenants in Singapore?**
No. Deposit norms (one month's rent), minimum stay (three months private, six months HDB), and the occupancy cap all apply the same way regardless of nationality.

**I'm on a Work Permit in construction. Can I rent a co-living room myself?**
If you're in Construction, Marine Shipyard, or Process work, your employer must arrange and get approval for your accommodation before you arrive, so you generally won't be renting independently. If your pass type changes later, you become a standard renter.

**Will I need a guarantor as a Nepali renter?**
Not automatically. Some operators ask for one if you're on a Student Pass, renting for the first time in Singapore, or don't yet have a local guarantor to vouch for you, the same criteria applied to any first-time foreign renter.

**Who do I contact if I have a tenancy dispute as a Nepali national in Singapore?**
Deposit and tenancy disputes go through Singapore's Small Claims Tribunal, the same path available to any tenant. Nepal's Honorary Consulate in Singapore, or the Embassy of Nepal in Bangkok, can assist with document legalisation or consular support if that becomes relevant, but neither adjudicates tenancy disputes directly.
