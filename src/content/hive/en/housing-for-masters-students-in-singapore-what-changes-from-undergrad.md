If you're moving to Singapore for a master's degree, the biggest housing difference from undergrad is this: you almost certainly won't get a guaranteed hall room, your programme is likely 12 to 18 months instead of three to four years, and you're probably arriving with a bit more money and a lot less patience for shared bathrooms down a corridor. That combination pushes most master's students toward private rentals or co-living rooms rather than campus halls, even when a hall spot is technically available to apply for.

This isn't a small adjustment. It changes how early you need to start looking, what kind of lease actually makes sense, and what you should budget for. Here's what's different, campus by campus, and what to do about it.

## Do Master's Students Get Guaranteed Housing at NUS, NTU, or SMU?

No, not in the way first-year undergrads sometimes do. At **NUS**, even undergraduate freshmen aren't automatically guaranteed a hall spot outside of the residential college programmes (Ridge View, UTown College, NUS College), which come with a genuine two-year guarantee ([NUS OSA](https://www.hosteladmission.nus.edu.sg/app/answers/detail/a_id/2654/~/are-freshmen-assured-on-campus-accommodation)). Graduate students apply through a separate process via NUS Graduate School, mainly for Prince George's Park Residences, with subsidised rates for students on MOE-subsidised graduate programmes and non-subsidised rates for self-funding programmes ([NUS Graduate School](https://nusgs.nus.edu.sg/accomodations/)). There's no guarantee built into that process.

At **NTU**, Graduate Halls 1 and 2 exist specifically for postgrad students, but priority goes to incoming students on research programmes (PhDs, mostly) who haven't lived in Singapore before ([NTU Graduate Housing](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/life-at-ntu/accommodation/graduate-housing)). If you're on a one-year taught master's rather than a research track, you're further down the queue, competing for whatever's left.

At **SMU**, there's no traditional dorm system at all for postgrads. The university points students toward a mix of options: Prinsep Street Residences (open to both undergrad and postgrad), Campus by the Assembly Place (open to undergrads, PhDs, and master's students), Keystone Clarke Quay, and a smaller block of leased beds at CAMPUS ([SMU Graduate Studies](https://graduatestudies.smu.edu.sg/accommodation-psr)). These are third-party operators SMU has arrangements with, not university-run halls, and rates aren't subsidised the way undergrad hall fees can be.

The upshot: budget on the assumption you're finding your own place, and treat any hall offer as a bonus rather than a plan.

## Your Programme Is Shorter, So Your Lease Needs to Match

Most taught master's programmes in Singapore run 12 to 18 months, sometimes even less for intensive coursework tracks, compared to three or four years for a bachelor's degree ([NTU Graduate Admissions](https://www.ntu.edu.sg/admissions/graduate/coursework)). That changes the maths on a lease completely.

A four-year undergrad has room to sign a one-year lease, get a feel for the market, and negotiate a better renewal. A master's student doesn't. If your programme is 12 months, a standard one-year tenancy on a private condo means your lease and your degree end at almost the exact same time, with zero slack for a delayed graduation, a thesis extension, or a job search that runs a few weeks past your original end date.

This is the single biggest reason master's students lean toward co-living over a full private rental. A room in a co-living setup, like the ones we run at [Lazybee](https://lazybee.sg), typically comes with more flexible lease lengths than a standard 12-month private tenancy, room-by-room rather than whole-unit, and everything (furniture, wifi, utilities, a cleaner) already sorted. That matters more when you've got 14 months to get settled and get out, not four years to slowly build a life around a fixed address.

One legal constraint applies regardless of programme length: under URA rules, the minimum stay for any private residential property in Singapore, condo or landed, is three consecutive months. This was revised down from six months in 2017 and hasn't changed since ([URA](https://www.ura.gov.sg/guidelines/property-and-business-owners/property/short-term-accommodation/)). Anything shorter than that on a private property is illegal, whoever is offering it. If your programme runs under three months (rare, but exchange-adjacent short courses exist), you're looking at hotels or serviced apartments instead, not a private rental or most co-living rooms.

## Budget: Master's Students Usually Have More to Work With, and Different Priorities

Master's cohorts skew older than undergrad ones. Plenty of master's students in Singapore are in their mid-20s, several years into a first job before coming back for a degree, sometimes on a company sponsorship or scholarship that covers part of the cost. That's a genuinely different financial position from an 18-year-old freshman starting out on a student loan and a monthly allowance from home.

In practice, that shows up in what people actually prioritise on a room search:

- **Undergrads** more often optimise hard for price first, then commute to campus, and are willing to trade privacy (shared rooms, more housemates) to hit a lower number.
- **Master's students** more often have room in the budget to prioritise a private room over a shared one, a shorter commute to the CBD as well as campus (since a chunk of master's cohorts work part-time or network actively during the programme), and move-in speed over squeezing out the cheapest possible rate.

None of that is a rule. Plenty of master's students are on a tight scholarship stipend and every dollar counts, in which case our guide on [housing for PhD students on a stipend budget](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/housing-for-phd-students-in-singapore-on-a-stipend-budget) covers a lot of the same budgeting logic, even though it's written for the PhD case. But if you're coming in with savings from a few years of work, it's worth being deliberate about what you're actually paying for rather than defaulting to the cheapest room on the market out of undergrad habit.

## What Actually Changes: A Quick Comparison

| | Undergrad | Master's |
|---|---|---|
| Programme length | 3 to 4 years | 12 to 18 months typically |
| Hall guarantee | Sometimes, first year only, or via residential colleges | Essentially none |
| Lease strategy | Can afford a standard 1-year lease and renew | Needs a lease that matches (or beats) programme length |
| Typical age | 18 to 22 | Often mid-20s, sometimes older |
| Budget flexibility | Usually tighter, allowance-based | Often more flexible, sometimes employer-sponsored |
| Priority when room-hunting | Price, then campus commute | Private room, flexibility, move-in speed |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do NUS, NTU, and SMU guarantee housing for master's students?**
No. NUS and NTU run graduate hall applications with limited spots and no guarantee (NTU gives priority to research-track students who haven't lived in Singapore before). SMU has no traditional postgrad dorms and directs students to a set of third-party operators instead.

**How long is a typical master's programme in Singapore, and how does that affect housing?**
Most taught master's programmes run 12 to 18 months. That's short enough that a standard one-year private lease can end up misaligned with your actual programme dates, which is why many master's students look at co-living rooms with more flexible lease lengths instead.

**What's the minimum legal rental period in Singapore?**
Three consecutive months for any private residential property, condo or landed house, under URA rules revised in 2017. Anything shorter isn't a legal private tenancy.

**Is a private room worth the extra cost for a master's student?**
For many, yes, especially with a shorter programme where the trade-off is between saving a bit more per month versus having your own space and less disruption while managing a heavier academic and often work-adjacent schedule. It comes down to your specific budget, not a universal answer.
