If you're a teacher moving to Singapore for a role at an international school, your housing search should start with one question: which campus am I actually posted to? Singapore has more than 30 international schools spread right across the island, from Woodlands in the north to Tampines in the east to Jurong West in the west, so "live near the international schools" isn't a real strategy. You'll almost certainly be on an Employment Pass, some schools help with housing and some don't, and because most international schools run an August school year, the lease market gets tight every June and July. This guide covers all three, so you're not figuring it out after you land.

## Where are Singapore's international schools, and why does it matter?

Unlike a single university with one campus, Singapore's international schools are scattered across the whole island, each drawing its own catchment of expat families. That means the "best area to live" question doesn't have one answer. It depends entirely on where your school is.

Some schools also run more than one campus and shift them over time, so always confirm the current address with the school directly rather than an old blog post or forum thread. A few schools have consolidated campuses in recent years, which is exactly the kind of detail that goes stale fast.

The practical takeaway: figure out your actual campus first, then look at housing within a reasonable commute of that one address. Don't default to a generic "expat area" like Holland Village or Tanjong Katong just because it sounds like where teachers live. Plenty of teachers do live there, plenty don't, depending entirely on which school signed them.

## What visa do international school teachers need?

Almost all foreign teachers hired directly by a Singapore international school come in on an Employment Pass (EP), the standard MOM pass for foreign professionals. It's not usually a Personalised Employment Pass or a work permit category, just the regular EP that any professional hire would get.

The EP has a minimum qualifying salary that scales with age under MOM's COMPASS framework, starting lower for candidates in their early 20s and rising for older, more experienced hires. The exact dollar thresholds get revised periodically, so check the current figures on MOM's website directly before you rely on a number from any third-party site, including this one. What matters for your housing search is simpler: once your EP is approved, you can sign a lease or a co-living licence agreement in your own name, the same as any other EP holder. For the general mechanics of renting on an EP, including deposits and what landlords typically ask for, see our [guide to renting on an EP or Student's Pass](/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore).

## Does your school pay for housing?

Sometimes, not always. Some international schools build a housing allowance or a relocation stipend into the package for overseas hires, especially at the more established schools competing hardest for experienced teachers. Others offer nothing beyond the salary, and leave housing entirely up to you. A number sit somewhere in between, covering part of the cost or offering it only for the first year.

Don't assume either way. If you're mid-negotiation on an offer, ask HR directly and in writing exactly what's covered: a fixed monthly allowance, a lump sum, temporary housing while you search, or nothing. That answer changes your whole budget, and it's a much easier conversation to have before you sign than after. If there's no allowance, or it doesn't stretch far, a room in a co-living house with all-in rent is usually the more predictable option than a bare rental where you're also paying a deposit, agent's fee, and furnishing costs out of pocket.

## Why lease timing around July and August matters

Most international schools in Singapore follow the Northern Hemisphere academic calendar, with the new school year starting in late July or August. That means a large share of new teacher hires land in Singapore within the same few-week window every year, all looking for housing at once.

Two effects follow from that. First, good rooms and units near the more sought-after schools get booked out fastest in June and July, so starting your search two to three months ahead of your start date gives you far more choice than starting two weeks out. Second, if your contract confirms in, say, April or May for an August start, it's worth locking in housing well before you land rather than waiting to view in person, since remote viewing and video walkthroughs are standard practice for relocating tenants. If a school does offer temporary housing for your first week or two, that buffer is useful exactly because it takes the pressure off finding a permanent room the moment you arrive.

Not every international school in Singapore runs an August start. A handful follow a January-to-December or other calendar, so confirm your own school's academic year before assuming the July/August rush applies to you.

## Whole-unit rental or a co-living room: what fits a teacher's situation?

If you're relocating with a partner and kids, a whole HDB or condo unit gives you the space and stability a family needs, but it usually means a 12-month lease, one to two months' deposit, and either bringing your own furniture or paying to furnish a bare unit. That's a lot to arrange from overseas before your first day at school.

If you're moving on your own, or your family is following a few months later once the school term and your own housing are settled, a private ensuite room in a co-living house solves a different problem: you get your own space and a locked door from day one, rent is typically all-in on utilities and wifi so there's no setup admin, and the minimum stay is shorter than a standard tenancy. That matters if you're not yet sure whether this posting turns into a multi-year stay or a single contract.

It's also a genuinely useful bridge option even for teachers eventually planning a family move: a room gets you settled and working while you house-hunt properly for something bigger, without needing to sign a year-long lease sight unseen from another country.

## FAQ

**Do international school teachers in Singapore need a work visa?**
Yes. Foreign teachers hired directly by a Singapore international school are almost always sponsored on an Employment Pass, the standard MOM pass for foreign professionals. The school's HR or admin team typically handles the application once your contract is signed.

**Will my school pay for my housing in Singapore?**
It depends on the school. Some offer a housing allowance or relocation stipend, others don't, and some cover only part of the cost or only the first year. Confirm this in writing during offer negotiation rather than assuming.

**When should I start looking for housing if I'm starting a teaching job in August?**
Ideally two to three months before your start date. Most international schools run an August school year, so June and July are the busiest months for teacher relocations, and the best rooms and units near sought-after schools get taken first.

**Should I live near one specific school if I don't know my exact posting yet?**
No, wait until your campus is confirmed. Singapore's international schools are spread across the whole island, so a home near one school could be a long commute from another. Confirm the exact campus address first, then house-hunt around it.

**Is co-living a realistic option for a teacher moving alone?**
Yes. A private ensuite room with all-in rent removes most of the setup admin of a bare rental, and the shorter minimum stay suits a first-year contract better than a 12-month lease signed sight unseen from overseas.
