A postdoc in Singapore usually earns somewhere between S$5,000 and S$9,500 a month, depending on the institution, the field, and how many years out from the PhD you are. That's a real step up from a PhD stipend, and it changes what kind of housing actually makes sense. You're no longer stretching a training grant to cover rent. You're an early-career professional on a fixed-term contract, often new to Singapore, often on an Employment Pass, and trying to find a place near your lab without signing a two-year lease for a one- or two-year appointment.

This piece is about that specific gap. Not the PhD student piece, not the general "moving to Singapore for work" piece. Postdocs and early-career research fellows have a distinct set of constraints: better income than a grad student, but a contract that's still short and renewal-dependent, plus a work pass that's tied to the appointment.

## How much do postdocs in Singapore actually earn?

Pay varies a fair amount by institution and seniority, but a few real numbers give a sense of the range:

- **A*STAR-affiliated postdocs**: entry-level (0-2 years post-PhD) around S$5,000-6,200/month gross, rising to roughly S$6,200-7,500/month at 2-5 years, and S$7,500-9,500+/month for senior or fellowship-holding postdocs.
- **NUS**: general postdoctoral fellow pay averages around S$7,000/month, though this varies sharply by programme. The Asia Research Institute's fellowship pays a flat S$6,000/month inclusive of housing and living costs, while named fellowships like the Lee Kuan Yew Postdoctoral Fellowship run from roughly S$78,000 to S$204,000 a year depending on track and seniority.
- **NTU**: general postdoctoral research fellow pay clusters around S$58,000-73,000 a year (roughly S$4,800-6,000/month), with the Presidential Postdoctoral Fellowship paying S$80,000/year base plus a separate S$18,000/year housing grant, and Wallenberg Foundation fellows starting at S$70,000/year base plus a cost-of-living allowance.

The spread is wide because "postdoc" covers everyone from a fresh PhD in their first year of a standard research fellow contract to a named fellow on a prestigious, better-funded track. What's consistent is that even the lower end clears a PhD stipend by a comfortable margin, and the upper end starts to look like a proper early-career professional salary.

## What work pass do postdocs actually hold?

Most postdocs in Singapore are on an **Employment Pass (EP)**, sponsored by the university or research institute that hired them. The EP's minimum qualifying salary is S$5,600/month in most sectors as of 2026 (rising to S$6,000/month for new applications from January 2027), with the bar increasing for older applicants and every application also assessed under the COMPASS points framework.

A **Personalised Employment Pass (PEP)** is a different thing and doesn't apply to most postdocs. It requires a fixed monthly salary of at least S$22,500 to qualify, which is well above what almost any postdoc appointment pays. If you've seen PEP mentioned alongside postdoc housing elsewhere, that's usually a mix-up. Unless you're on a genuinely exceptional, senior fellowship, assume EP.

The practical housing implication: an EP is tied to your current employer and appointment. If your contract isn't renewed or you switch institutions, your pass status changes with it. That's a real reason to avoid a housing commitment that outlasts the certainty of your contract.

## Why a postdoc's housing problem is different from a PhD student's

A few things separate postdoc housing needs from a PhD student's:

**The budget is real, but the contract still isn't long.** Most postdoc appointments run one to three years, sometimes renewed year to year. You can afford more than hall or a shared HDB room split five ways, but you still don't want to sign a standard two-year tenancy when you don't know if you'll be here past year one.

**You're not on campus housing anymore, usually.** PhD students often stay in university halls or graduate residences for at least part of their candidature. Postdoc positions rarely come with that option, or if they do, availability is limited and eligibility rules are stricter. You're renting on the open market from day one.

**You need to move fast, often from overseas.** Many postdocs are hired from a lab in another country and start within weeks of the offer. There's no time to fly in, view ten units, negotiate a lease, and furnish an empty room before day one at the lab. Somewhere move-in ready with a short lead time actually matters here.

**Guarantors and local credit history are a real barrier.** Standard tenancy agreements in Singapore often expect a guarantor or a longer track record, which a new arrival on a fresh EP doesn't have. Co-living operators typically don't require either.

## What to actually look for

Given the constraints above, a few things matter more than they would for a longer-term local hire:

- **Lease length that matches contract length.** A room that can be taken for six to twelve months without penalty fits a postdoc contract far better than a standard two-year tenancy with an early-termination clause.
- **Proximity to the actual research node, not just "central."** A*STAR's Biopolis and Fusionopolis clusters sit in one-north. NUS's science and engineering faculties sit around Kent Ridge. NTU's main campus is out in the west, near Jurong. "Near the CBD" isn't the same as near your bench.
- **Furnished, with utilities and WiFi bundled.** You're not buying furniture for a one-year posting. A room with a bed, desk, aircon, and internet already sorted removes a week of setup most postdocs don't have time for.
- **A quiet-enough room to actually work in.** Research schedules aren't always 9-to-5. Papers get written at odd hours before submission deadlines. A living situation where you're not fighting for desk space or dealing with noise matters more than it sounds.

## FAQ

**Do postdocs in Singapore need a work pass sponsored by their university?**
Yes. Almost all postdocs are on an Employment Pass sponsored by the hiring university or research institute (NUS, NTU, A*STAR, or an affiliated research institute). The pass is tied to that specific employment.

**Can a postdoc qualify for a Personalised Employment Pass instead of an EP?**
Only if the fixed monthly salary is at least S$22,500, which is well above typical postdoc pay even at the senior end. Almost all postdocs are on a standard EP, not a PEP.

**Is postdoc pay in Singapore enough to live comfortably?**
At S$5,000-9,500+/month, postdoc pay clears the Employment Pass minimum salary comfortably at most levels and supports renting a private room without roommates split five or six ways, which is common on a PhD stipend. It's a materially different budget from graduate student housing.

**How far in advance should a postdoc arrange housing before starting?**
Given EP processing timelines and the short runway most people have between accepting an offer and starting, arranging a flexible, furnished room that doesn't require a long lease or a guarantor is usually more practical than trying to negotiate a standard tenancy from overseas.
