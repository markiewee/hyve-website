If your internship runs 3 to 6 months, your housing search sits on a knife edge you may not know about yet. Singapore's private rental market has a hard floor of a 3-month minimum stay, so a standard summer internship of exactly 12 weeks leaves almost no slack between your move-in date and your flight home. The right options are co-living rooms and serviced apartments built for this exact length of stay, not a standard 12-month condo lease and not a hotel. Get your pass sorted, then book to your exact dates, and the rest is manageable.

That's the short version. The details below cover which pass you'll actually be on, why the 3-month threshold matters more than most guides admit, and how to pick housing that won't leave you stuck mid-internship.

## Which pass will you actually be on?

Most interns in Singapore land on one of two passes, and they work very differently.

The **Training Employment Pass (TEP)** is the standard route if a Singapore-registered company is sponsoring your placement. It's built for exactly this: professional, managerial, or specialist training, capped at 3 months, and it does not renew. The Ministry of Manpower requires a fixed monthly salary of at least S$3,000, or acceptance as a student at a recognised institution. Your employer applies on your behalf through the EP Online portal, and processing typically takes around three weeks ([Sleek](https://sleek.com/sg/resources/internship-and-student-visas-in-singapore/), [Singapore Employment Agency](https://singaporeemploymentagency.com/training-employment-pass-singapore-2026/)).

The **Work Holiday Pass** covers a different crowd: undergraduates and recent graduates aged 18 to 25 from a university in Australia, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Switzerland, the UK, or the US. It doesn't need an employer to sponsor you, and it's valid for up to 6 months, which lines up neatly with a longer placement ([Singapore Global Network](https://singaporeglobalnetwork.gov.sg/work-holiday-sg/about-whp/), [MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-holiday-programme)). It caps at 2,000 pass holders at any one time, so it's worth applying early if you're eligible.

|---|---|---|
| Who it is for | A Singapore-registered company sponsoring your placement, for professional, managerial or specialist training | Undergraduates and recent graduates aged 18 to 25 from a university in Australia, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Switzerland, the UK or the US |
| Employer sponsorship | Required. The employer applies through EP Online | Not required |

## Why the 3-month minimum stay is the number that decides everything

Singapore requires every occupant of a private residential property, room or whole unit, to stay a minimum of 3 consecutive months. HDB flats run a longer minimum of 6 months. This isn't a co-living-specific rule. It's the baseline rental law that any landlord or operator has to work within, and it applies whether you're renting a whole condo or a single room in a shared apartment. We've written the full breakdown of what counts as a breach and who's actually liable in [Is Co-Living Legal in Singapore?](/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore)

Here's where it bites interns specifically. A 12-week internship is functionally 3 months. If your start date and end date don't line up exactly with a 3-month tenancy, you're either paying for days you don't need or scrambling to extend at the last minute. There's no legal way to book housing for 10 weeks and call it done. You're renting for a minimum of 3 months regardless of how long you're actually working, so the placement that runs a few days short of 3 months still needs a full 3-month booking, and a 4 to 6 month placement has more breathing room but still needs its exact dates locked down.

The practical move is to work backwards from your actual last working day, add any handover or travel buffer you want, and book housing to that exact window rather than a rounded "about 3 months" guess. If your internship might extend, ask about that possibility with your employer before you're two weeks from your original end date, since a mid-lease extension is a very different conversation than a fresh booking.

## Serviced apartment, co-living room, or a direct short lease

Three real options exist for a stay this length, and they suit different budgets and expectations.

**Serviced apartments** (Ascott, Fraser Suites, Somerset, Oakwood and similar operators) are fully furnished with weekly housekeeping and flexible short stays, some from as little as 7 nights. They're the most hotel-like option and the most expensive per month for a 3 to 6 month stretch, which makes them a better fit for a company-funded relocation package than a self-funded internship budget.

**Co-living rooms** are furnished, bills-included, and increasingly built around exactly this length of stay. A well-run operator prices for 3-month-plus stays specifically, which means you're not subsidising someone else's flexible-daily-rate model. You also get a built-in social layer, other tenants in the same unit, which matters when you're new to the city for a defined stretch and don't have time to build a network from zero.

**Direct short leases** from individual landlords exist too, but they're less predictable. Some landlords will do 3 to 6 months at a premium on the monthly rate, but you're negotiating terms, deposit, and furnishing one-off with someone who may prefer a longer-term tenant. It can work, but expect more back-and-forth and less standardisation than a serviced or co-living option ([Moving Managed](https://movingmanagedsg.com/blog-short-term-housing-singapore-2026.html), [Cove](https://cove.sg/search/short-term-rental-singapore)).

## What to check before you book

- **Confirm the exact minimum stay against your placement dates.** Don't book "3 months" if your internship is 3 months and 4 days. Round in your favour, not the operator's.
- **Ask what happens if your internship ends early or extends.** Placements do get cut short or stretched, and the terms for either scenario should be in writing before you sign, not negotiated after the fact. Our guide on [early termination in co-living leases](/early-termination-coliving-lease-singapore) covers how notice periods and deposits actually work if your dates change.
- **Check what's included.** Utilities, wifi, and basic furnishing should be bundled into a co-living or serviced apartment rate. A direct rental usually means you're setting these up yourself, which eats time you don't have in a short placement.
- **Verify the location against your actual commute**, not just the district name. A short internship doesn't leave room for a long commute to eat into your evenings and weekends.
- **Get the deposit and refund terms in writing.** For a stay this short, a slow deposit return can genuinely delay your final paycheque-adjacent admin before you leave the country.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I rent for exactly 3 months if my internship is exactly 12 weeks?**
Only if 12 weeks meets or exceeds the legal minimum of 3 consecutive months. Check your actual start and end dates against the calendar rather than assuming "12 weeks" and "3 months" are interchangeable, since they're not always the same number of days.

**Do I need a Training Employment Pass or a Work Holiday Pass?**
It depends on whether a company is sponsoring you. The Training Employment Pass needs an employer to apply on your behalf and caps at 3 months with no renewal. The Work Holiday Pass doesn't need employer sponsorship, runs up to 6 months, and is limited to eligible nationalities aged 18 to 25.

**What if my internship gets extended past my lease?**
This needs to be handled with your housing provider directly, ideally before your original end date rather than after. Ask what an extension actually looks like in practice, whether it's a straightforward extension of the same room or a fresh booking, before you assume it's automatic.

**Is co-living cheaper than a serviced apartment for a short stay?**
Generally yes, since serviced apartments price for hotel-adjacent flexibility and co-living operators price around the 3-month-plus stay directly. Compare bills-included rates rather than headline rent alone, since that's where the real difference usually shows up.

**Do I need a guarantor as an intern with no local credit history?**
Requirements vary by operator. Co-living providers built around international and short-stay tenants typically don't require a local guarantor the way a traditional landlord letting a whole unit might, but confirm this directly before you commit.
