Singapore doesn't have a single Japanese district the way it has a Chinatown or a Little India. Japanese residents, more than 32,000 of them as of October 2024 ([Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, via Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1080637/japan-number-japanese-residents-singapore/)), are spread across a handful of pockets instead: River Valley and Robertson Quay for restaurants and riverside living, Orchard's Cuppage Plaza for a genuine after-hours Japanese social scene, and Clementi and West Coast for families near the Japanese school campuses. None of Lazybee's three houses sits inside any of these pockets, but all three are a workable MRT ride from at least one of them, which matters more than living inside the enclave itself for most students and young professionals.

This guide covers where these pockets actually are, what's in each one, and how far Chiltern Park, Ivory Heights, and Thomson Grove sit from them.

## Where Japanese Residents in Singapore Actually Cluster

There was a historical "Little Japan" once, a cluster of Japanese shops, schools, and a Shinto shrine around Middle Road, in what's now part of Bugis, dating back to the early 1900s. That neighbourhood doesn't exist in that form anymore. Today's Japanese community in Singapore is smaller as a share of the population, around 0.5 percent of Singapore's 6.11 million residents, and settled in more spread-out, function-specific pockets rather than one residential enclave.

Three pockets matter most in practice. **River Valley and Robertson Quay** carry the density of Japanese restaurants and a riverside, expat-heavy residential feel. **Cuppage Plaza**, tucked just off Orchard Road near Somerset MRT, is where a lot of the actual social and business community life happens after hours. **Clementi and West Coast** are where Japanese families tend to settle, largely because that's where the Japanese School Singapore's campuses are.

None of this means Japanese residents live exclusively in these three areas. Plenty live wherever their job or budget puts them, same as any other nationality in Singapore. But if community, familiar food, or proximity to a Japanese school is part of your decision, these are the three places to know.

## What's Actually in Each Pocket

**River Valley and Robertson Quay** hold one of Singapore's denser clusters of Japanese restaurants, including Ikoi, Nagae, Sugisawa, Sushi Kou, and Himonoya, alongside the riverside bar and cafe scene that makes the area popular with expats generally, not just Japanese ones. Meidi-Ya, a long-running Japanese supermarket chain, stocks Japanese groceries hard to find at a regular NTUC, with outlets at Millenia Walk and Great World (Liang Court, its old River Valley home, was demolished in 2021 and redeveloped into CanningHill Piers).

**Cuppage Plaza**, off Orchard Road a short walk from Somerset MRT, functions differently. It's known among Japanese expats as the closest thing Singapore has to a Tokyo yokocho, a cluster of small, late-opening izakayas and Japanese-only-menu spots like Kazu Sumiyaki and Izakaya Nijumaru, popular with Japanese businessmen after work. Isetan Scotts, a Japanese department store with its own supermarket floor, sits nearby at Shaw House on Orchard Road, and the Japan Creative Centre, a cultural centre run jointly by Japan and Singapore, is a short distance further at 4 Nassim Road.

**Clementi and West Coast** are the family end of the spread. The Japanese School Singapore runs its elementary campus on Clementi Road and its junior high campus on West Coast Road, with a second elementary campus out in Changi. The Japanese Association Singapore, the community's main formal body, is based at 120 Adam Road. Families who want their kids close to a Japanese-curriculum school tend to gravitate toward this side of the island.

## How Far Lazybee's Houses Sit From Each Pocket

Lazybee runs three co-living houses across Singapore, and each one connects to these pockets differently.

**Chiltern Park**, in Serangoon, is a direct North East Line ride to Dhoby Ghaut in around 12 minutes, then one stop to Somerset and Cuppage Plaza, for a total of roughly 15 to 20 minutes door to platform ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Serangoon-MRT-Station/Somerset-MRT-Station)). Of the three houses, this is the fastest route into the Orchard and Cuppage pocket.

**Thomson Grove**, near Lentor MRT on the Thomson-East Coast Line, is a direct 13-minute ride to Orchard MRT with no transfer, since the TEL runs straight through ([Land Transport Guru](https://landtransportguru.net/somerset-station/); TEL station data). From Orchard it's a short walk across to Somerset and Cuppage Plaza, and the same line continues on toward the river, putting Robertson Quay within reasonable reach too.

**Ivory Heights**, in Jurong East, sits furthest from these pockets. Jurong East and Somerset are both on the North-South Line, but the ride between them runs the length of the line, around 35 to 38 minutes ([Rome2Rio](https://www.rome2rio.com/s/Jurong-East-MRT-Station/Somerset-MRT-Station)). Ivory Heights makes more sense if your priority is the west side of the island generally, including proximity to NUS, rather than the Orchard or River Valley pockets specifically.

## What Matters Beyond Which Pocket You're Near

**Safety.** Singapore ranked second globally on the Safe Cities Index, behind only Tokyo, and Japanese residents moving here are typically already used to that baseline, not stepping down from it. All three Lazybee neighbourhoods, Serangoon, the Lentor Hills area near Thomson Grove, and Jurong East, are established residential estates with well-lit MRT access and managed buildings.

**A room that's already set up.** Arriving from Japan with limited luggage and no local network is a different move from arriving with a full household in tow. A furnished room with wifi and utilities already bundled removes a real source of first-week friction, before you've worked out where the nearest Meidi-Ya or Isetan is.

**Matching your lease to your pass.** Singapore's private rental market has a hard floor of three months minimum, no exceptions, co-living included. Whether you're here on a Student Pass, an Employment Pass, or an S Pass, match your lease length to your pass validity rather than the landlord's default term. ## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a Japanese neighbourhood in Singapore?**
Not in the Chinatown or Little India sense. A historical "Little Japan" existed around Middle Road, now part of Bugis, in the early 1900s, but today's roughly 32,000 Japanese residents are spread across function-specific pockets: River Valley and Robertson Quay for restaurants and riverside living, Cuppage Plaza off Orchard Road for the social and business community, and Clementi and West Coast for families near the Japanese School Singapore's campuses.

**Where do most Japanese expats in Singapore live?**
There's no single answer, since housing choice tracks job location and budget as much as community. River Valley, Orchard, and the Clementi and West Coast area near the Japanese school campuses are the three areas most associated with Japanese residents specifically, but plenty live elsewhere on the island entirely.

**Which Lazybee house is closest to Orchard and Cuppage Plaza?**
