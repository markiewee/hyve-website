If you're at SMU's Yong Pung How School of Law, you're already in the city. Your campus sits on Armenian Street, a short walk from City Hall and a few MRT stops from Raffles Place, so CBD proximity is baked into your degree. If you're at NUS Law, the calculation just changed: the faculty moved from its long-time Bukit Timah campus to Kent Ridge in 2026, which puts you further from the CBD than NUS Law students have been in almost twenty years, right as internship and networking season kicks off. Either way, the reason to live near the CBD is the same: law firms, moot rounds, and the events that actually build your legal career all happen downtown, not on campus.

## NUS Law Moved to Kent Ridge. Here's Why That Matters for Where You Live

For close to two decades, NUS Law ran out of the Bukit Timah campus, a separate site on Bukit Timah Road, apart from the main Kent Ridge campus where most other faculties sit. Anyone who researched NUS Law housing even a year or two ago would have read that Bukit Timah, not Kent Ridge, was the address to plan around.

That's no longer current. NUS Law has relocated to Kent Ridge, joining the rest of the university, with the faculty marking the move as a return to the main campus after its long stint at Bukit Timah. If you're starting at NUS Law now, your lectures, tutorials, and faculty life happen at Kent Ridge, on the Circle Line in the far west of the island.

Practically, this pushes NUS Law students further from the CBD than the old Bukit Timah site did. Bukit Timah sat roughly in the middle of the island. Kent Ridge sits at the southwestern edge, well outside the financial district, and further still from Raffles Place, Marina Bay, and the courts.

That gap matters more for law than for most other degrees, because so much of a law student's actual career groundwork, the internships, the moots, the networking, happens in town, not on campus.

## Why Law Students Specifically Want to Be Near the CBD

**Law firms cluster in the CBD, not near either campus.** Singapore's largest firms, the ones running the internship and training pipelines most law students go through, are based in Raffles Place, Marina Bay, and the surrounding CBD core. Allen & Gledhill, for instance, runs out of One Marina Boulevard. None of the major firms are anywhere near Kent Ridge, and few are near Bras Basah either, so a CBD-adjacent room cuts the commute for whichever firm you land at, not just one.

**Practice training and vacation attachments run out of these same offices.** Before qualifying as an advocate and solicitor in Singapore, law graduates go through a structured practice training period at a law firm, on top of whatever internships or vacation attachments happen during the degree itself. Those placements sit wherever the firm's office is, almost always the CBD, and they don't pause for a long commute from the west side of the island.

**Moot court and inter-varsity events are usually held in town, not on campus.** Mooting is a core part of legal training everywhere, and in Singapore that means practice rounds, judged rounds, and the odd guest-judge session held at law firm offices, the Supreme Court precinct, or event spaces in the city, since that's where the judges, partners, and alumni volunteering their evenings actually are. A room near the CBD means getting to a 7pm moot practice doesn't eat your whole evening in transit.

**Networking events default to downtown venues.** Firm mixers, alumni panels, and the informal coffee chats that turn into internship offers all tend to happen where the firms are. For a Kent Ridge-based NUS Law student, that's now a real trip each way. For an SMU Law student, it's usually a short walk.

## SMU Law: Already in the City, Already an Advantage

SMU's Yong Pung How School of Law sits on Armenian Street, in the Bras Basah and City Hall precinct, genuinely inside the city rather than adjacent to it. That's not a marketing line, it's the actual campus address, and it's one of the reasons SMU built its whole undergraduate model around being embedded in the CBD rather than on a suburban campus.

For SMU Law students, the "near the CBD" question mostly answers itself: campus already is near the CBD. The housing decision becomes about which pocket of the city centre, Bras Basah, Bugis, the Beach Road corridor, or slightly further out toward the East Coast fringe, fits your budget and commute preference best, rather than whether to be near the centre at all.

## NUS Law vs SMU Law: The Housing Calculation Now

| | NUS Law (2026 onward) | SMU Law |
|---|---|---|
| Campus location | Kent Ridge, southwestern edge of the island | Armenian Street, Bras Basah / City Hall, in the CBD fringe |
| Distance from CBD firms | Meaningfully further than the old Bukit Timah site | Walkable to short MRT ride |
| Case for CBD-adjacent housing | Strong: campus is now far from firms, moots, and events | Built in: campus and firms are close together already |
| What to optimise for | A room that's reasonably central, splitting the difference between campus and CBD, or leaning CBD if internships matter more than lecture commutes | A room in the city centre or city fringe, since almost everything you need is already nearby |

Neither situation is bad. It just means NUS Law students now face a genuine tradeoff between campus commute and CBD access that didn't exist in the Bukit Timah years, while SMU Law students get both by default.

## So Where Should You Actually Live?

For general CBD commute math, MRT lines, and how travel time actually breaks down by neighbourhood, that's covered properly in [where to live by commute time to the CBD](/blog/where-to-live-by-commute-time-to-cbd-singapore), so we won't repeat it here. The short version for law specifically:

If most of your week is lectures and tutorials, weight toward campus. If your week is increasingly internships, attachments, and moot rounds as you move through the degree, a CBD-adjacent room starts paying for itself in time back, especially once you're doing vacation attachments or your practice training stint at a firm downtown.

Co-living rooms tend to suit this stage well, since they're furnished, come with flexible lease terms that fit a semester or an attachment period, and don't require chasing a landlord for a short-term let. ## FAQ

**Is NUS Law still at Bukit Timah?**
No. As of 2026, NUS Law has relocated to the main Kent Ridge campus after close to two decades at the separate Bukit Timah site. Older guides and forum posts that still say Bukit Timah are out of date.

**Is SMU Law actually in the CBD?**
SMU's Yong Pung How School of Law is on Armenian Street, in the Bras Basah and City Hall area, which sits on the fringe of the CBD rather than deep inside it. It's genuinely close, a short MRT ride or a walkable distance to Raffles Place, and much closer than either NUS campus.

**Why do law students care more about CBD proximity than other students?**
Because so much of the practical side of a law degree, internships, vacation attachments, practice training, and moot court, happens at law firm offices and event venues in the CBD rather than on campus. A commute that's fine for lectures can become a real burden once you're also getting to a firm several days a week.

**Does living near the CBD help before I even start internships?**
Some. Networking events, guest lectures held off campus, and moot practice sessions all tend to happen in town from early in the degree, not just once formal internships start.
