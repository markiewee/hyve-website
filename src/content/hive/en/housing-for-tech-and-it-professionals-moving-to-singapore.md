Housing for tech and IT professionals moving to Singapore isn't anchored to one address the way it is for banking or law. Software and IT roles cluster around two very different areas, one-north/Buona Vista and the CBD, and a growing number of companies aren't fixed to either. Most tech professionals move on an Employment Pass, with Tech.Pass as a narrower route for a small group of proven tech leaders and founders. Because hybrid and remote work is normal in tech, commute time matters less here than it does for a trading desk or a law firm, which changes how you should actually pick a room.

This guide covers where Singapore's tech employers actually sit, which work pass applies to you, and why the usual "live as close to the office as possible" advice matters less for this profession than most others.

## Where Do Tech Companies In Singapore Actually Work?

There's no single tech district in Singapore the way there's a single financial district. Two areas do most of the heavy lifting, plus a long tail of offices scattered across the island.

### One-north and Buona Vista

One-north is Singapore's purpose-built research and technology precinct, developed by JTC in the Buona Vista/Queenstown area and served by Buona Vista MRT station on the Circle Line and East-West Line. It's split into precincts: Fusionopolis and Mediapolis for infocomm, media, and engineering companies, Biopolis for biomedical research, and Vista for corporate offices.

The software and consumer tech side of one-north sits mostly in Fusionopolis and Mediapolis. Confirmed tenants include Google's Asia-Pacific headquarters, Grab (headquartered at 3 Media Close, within Mediapolis), Razer, Shopee, and Ubisoft Singapore, alongside JTC LaunchPad @ one-north, a startup cluster that includes BLOCK71 Singapore ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-north)). If your offer letter names one of these, one-north is your actual commute anchor, not "somewhere central."

### The CBD

Singapore's financial district, Raffles Place, Marina Bay, and Shenton Way, isn't just banks. It's also where a lot of fintech and bank-employed engineering teams sit, since major banks run substantial in-house technology operations out of their CBD towers rather than a separate tech campus. DBS, for instance, anchors a large footprint at Marina Bay Financial Centre that includes its technology functions, not just front-office banking ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina_Bay_Financial_Centre)). If you're joining a bank's tech or engineering arm, or a fintech headquartered in the CBD, this is your likely base.

### Everywhere Else

Beyond these two clusters, tech offices are distributed across the island: Changi Business Park, Paya Lebar, and smaller offices in Orchard, Novena, and the east. Plenty of smaller software companies, regional tech offices, and IT consultancies don't sit in either flagship cluster at all. The honest answer for a lot of tech roles is: check the actual office address on your offer letter before assuming one-north or the CBD applies to you.

## Which Work Pass Applies to You?

Almost every tech professional moving to Singapore lands on one of two passes, and they're built for very different situations.

### The Employment Pass Is the Default Route

The **Employment Pass (EP)** is the standard route for the overwhelming majority of tech hires, whether you're a software engineer, product manager, data scientist, or IT specialist joining an existing company. It requires a Singapore-registered employer to sponsor you and runs against a minimum qualifying salary that rises with age.

As of the framework currently in effect, the general Employment Pass qualifying salary starts at S$5,600 a month for applicants aged 23 and under, rising to S$10,700 at 45 and above. Financial services roles, relevant if you're joining a bank's tech team or a regulated fintech, are held to a higher line: S$6,200 at 23 and under, rising to S$11,800 at 45 and above, with both thresholds already scheduled to rise again from 1 January 2027 ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)).

Meeting the salary bar is only the first stage. EP applicants also need to clear the Complementarity Assessment Framework (COMPASS), a points-based test weighing salary, qualifications, workforce diversity, and support for local employment, with a minimum pass mark of 40 points ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)). Fast-growing tech companies newly expanding into Singapore may separately qualify for the **Tech@SG Programme**, which can smooth EP applications for their hires.

### Tech.Pass Is a Narrow, High-Bar Route

**Tech.Pass** isn't a general tech-worker visa. It's built for a small group of established tech leaders, entrepreneurs, and investors, and applicants only need to meet one of three criteria, not all of them:

- A last-drawn fixed monthly salary of at least S$20,000 in the previous year, or
- At least 5 years in a senior leadership role at a tech company with a valuation or market capitalisation of at least US$500 million, or funding raised of at least US$30 million, or
- At least 5 years building a tech product with either 100,000+ monthly active users or at least US$100 million in revenue

Tech.Pass runs for 2 years and can be renewed once for a further 2 years. Unlike an EP, it isn't tied to a single employer: holders can start and run their own company, work for multiple Singapore firms at once, sit on boards, invest, teach at local universities, and mentor or advise startups ([Paul Hype Page](https://www.paulhypepage.com/tech-pass-singapore/)). The pass is capped at 500 holders at any one time, allocated on a first-come, first-served basis. If you're joining an existing company as an individual contributor or a mid-level manager, you're almost certainly on an EP, not Tech.Pass, regardless of how senior "tech" makes the title sound.

## Why Commute Matters Less For Tech Than For Most Other Jobs

Banking, law, and consulting run on fixed office hours or unpredictable late finishes tied to a physical desk. Tech doesn't, for the most part. Hybrid work, two or three days in the office rather than five, is standard at most Singapore tech employers, and even fully in-office roles rarely carry the same after-hours desk-bound culture as a trading floor.

That changes the housing calculation. A 30-minute commute that would cost a banker sleep on a 9pm finish costs a tech employee almost nothing on a day they're only in twice a week. It means you can weigh space, price, and neighbourhood fit more heavily than raw proximity, provided the commute is still manageable on the days you do go in.

It doesn't mean location stops mattering entirely. A room with no reasonable MRT line to one-north or the CBD still turns hybrid days into a slog, and a home setup that can't support a full day of video calls on remote days is its own problem. The shift is from "live as close as possible" to "live somewhere with a workable line in and a room you can actually work from."

## What to Check Before You Book

- **Confirm your actual office address**, not just the company name. Some tech employers split teams between one-north, the CBD, and a smaller satellite office, and your specific team's location may not match the headline HQ.
- **Check the MRT line, not just the district.** A room technically near one-north that needs two transfers to reach Buona Vista station isn't actually convenient. Direct Circle Line or East-West Line access matters more than map distance.
- **Confirm your pass route before you commit to a lease.** EP processing and start dates move fast for tech hires, often within a few weeks of an offer, so line up housing you can book and confirm remotely rather than waiting on an in-person viewing.
- **Prioritise a room you can actually work from on remote or hybrid days.** Reliable wifi, a quiet space for calls, and a proper desk setup matter more for tech roles than for jobs where you're never expected to work from your room.
- **Weigh space and price against commute days, not commute distance alone.** If you're only in the office two or three days a week, a slightly longer but cheaper or larger room can be the better trade.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do most tech companies in Singapore have offices?**
Two clusters dominate: one-north/Buona Vista (Fusionopolis, Mediapolis, and the JTC LaunchPad startup cluster) for consumer tech and infocomm companies, and the CBD (Raffles Place, Marina Bay, Shenton Way) for bank technology teams and CBD-based fintechs. Many tech offices sit outside both, so always check your actual offer letter.

**Should I get an Employment Pass or a Tech.Pass?**
Almost every tech hire joining an existing company as an employee needs an Employment Pass, sponsored by that employer. Tech.Pass is a narrow route for established tech leaders, founders, and investors who meet a high bar on past salary, company scale, or product success, and it isn't employer-tied.

**Does commute really matter less for tech jobs than other professions?**
Generally yes, because hybrid work, typically two or three office days a week, is standard across most Singapore tech employers, unlike banking or law where long or unpredictable office hours are common. It still matters on the days you're in, so a workable line to your office remains worth checking.

**Is the Employment Pass salary threshold different for tech roles at banks?**
Yes, if the employer is classified in financial services. Bank-employed engineers and tech staff are held to the higher financial services Employment Pass salary line, not the general threshold, even though the role itself is technical rather than front-office banking.

**Can I live somewhere central and just accept a longer commute since I work hybrid?**
Within reason. A hybrid schedule buys you flexibility, not immunity from a bad commute. A room with no direct line to your actual office still costs real time on the days you do go in, so weigh it against the money or space you'd save rather than ignoring it entirely.
