Sri Lankans moving to Singapore for university or work are usually weighing two things at once: how far a room sits from NUS, NTU, or SMU, and how far it sits from the CBD, since many end up working in finance, tech, or consulting after graduating, or arrive already employed. The good news is you rarely have to choose one over the other. Several MRT-served neighbourhoods keep both journeys reasonable, and this guide walks through the actual commute times, the areas that do this well, and where Singapore's small but established Sri Lankan community touchpoints sit.

## Where Do Sri Lankans in Singapore Tend to Gather?

Unlike Little India for the Indian community or Peninsula Plaza for the Burmese community, Singapore doesn't have one single "Sri Lankan district." The community is smaller and more dispersed. That said, a few genuine anchor points exist, clustered loosely around the same North East Line corridor.

The **Sri Lankaramaya Buddhist Temple** on St. Michael's Road in Bendemeer is the oldest Sri Lankan Theravada Buddhist temple in Singapore, built in 1952 and run by the Singapore Sinhala Buddhist Association, dating back to 1920 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankaramaya_Buddhist_Temple)). A short ride away, **Tekka Centre** in Little India is home to Raja Bojun, widely reported as the only Sri Lankan hawker stall in Singapore, and the wet market downstairs regularly stocks produce flown in from Sri Lanka ([DanielFoodDiary](https://danielfooddiary.com/2023/10/17/tekkacentre/)). Sri Lankan grocery shops, including outlets on Upper Dickson Road nearby, round out the essentials. Restaurants such as Kotuwa and Cloudstreet sit further afield, scattered across the island rather than concentrated in one enclave, which is normal for a smaller diaspora.

The practical takeaway: Bendemeer, Farrer Park, Boon Keng, and Little India, all on the North East Line, form the closest thing Singapore has to a Sri Lankan community corridor. Any area on that same line gives you an easy, direct ride to it.

## How Far Is Each Neighbourhood From NUS, NTU, and SMU?

This is where the planning gets decided, since the three universities sit in different parts of the island and the same "convenient" neighbourhood can mean a 20-minute ride to one campus and well over an hour to another.

| From | To NUS (Kent Ridge) | To NTU | To SMU (Bras Basah/City Hall) | To CBD (Raffles Place) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Serangoon** | About 25 min, direct on the Circle Line | Over an hour, two changes | Roughly 20 to 25 min, one change at Dhoby Ghaut | About 30 min, one change |
| **Jurong East** | Around 30 to 45 min, one change plus NUS's internal shuttle | About 40 min, direct to Pioneer then a short bus ride on service 179 | About 35 min, direct East-West Line to City Hall then a short walk | About 27 to 29 min, direct on the East-West Line |
| **Lentor / Upper Thomson** | Roughly 35 to 45 min, one change (best checked on the day) | Long, likely over an hour with two changes | Roughly 20 to 25 min, one change at Stevens onto the Downtown Line | About 22 min, direct on the Thomson-East Coast Line to Marina Bay |

A few patterns stand out. **Jurong East is the strongest all-rounder for NTU and the CBD**, since it sits a direct ride from both. **Serangoon and Lentor both deliver a fast CBD commute**, useful if you're a working professional rather than a student. **NTU is the outlier everywhere except Jurong East**, since it sits in the far west and nothing else on this list reaches it without a long ride or a bus transfer at the end.

If your daily route is anything other than Jurong East to NTU or Serangoon to NUS, test the exact journey on Google Maps or Citymapper at a weekday peak-hour departure before committing. Interchange stations like Dhoby Ghaut and Stevens can add real minutes at 8:30am that an off-peak search won't show.

## Students vs Working Professionals: Different Priorities, Same City

A first-year student on a Student Pass and a Sri Lankan professional a few years into an EP-holding career are usually optimising for different things, even though they're often choosing between the same handful of neighbourhoods.

Students tend to weight campus distance heavily, since a bad daily commute compounds over a full semester. Working professionals, especially in finance, tech, or consulting roles near the CBD, usually weight the office commute more, and treat the Bendemeer or Little India community touchpoints as a weekend visit rather than a daily need.

Neither priority is wrong, but conflating them leads to a bad pick. A student choosing Lentor purely because a working colleague recommended it may find the NUS or NTU commute genuinely inconvenient, even though it's a strong choice for someone commuting to Raffles Place every morning.

## Myth: You Have to Pick Between Community and Commute

This assumption is only partly true. If your target is NUS or SMU, several areas on the North East Line or with a clean one-change route into it keep both the community corridor and campus within a reasonable ride. It's genuinely true for NTU, where the west-side geography doesn't cooperate with a North East Line home base, and where Jurong East, despite sitting further from the community corridor, becomes the more sensible pick.

The honest answer depends on which campus, or which office, you're actually commuting to. Don't rule an area out on a general impression before checking your own route.

## What to Check Before You Sign, Student or Professional

A handful of Singapore rental basics apply regardless of visa type or which campus or office you're commuting to.

- **Three months is the legal minimum stay for any private rental in Singapore**, HDB, condo, or landed. No operator, co-living included, can legally offer a shorter term.
- **A licence agreement covers a room in a shared home; a tenancy agreement covers a whole unit.** Know which one you're signing before you pay a deposit.
- **Match your pass type to what the operator or landlord expects.** Student Pass, Dependant's Pass, or a work pass each carry slightly different documentation requirements. Our [guide to renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) covers this in full.
- **Match your lease length to your actual course or contract length**, not the landlord's default 12-month offer.
- **A shorter MRT ride on the map doesn't always mean a shorter real commute.** Our [guide to choosing a home by commute time to the CBD](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/where-to-live-by-commute-time-to-cbd-singapore) walks through how to test a listing's real commute before you book it, and our [NUS and NTU student housing guide](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/bukit-timah-clementi-student-housing) covers the campus side in more depth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a "Little Sri Lanka" neighbourhood in Singapore?**
Not in the way Little India or Peninsula Plaza function for other communities. The closest thing is the Bendemeer, Farrer Park, Boon Keng, and Little India stretch on the North East Line, home to the Sri Lankaramaya Buddhist Temple, the Raja Bojun stall at Tekka Centre, and nearby Sri Lankan grocery shops.

**Which neighbourhood works best for NUS?**
Serangoon offers a direct, roughly 25-minute ride on the Circle Line. Jurong East and Lentor both need a transfer and run longer, so test the exact route for your specific campus building.

**Which neighbourhood works best for NTU?**
Jurong East, by a clear margin. It's a direct ride to Pioneer with a short bus connection into campus. Serangoon and Lentor both involve a long commute to NTU and aren't a good fit if that's your daily campus.

**Which neighbourhood works best for a CBD job?**
Lentor delivers the fastest ride, about 22 minutes direct on the Thomson-East Coast Line. Serangoon and Jurong East are both reasonable too, at roughly 27 to 30 minutes.

**Can Sri Lankan students and professionals rent co-living rooms in Singapore?**
Yes, subject to the same three-month legal minimum as any private rental, and subject to the operator confirming your pass documentation, whether Student Pass, Dependant's Pass, or work pass. Check requirements before paying any deposit.
