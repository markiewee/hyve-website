Coming to Singapore to study and bringing a partner along changes your housing search. Most co-living rooms are built and priced for one person, so a couple usually needs to book a bigger private room as a pair, take a whole unit, or find a place that explicitly allows two occupants in one room. Visa-wise, a Student's Pass does not let you sponsor a partner for long-term stay in Singapore, so most partners come in on their own pass, whether that's their own Student's Pass, a work pass, or repeat visitor visits. Neither of those is a dealbreaker. It just means you plan for it early instead of discovering it after you've paid a deposit.

This guide covers both halves: the housing side (what actually changes when two people move into one room) and the visa side (what a partner can and can't do on Singapore soil long-term). Neither of us is going to sugarcoat the visa part, since getting it wrong costs you an overstay fine and a rough conversation with ICA.

## Can two people share one co-living room?

It depends entirely on the operator and the room, not on a blanket Singapore rule. Co-living rooms are usually sized and priced around a single bed and one occupant, so subletting half your room to a partner without telling the operator is a lease breach almost everywhere, Lazybee included. The honest options are:

- **Ask the operator directly if the room supports two occupants.** Some larger rooms can, especially ones built around a queen bed.
- **Book a bigger private room built for a couple**, where the rent already accounts for two people.
- **Take a whole unit** if you want your own space and don't need the built-in community of a shared house.
- **Keep it as visits rather than a co-tenancy** if your partner isn't relocating permanently, and just check the guest policy for how many nights are fine before it counts as a second occupant.

What you should not do is quietly move a partner in and hope nobody notices. Co-living houses are shared spaces with a handful of other tenants and usually a house captain who's around often. It gets noticed, and it puts your own tenancy at risk over something a two-minute conversation with the operator could have sorted out.

## Does your partner need a Long-Term Visit Pass?

Only if they intend to live in Singapore long-term and aren't studying or working here themselves. The Long-Term Visit Pass (LTVP) is Singapore's main pass for a dependant who isn't a citizen, permanent resident, or pass holder in their own right, and the eligibility rules for who can sponsor one are narrower than most students expect.

**A Student's Pass cannot sponsor an LTVP.** The categories that can sponsor an LTVP are Singapore citizens, permanent residents, and holders of an Employment Pass or S Pass, not Student's Pass holders. So if you're the one studying here on a Student's Pass, you cannot put your partner on an LTVP as your dependant, married or not.

That leaves your partner with a narrower set of realistic paths if they want to actually live in Singapore alongside you:

- **Their own Student's Pass**, if they're also enrolling in a Singapore institution.
- **Their own work pass** (Employment Pass, S Pass, or similar), if they have a job offer here. Once they hold that pass, they may then be able to sponsor you or vice versa depending on income and relationship status, but that's a separate track from the student route.
- **Repeat Social Visit Pass entries**, if they're not relocating permanently and are visiting for shorter, out overlapping stretches. This isn't a long-term living arrangement and comes with its own limits on how often you can re-enter this way.

**The relationship doesn't need to be marriage.** LTVP eligibility does extend to common-law partners in some tracks, notably common-law spouses of Employment Pass or S Pass holders, but the bar for what counts as "common-law spouse" is specific (recognised relationships under home-country law, or documented long-term cohabitation, not just a relationship label) and it still requires the sponsor to hold a qualifying pass.

### So what does a student couple actually do?

Realistically, one of three things:

1. **Both of you get your own Student's Pass**, each enrolled somewhere in Singapore. This is the cleanest path since neither of you is dependent on the other's pass.
2. **One of you studies, the other works.** If your partner lands a job that qualifies for an Employment Pass or S Pass, that opens up sponsorship options later, though it's a separate process from your studies.
3. **One of you is here on a pass, the other visits on a Social Visit Pass** for shorter stretches rather than relocating permanently. Fine for a long-distance-but-not-quite arrangement, not a substitute for both of you actually living here full time.

None of these are things a co-living operator can fix for you. Get the visa question answered by ICA or a registered immigration consultant before you lock in housing, not after, because the pass your partner ends up holding affects how long they can legally stay, which affects whether you need a room built for one long-term tenant or two.

## What to check before you book, as a couple

A short list, since this is the part actually inside your control:

- **Confirm two-occupant status with the operator in writing**, not just verbally, before you sign anything.
- **Check the room size and bed configuration.** A single bed in an 8 to 12 sqm room is a rough fit for two people long-term, comfort matters as much as legality.
- **Ask about the guest and overnight policy** if your partner is visiting rather than living in, so you know how many consecutive nights are fine before it needs a formal conversation.
- **Work out your partner's visa status before, not after**, moving day. A Student's Pass application, an Employment Pass application, or an LTVP assessment all take weeks, and none of them are same-week processes.
- **Budget for two people's worth of daily costs** even if you're only paying one room's rent, since food, transport and personal spending don't halve just because you're sharing a bed.

## The bottom line

Bringing a partner to Singapore as a student is common enough that operators and immigration authorities both have paths for it, just not one single simple path. On the visa side, know early that a Student's Pass can't sponsor an LTVP, so your partner's own status, whether that's their own Student's Pass, a work pass, or visiting on a Social Visit Pass, is the thing that actually determines how long they can stay.

## FAQ

**Can my partner live with me in my co-living room without telling the operator?**
No. Rooms are contracted and priced for the tenant on the agreement. Moving a partner in without informing the operator is a lease breach, and shared houses with other tenants and a house captain around usually make it obvious fast anyway.

**Can I sponsor my partner for an LTVP while I'm on a Student's Pass?**
No. LTVP sponsorship in Singapore requires the sponsor to be a citizen, permanent resident, or holder of an Employment Pass or S Pass. A Student's Pass does not qualify as a sponsoring pass.

**Does my partner need to be married to me to qualify for any pass?**
Not necessarily. Some LTVP tracks recognise common-law partners, particularly for Employment Pass or S Pass sponsors, but the bar for what counts is specific and documentation-heavy. Marriage isn't required everywhere, but it does simplify some categories.

**What's the realistic option if neither of us wants to marry and neither has a job offer yet?**
Both of you applying for your own Student's Pass through separate enrolments is usually the cleanest route, since neither pass depends on the other person's status.
