Vietnamese students heading to NUS or Bukit Timah should look at Clementi, Dover, Buona Vista, Queenstown, or Bukit Timah itself. NTU students should look at Pioneer, Boon Lay, or Jurong East. SMU students, whose campus sits right in the city centre, have the widest radius to choose from, since almost anywhere on a direct MRT line into town works. Match the area to your actual campus first, check the room is on a lease of three months or more (the legal floor in Singapore), and everything else, community, food, cost, follows from there.

That's the short version. Vietnam sends a steady and growing stream of students to Singapore's universities each year, on ASEAN scholarships, self-funded places, and exchange programmes alike, and most of them face the same housing decision in their first few months: hall if you can get it, off-campus or co-living if you can't or if you'd rather not compete for a spot. Here's how to think through that decision properly.

## Where Are Vietnamese Students Actually Studying in Singapore?

Three universities account for most of it: the National University of Singapore (NUS), Nanyang Technological University (NTU), and Singapore Management University (SMU). Each has a genuinely different campus location, so "housing near the main campuses" means three different answers, not one.

NUS's main Kent Ridge campus sits in the southwest, on the Circle Line, with a separate site for Law and public policy at Bukit Timah. NTU is further west again, near Jurong West, connected to the MRT network via a free shuttle bus from Pioneer and Boon Lay stations. SMU is the outlier: a compact, purpose-built campus in the Bugis-Bras Basah district, a two-minute walk from Bencoolen MRT and five from Bras Basah MRT, right in the middle of town ([SMU](https://www.smu.edu.sg/campus-life/visiting-smu)).

| University | Campus | Getting there | Where to look for a room |
|---|---|---|---|
| NUS | Kent Ridge in the southwest, on the Circle Line, plus a separate Bukit Timah site for Law and public policy | Circle Line | Clementi, Dover, Buona Vista, Queenstown, Bukit Timah |
| NTU | Near Jurong West, further west again | A free shuttle bus from Pioneer and Boon Lay stations | Pioneer, Boon Lay, Jurong East |
| SMU | Compact, purpose-built campus in the Bugis and Bras Basah district | Two minutes' walk from Bencoolen MRT, five from Bras Basah | The widest radius of the three. Rochor, Selegie, Bugis, the fringe of Little India, or Joo Chiat and Katong via the Thomson-East Coast Line |

There's a real, organised Vietnamese student presence across all three. The Vietnamese Youth Association in Singapore (VNYA) was formed in 2016 as a joint effort across seven Singapore institutions including NUS, NTU and SMU, and was formally recognised in 2019 under the Vietnam National Union of Students ([Vietnam+](https://en.vietnamplus.vn/vietnamese-students-in-singapore-actively-join-community-activities-post245734.vnp)). It works alongside the Vietnamese Embassy and the local Vietnamese Community Liaison Committee on events, mentoring, and support for newer arrivals, which is worth knowing about before you land, not after.

## Housing Near SMU: The City Option

SMU doesn't have a "student belt" around it the way NUS and NTU do, because it sits inside the city itself. Areas like Rochor, Selegie, Bugis, and the fringe of Little India all put you within a short walk or one MRT stop of campus. Slightly further out, Joo Chiat and Katong on the east side connect into town via the Thomson-East Coast Line, trading a few extra minutes on the train for a genuinely different, quieter neighbourhood at the end of the day.

## Where to Find Vietnamese Food and Community

This is the part generic housing guides skip, and it matters more than people expect in the first few months. A few reference points, so you know where to look:

- **Joo Chiat and Katong**, on the east side of the island, is where you'll find Viet Valuemart, a dedicated Vietnamese grocery store on Joo Chiat Road, alongside Vietnamese restaurants like Long Phung and Banh Mi Saigon. It's also one of the areas covered in our [Joo Chiat and Katong renter's guide](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/joo-chiat-katong-renters-guide), and it sits on a direct line into the SMU end of town.
- **Golden Mile Complex on Beach Road** has a Vietnamese grocery section inside its Thai supermarket on the second floor, a useful stop if you're closer to the city centre than the east.
- **Geylang** has its own cluster of Vietnamese eateries, including Gánh Xưa, for anyone based in that part of the island.
- The **Vietnamese Embassy** is at 10 Leedon Park, near Farrer Road, for passport and consular matters, and it works with VNYA on community events through the year.

## Visa and Lease Basics Specific to Vietnamese Students

Vietnamese passport holders can enter Singapore visa-free for up to 30 days for a visit, but that exemption has nothing to do with studying here. Anyone enrolled at NUS, NTU, SMU, or another approved institution needs a Student's Pass, applied for through the university, separately from that visa-free entry allowance.

Once you're here on a Student's Pass, the housing rules are the same ones that apply to every renter in Singapore, not something specific to your nationality: a minimum stay of three consecutive months on any private rental, room or whole unit, HDB or condo. We've written a full breakdown of how pass type and lease length interact in our [guide to renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](https://www.lazybee.sg/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore), which is worth reading before you sign anything, especially if your course runs for a single semester rather than a full year.

One thing worth flagging for scholarship holders specifically: ASEAN Undergraduate Scholarships, which many Vietnamese students at NUS, NTU, and SUTD are on, come with an annual accommodation allowance rather than a housing guarantee. Hall or PGP is common in year one if you apply early enough, but it isn't automatic every year, so it pays to have an off-campus or co-living option scoped out rather than assuming a hall place will always come through.

## On Campus, Off Campus, or Co-Living

- **On campus** (hall or PGP) is usually cheapest if you get a place, but it's application-based and the windows close early, often before an offer letter has even arrived.
- **Off campus**, renting a whole flat or a room through an individual landlord, gives you the most control over who you live with and where, at the cost of chasing listings and negotiating terms yourself.
- **Co-living**, a furnished room with utilities and wifi bundled into the rent and the term published up front, tends to suit exchange students, postgrads, or anyone who'd rather not spend their first weeks in Singapore landlord-hunting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do Vietnamese students need a visa to study in Singapore?**
The 30-day visa-free entry for Vietnamese passport holders covers short visits only. Studying here requires a Student's Pass, arranged through your university, regardless of the visa exemption.

**Is there a Vietnamese student community across NUS, NTU, and SMU?**
Yes. The Vietnamese Youth Association in Singapore (VNYA) was set up jointly across several institutions including NUS, NTU, and SMU, and is officially recognised by the Vietnam National Union of Students. It works with the Vietnamese Embassy on events and support for students.

**Where can I buy Vietnamese groceries in Singapore?**
Viet Valuemart on Joo Chiat Road is a dedicated Vietnamese grocery store. Golden Mile Complex on Beach Road also has a Vietnamese section inside its Thai supermarket, and Geylang has a cluster of Vietnamese restaurants.

**Can I rent for just one semester as an exchange student?**
Yes, as long as the term is three months or longer, which covers most single semesters. Anything shorter than three months isn't legally available on the private rental market, hall or licensed short-stay accommodation only.

**Which area is best if I don't know my exact class schedule yet?**
Pick based on campus first: Clementi or Dover for NUS, Pioneer or Boon Lay for NTU, and anywhere on a direct line into town for SMU. Fine-tune once your timetable is confirmed using the commute guide linked above.
