Housing for management consultants on a short Singapore assignment runs into one hard fact fast: no landlord can legally offer less than Singapore's private rental minimum of 3 consecutive months, whatever your staffing plan says. A 6-week case, a 10-week diligence sprint, even a fly-in-fly-out project that only needs you on the ground three days a week, all still need a room booked for a minimum of 3 months if you want your own furnished base rather than a hotel. Co-living rooms close to the CBD are the most practical fit, furnished, bills-included, priced for exactly this length of stay rather than a 12-month lease. The rest of this guide covers why consulting staffing collides with that 3-month floor more than almost any other profession, and what to do about it.

## How long is a typical assignment for a management consultant?

This is the part that makes consulting a different housing problem than almost any other white-collar job. A banking analyst or an in-house counsel takes a permanent role and needs housing for a year or longer. A consultant gets staffed project by project, and projects range from a diagnostic that wraps in a few weeks to a transformation program that runs a year or more in phases. Most individual staffings land somewhere in between, commonly a matter of weeks to three or four months, with many first assignments on the shorter end of that range.

Layer on top of that the fly-in culture still common for regional engagements, where a consultant based elsewhere works on-site in Singapore part of the week and travels the rest. Even when you're based here for a full project, the staffing plan often shifts, an engagement extends, a phase gets cut short, or you roll onto a different case with a different timeline before you've unpacked.

None of this maps onto a housing market built around 12-month leases, which is exactly the mismatch this guide is about.

## The 3-month minimum stay problem, stated plainly

Singapore law sets a minimum stay of 3 consecutive months for any occupant of a private residential property, whether that's a whole condo or a single room in a shared unit. HDB flats run a longer 6-month minimum. This isn't a co-living rule or an operator's pricing choice, it's the baseline rental law every landlord and operator has to work within, and no one can legally offer you a shorter stay in a private residential room no matter how short your actual assignment is.

| Property type | Legal minimum stay | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| Private residential, whole condo or a single room in a shared unit | 3 consecutive months | Baseline rental law. No landlord or operator can legally go shorter |
| HDB flat | 6 months | HDB |
| Serviced apartment or hotel | Can be shorter in some cases, under different licensing | Comes at a steep monthly premium over co-living |

For a consultant on a 6 to 10 week case, this is a genuine mismatch, not a minor inconvenience. You'll end up paying for housing beyond your working dates, because the legal floor is longer than the assignment. The honest move is to accept that up front rather than search for a workaround that doesn't exist. A serviced apartment or hotel can, in some cases, offer shorter stays than a private residential room, since they typically fall under different licensing, but they carry a steep monthly premium over co-living, and that premium is usually the real tradeoff, not whether a shorter stay exists at all.

If your case is likely to extend, which happens often enough in consulting to plan for, booking the full 3 months from the start and treating anything shorter as a bonus beats booking short and hoping for an easy extension later.

## What actually works for a project-based stay

**Co-living rooms** close to the CBD are usually the best value for a 3-month-plus consulting stay. They're furnished, bills are bundled into rent, and a well-run operator prices specifically for stays in this range instead of penalising you for not signing a year. You also get an easier exit if staffing changes mid-project, since notice terms on a co-living licence are generally simpler than breaking a standard tenancy.

**Serviced apartments** (think Ascott, Fraser Suites, Somerset, Oakwood) suit consultants whose firm is picking up the tab through a relocation or per diem budget, since they're priced at a level that assumes an expense account, not a personal one. They can also offer stays under 3 months in some cases, which matters if your engagement genuinely runs shorter and your firm is footing a premium for that flexibility.

**A firm-block booking or corporate housing arrangement** is common at the larger firms for consultants rotating through Singapore regularly. If your firm already has an arrangement in place, that's worth checking before you search independently, since it may already solve the 3-month problem at the firm level rather than yours.

## What to check before you book

- **Confirm your actual last working day first**, then book to that date plus any buffer, rather than booking "about 3 months" and hoping it lines up.
- **Ask what happens if the engagement extends or ends early.** Both happen often in consulting, and the terms for either should be settled before you sign, not renegotiated after the fact.
- **Check what's bundled into the rate.** Utilities, wifi, and basic furnishing should already be included in a co-living or serviced rate, since setting these up yourself eats time you don't have mid-project.
- **Weigh the commute against your actual working hours**, not just the district name, since consulting hours can run late and a bad commute costs more time here than in most other roles.
- **Get deposit and notice terms in writing**, particularly if there's a real chance your staffing changes and you need to move out earlier than planned.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I rent for less than 3 months if my consulting case only runs 6 weeks?**
Not in a private residential room. Singapore's 3-month minimum stay applies regardless of how long your actual assignment runs. A serviced apartment or hotel may offer shorter stays in some cases, but typically at a significant premium over co-living.

**Does my consulting firm usually cover housing costs?**
It varies by firm and by engagement. Some cover housing directly or provide a per diem, others expect you to arrange and expense it yourself. Confirm this with your staffing or mobility team before you commit to anything, since it changes whether a serviced apartment or a co-living room makes more financial sense.

**What if my case gets extended past my original booking?**
Raise it with your housing provider as soon as the extension looks likely, ideally before your original end date rather than after. Ask whether an extension is a straightforward continuation of the same room or a fresh booking, since that affects both price and availability.

**Is co-living a normal choice for someone at a top-tier consulting firm, or is it seen as a downgrade from a serviced apartment?**
It's a practical choice, not a status downgrade. Plenty of consultants on personal budgets or shorter engagements choose co-living specifically because it's furnished, bills-included, and priced for a 3-month stay without the serviced apartment premium.

**Where should I live relative to my client site if it changes mid-engagement?**
Staying close to the general CBD rather than one specific building gives you more flexibility if you're reassigned to a different client site within the same district.
