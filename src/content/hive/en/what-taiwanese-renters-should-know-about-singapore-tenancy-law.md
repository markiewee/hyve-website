There's no separate tenancy law for Taiwanese nationals in Singapore. Whether you're arriving on a Student Pass, an Employment Pass (EP), or an S Pass, you follow the same rules as any other foreign renter: a valid pass in hand before you sign, a minimum stay of three months in private residential property (six months in HDB), and a licence or tenancy agreement setting out your deposit and notice period. The part that actually catches Taiwanese renters out isn't the law itself. It's the gap between Singapore's 30-day visa-free entry and the pass you actually need before a landlord or operator can let you sign.

This piece draws on what we've already published on Singapore tenancy law and frames it for the situation a Taiwanese renter is likely to be in: possibly here first on a short visa-free visit to scout the city, a pass application in progress, and a room to lock down once the paperwork lands.

## The Law Doesn't Change Based on Your Nationality

Singapore's rental rules apply the same way regardless of where you're from. The Urban Redevelopment Authority sets a three-month minimum stay for private residential property, HDB sets six months for flats, and there's an occupancy cap on how many unrelated people can share a unit. None of that shifts because you're from Taiwan, India, or Australia. The full mechanics, including what counts as a breach and who's liable if a unit gets flagged, are in [Is Co-Living Legal in Singapore?](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/is-coliving-legal-in-singapore)

Where Taiwan does come up is in the numbers, not the law. Taiwan is one of Singapore's Ministry of Manpower "traditional source countries" for certain work pass categories, alongside Malaysia, China, Hong Kong, and South Korea. For the passes that matter to most co-living renters, EP, S Pass, and Student Pass, the process is identical to any other nationality: apply, get the physical pass issued, then sign.

## The 30-Day Visa-Free Window Isn't a Pass to Rent On

Taiwan (ROC) passport holders can enter Singapore visa-free for up to 30 days for business or social visits, granted at the discretion of ICA officers at the point of entry. That's genuinely useful for flying in to view rooms and get a feel for a neighbourhood before committing to anything. It isn't, on its own, a basis to sign a lease or licence agreement.

A properly run operator, Lazybee included, asks to see your issued Student Pass, EP, or S Pass, not a boarding pass or an entry stamp, before confirming a room. If you're viewing ahead of your pass being issued, say so upfront. It's a normal sequence, and worth planning your trip around rather than trying to sign the same week you land.

## Which Pass Actually Lets You Sign a Lease

A Student Pass, EP, or S Pass each let you rent, but only once the pass is physically issued, not while an application is "in progress" or you're holding a job offer or admission letter. The full breakdown of which passes qualify and what documents a legitimate operator checks is in [Can Foreigners Rent in Singapore? Passes & Documents](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/can-foreigners-rent-in-singapore). Expect to show your passport, the pass card itself, proof of employment or enrolment, and a local contact number, checked against MOM's or ICA's own verification system before a room is confirmed.

If you're weighing an EP against an S Pass against a Student Pass, and how each should shape how long a lease you commit to, that comparison is covered directly in [Renting on an EP vs S Pass vs Student Pass in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore). One rule worth carrying from that piece regardless of pass type: match your lease length to your pass validity, not the landlord's default term. Signing a 12-month lease against a pass with eight months left creates a problem that has nothing to do with the room.

## Licence Agreement or Tenancy Agreement: What You're Actually Signing

If you're renting a room rather than a whole unit, the contract in front of you is almost certainly a licence agreement, not a tenancy agreement. That's standard across co-living in Singapore and isn't a red flag by itself. The real difference, and the specific clauses that only show up in licence agreements, is set out in [Licence Agreement vs Tenancy Agreement in Singapore](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/licence-agreement-vs-tenancy-agreement-singapore). Read it before signing, particularly the section on whether the operator can reassign your room during your stay.

## Security Deposit and Stamp Duty: What's Standard

One month's rent is the norm for a co-living room deposit in Singapore, refundable in full barring real damage beyond fair wear and tear. There's no government-set figure for this, so it's the clause in your own agreement, not a statute, that governs the amount and the return conditions. What a deposit can and can't legally be deducted for, and the move-in photos that make a refund straightforward instead of a dispute, is in [Security Deposit Co-Living Singapore: What's Standard](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore).

Separately, most room agreements attract stamp duty at 0.4% of the total rent for the lease period, payable to IRAS within 14 days of signing (30 days if the agreement was signed outside Singapore). Full detail on the rate, who typically pays, and what happens if the window is missed is in [Stamp Duty on a Room Rental: Rate, Who Pays, Deadline](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/stamp-duty-room-rental-singapore).

## Practical Friction Points Worth Planning For

A few things aren't legal issues at all, but come up often enough for renters arriving from Taiwan that they're worth naming directly.

**No Singapore bank account yet.** IRAS accepts card payment through its e-Stamping portal, so no local banking in your first week isn't a dead end for stamp duty. Some operators, Lazybee included, walk tenants through e-stamping directly if they've just arrived.

**Passport name romanisation.** A minor mismatch in how your name is romanised across your passport, pass, and employment or admission letter can slow down verification. Check all three match before your first viewing, not after.

**Moving in with colleagues or classmates.** The occupancy cap applies regardless of group size or relationship. Confirm the unit's actual cleared occupancy count before assuming a group can all move in together.

**Guarantor expectations for students.** A Student Pass holder aged 18 or above can sign their own agreement, though some operators still ask for a guarantor or parent's contact as a risk preference, not a legal requirement.

## Documents to Have Ready

Before your first viewing, have both originals and digital copies of:

- Passport, valid for your intended stay
- Issued pass card (Student Pass, EP, or S Pass), not an offer letter, admission letter, or IPA
- Employment or enrolment letter matching your pass details
- A recent payslip or bank statement
- A local Singapore phone number, even a newly activated one
- An emergency contact name and number

Having this ready before your first viewing is often the difference between signing within a day and losing a week to document chasing.

## Where This Leaves You

None of the substance changes because you're renting from Taiwan rather than anywhere else. The law is the same. What's different is timing: the 30-day visa-free window is good for scouting but not for signing, and your pass needs to be in hand before a room is actually confirmed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I sign a room agreement in Singapore while I'm here on the 30-day visa-free visit?**
Generally no. A properly run operator checks for an issued Student Pass, EP, or S Pass before confirming a room, not a visa-free entry stamp. Use a short visit to view rooms and neighbourhoods, then sign once your actual pass is issued.

**Do Taiwanese nationals need a different type of pass to rent in Singapore?**
No. The same categories apply to every nationality: Employment Pass, S Pass, Work Permit, Student Pass, Dependant's Pass, or Long-Term Visit Pass. None carries different tenancy rules based on where you're from.

**I don't have a Singapore bank account yet. Can I still pay stamp duty on my agreement?**
Yes. IRAS accepts credit or debit card payment through its e-Stamping portal, so a local bank account isn't a prerequisite. Some operators will also handle the stamping step for you if you've just arrived.

**How long should my lease be if I'm on an Employment Pass or S Pass?**
Match it to your pass validity, not the landlord's default term. If your pass has less time left than the lease being offered, ask for a shorter or more flexible arrangement instead.

**Is it harder for a group of Taiwanese friends or colleagues to rent rooms in the same unit?**
Not legally harder, but the unit's occupancy cap still applies regardless of group size or relationship. Confirm the cleared occupancy count for the specific unit before assuming several of you can move in together.
