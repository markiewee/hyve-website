Housing for finance and banking professionals in Singapore comes down to one variable most other jobs don't weigh as heavily: how fast you can get to Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or Shenton Way, at any hour. Banking runs on long, unpredictable hours, especially in investment banking, trading, and markets roles, and a room 40 minutes from your desk turns a 9pm close into a 10:30pm one. This guide covers where Singapore's financial district actually sits, why the commute matters more here than in most professions, and how the Employment Pass rules work differently for finance hires than for everyone else.

If you're comparing this to housing for a business school internship or a short consulting assignment, this guide is written for the working, career-track version of the question instead. Those are different searches with different answers, linked at the bottom.

## Where Is Singapore's Financial District Actually Located?

Singapore's banking and financial services cluster sits in a compact stretch of the Downtown Core: Raffles Place, Marina Bay, and Shenton Way. It's smaller on the map than people expect, which is exactly why commute time compresses so much once you're actually inside it.

**Raffles Place** is the historical core, an MRT interchange (NS26/EW14) surrounded by towers like One Raffles Place and OCBC Centre. **Marina Bay** sits one stop further south, an interchange on three lines (NS27/CC33/TE20), home to Marina Bay Financial Centre and a growing share of the banks that used to sit only in Raffles Place. **Shenton Way**, once just a road name, now has its own Thomson-East Coast Line station (TE19), which opened in 2022 and put a direct MRT stop under a stretch of the district that used to mean a walk from Tanjong Pagar or Raffles Place.

These three points, plus Tanjong Pagar just south, form the actual walkable financial district. A room anywhere on the Thomson-East Coast, North-South, East-West, or Circle Line that reaches this cluster without more than one transfer is, in practice, in commuting range for a banking job. A room that needs two transfers or a bus leg isn't, no matter what the map distance says.

## Why Does Commute Matter More for Banking Than Other Jobs?

Most Singapore jobs run something close to a fixed 9-to-6. Banking, especially investment banking, trading, and markets-facing roles, doesn't. Deal teams work to closing timelines, not clock times. Trading desks run to market hours, which for a desk covering US markets can mean a late finish that has nothing to do with when the office day started.

That changes what a commute costs you. A 40-minute ride home isn't an annoyance tacked onto a normal day, it's 40 minutes taken directly off sleep on a night when you're already getting in late. Over a working year, a short commute back from Shenton Way is worth more to a banker than the same short commute is worth to someone leaving the office reliably at 6pm.

It also cuts the other way in the morning. Markets and deal teams often start early too, ahead of European or US market opens, or ahead of a client call scheduled around someone else's time zone. A room close to the CBD absorbs both ends of a day that a 9-to-5 role never tests.

### Does This Apply to Every Finance Job?

Not evenly. Back-office operations, compliance, and many corporate banking roles run closer to standard hours, and a longer commute is a genuine trade worth making for more space or lower rent. It's front-office roles, IB, trading, markets, and anything client-facing on a deal timeline, where proximity earns its cost fastest.

## The Employment Pass Threshold Is Higher for Financial Services

If you're moving to Singapore on an Employment Pass for a banking role, one number is worth knowing before you start budgeting for housing: the financial services sector has its own, higher salary bar. As of the current framework (in effect before 1 January 2027), the general Employment Pass qualifying salary starts at S$5,600 a month for applicants aged 23 and under, rising with age to S$10,700 at 45 and above. **Financial services roles are held to a higher line**: S$6,200 a month at 23 and under, rising to S$11,800 at 45 and above ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)).

That gap isn't small, and it isn't going away. A further increase is already scheduled for 1 January 2027, taking the general threshold to S$6,000 and the financial services threshold to S$6,600 at the youngest age band, scaling up to S$11,500 and S$12,700 respectively at 45 and above ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)).

Practically, this means two things for housing. First, if you're applying for an EP in a finance role, your salary has to clear a bar other EP applicants don't face, so check your offer against the financial services line specifically, not the general one you'll find quoted in generic relocation guides. Second, because the bar is higher, most working bankers on an EP are already earning well above the minimum on either side of the sector split, which means the housing constraint for most finance hires isn't affordability. It's finding something close enough to actually use the income on, rather than losing the evenings it was meant to buy back.

## Where to Actually Live for CBD Access

Living inside Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or Tanjong Pagar itself means paying prime-district rent for a small footprint, which is a real option if the budget supports it and the priority is a walk to work. Most finance professionals, including plenty of well-paid ones, choose instead to live a short, direct MRT ride away and take the trade of more space and a lower monthly outlay for a commute that's still under 20 to 25 minutes door to door.

That's where line choice matters more than raw distance on a map. A room on the same line as Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or Shenton Way, with no transfer, beats a room that's geographically closer but needs a change of trains.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where is Singapore's financial district?**
It's concentrated in the Downtown Core around Raffles Place, Marina Bay, and Shenton Way, with Tanjong Pagar just south. All four are reachable on the North-South, East-West, Circle, or Thomson-East Coast lines.

**Why does commute time matter more for banking jobs than other professions?**
Investment banking, trading, and markets roles run to deal timelines and market hours rather than a fixed workday, so a long commute costs more sleep and flexibility on the nights and mornings that matter most.

**Is the Employment Pass salary requirement different for finance jobs?**
Yes. Financial services roles are held to a higher qualifying salary than the general Employment Pass threshold, currently S$6,200 a month at the youngest age band versus S$5,600 for other sectors, both rising with age, with a further increase scheduled for 1 January 2027 ([MOM](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/employment-pass)).

**Do I need to live inside the CBD itself to work in banking in Singapore?**
No. A room on a direct MRT line into Raffles Place, Marina Bay, or Shenton Way with no transfer gets most of the benefit of a CBD address without the CBD rent, provided the ride stays short.

**Is this guide relevant if I'm only in Singapore for a few months?**
Not primarily. Short postings, including most consulting assignments, weigh lease flexibility more heavily than long-term commute optimisation. See the guide for management consultants on short Singapore assignments instead.
