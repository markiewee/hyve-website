Use this checklist before you commit to any room in Singapore: match your Student Pass validity to the lease length, confirm the deposit amount and refund conditions in writing, check exactly what's included in rent, view the room (or run a proper remote-viewing process) before paying anything, read the full tenancy or licence agreement, understand the notice period for moving out, confirm the unit is legally allowed to rent to the number of occupants living there, and know your stamp duty obligation. Work through it in order. Most first-time renters get burned by skipping a step, not by any single mistake.

The rest of this page breaks each item down by stage: before you view, during the viewing, before you sign, and the money and legal checks that sit underneath all of it.

| Stage | The point of it | Biggest single miss |
|---|---|---|
| Before you view | Set your constraints before a room can charm you out of them | Signing a 12-month lease on a pass with 7 months left |
| During the viewing | See the actual room, in its real condition, at a realistic hour | Accepting "this is the same layout as yours" instead of your room |
| Before you sign | Read the whole agreement, not the clauses someone highlights | Not documenting move-in condition with photos or an inventory |
| Money and legal | The part most first-timers skip entirely, and the costliest if it goes wrong | Not checking the unit is legally allowed to house the number of people in it |

## Before You View

Do this before you message an agent or book a single viewing.

- [ ] **Check your Student Pass validity date.** Find the exact expiry on your pass, not a rough guess. This is the number your lease length has to respect.
- [ ] **Match lease length to pass validity, not to the calendar.** A Student Pass typically runs one year or your course duration, whichever is shorter, and renews annually alongside your enrolment. Don't sign a 12-month lease on a pass with 7 months left.
- [ ] **Set a monthly budget that includes more than rent.** Add transport, food, and a buffer for the deposit and first month upfront. Know this number before you start browsing listings, not after you've fallen for a room.
- [ ] **Decide your radius from campus.** Pick a maximum commute time, then work out which MRT lines or bus routes actually get you there within it. Door-to-door, not straight-line distance on a map.
- [ ] **List your must-haves versus nice-to-haves.** Wifi included, aircon, laundry access, a lock on your door. Separate the things you'll walk away over from the things you'll compromise on.
- [ ] **Confirm who you're renting from.** A licensed co-living operator, a private landlord, or a subletting tenant are three different risk levels. Know which one you're dealing with before you view.

## During the Viewing

Never skip the viewing. If you're overseas and can't view in person, insist on a live video walkthrough where you direct the camera yourself, not a pre-recorded clip the operator sends over. A prerecorded video can hide a lot; a live call where you can say "open that door" or "show me the window" can't.

- [ ] **See the actual room, not a similar one.** "This is the same layout as yours" is not the same as seeing your room. If it's not available to view, ask why and get a specific date it will be.
- [ ] **Check natural light and noise at the actual time you'd be living there.** A room facing a busy road looks fine at 11am and sounds different at 11pm.
- [ ] **Test the wifi speed on your own phone**, not just "the wifi's good" from whoever's showing you around.
- [ ] **Open every cupboard, check the mattress, check the aircon actually blows cold.** Small things become daily irritations fast.
- [ ] **Count the bathrooms against the number of people sharing them.** Ask how many other tenants use the same bathroom, not just how many rooms are in the unit.
- [ ] **Check the door lock on your own room**, and ask whether other tenants or the operator hold a spare key.
- [ ] **Walk the route from the unit to the nearest MRT or bus stop yourself.** Listing distances are often "as the crow flies," not the real walk.
- [ ] **Ask to see the kitchen and common areas in current, lived-in condition**, not freshly staged for a viewing.
- [ ] **Meet or ask about your future flatmates.** Age range, whether it's mixed gender, whether anyone else is also a student. This matters more to daily life than most other factors on this list.

## Before You Sign

This is where the checklist earns its keep. Read everything before signing anything.

- [ ] **Read the entire tenancy or licence agreement**, not just the clauses someone highlights for you. Ask what a licence agreement means versus a tenancy agreement if you're not sure, since they carry different legal protections.
- [ ] **Confirm the deposit amount and what triggers a deduction.** Get this in writing: how much, what condition the room needs to be in at move-out, and the timeline for the refund after you leave.
- [ ] **Confirm what's included in the rent.** Utilities, wifi, cleaning of common areas, and any service or agency fees should all be spelled out as line items, not assumed.
- [ ] **Confirm furnished versus unfurnished, item by item.** Bed, mattress, wardrobe, desk, chair. Don't take "furnished" at face value; ask for a list or photos of what's actually in the room right now.
- [ ] **Confirm the notice period for moving out**, and whether it applies to you at all if you're on a fixed term with no early-exit clause. Ask specifically what happens if your course ends early or you need to leave before the term is up.
- [ ] **Check the minimum stay.** Singapore's rule for private residential property is a minimum stay of three consecutive months; HDB room rentals typically run a six-month minimum. Confirm which applies to your unit.
- [ ] **Ask who else is on the agreement**, and whether you're jointly liable for anyone else's unpaid rent or damage.
- [ ] **Get the move-in condition documented**, photos or a written inventory, before you move your things in. This is what protects your deposit later.
- [ ] **Confirm the operator or landlord's contact for maintenance issues**, and how fast they typically respond. Ask, don't assume.

## Money and Legal Checks

The part most first-time renters skip entirely, and the part that can cost the most if it goes wrong.

- [ ] **Understand your stamp duty obligation.** If your agreement is a lease or tenancy agreement, IRAS charges stamp duty at 0.4% of the total rent for the lease term, payable within 14 days of signing (30 if signed outside Singapore). By convention the tenant pays, though it's negotiable and should be stated in your agreement. If the average annual rent works out to S$1,000 or less, no duty is owed. A genuine room licence in a shared unit may not attract stamp duty at all, since the tax attaches to leases with exclusive possession, not licences. Check which one you've actually signed.
- [ ] **Ask whether the unit is legally allowed to house the number of people living in it.** The default cap in Singapore is six unrelated occupants per unit. Some larger units, 4-room and up HDB flats, or private units of at least 90 square metres, qualify for a relaxed cap of eight, but only if the owner has actually registered or gained approval for it. A unit crammed past its legal cap is a risk to you too, since it can be flagged and shut down mid-lease.
- [ ] **For an HDB room or flat, confirm the landlord holds HDB's approval to sublet to a non-citizen.** This approval isn't automatic. Ask to see it, don't take a verbal assurance.
- [ ] **Confirm your pass will still be valid for the full lease term**, including any notice period at the end. If you're renewing your Student Pass partway through the lease, flag that to your landlord before you sign, not after.
- [ ] **Keep your stamp certificate and signed agreement somewhere safe**, ideally both a digital and physical copy. This is what you'll need if a deposit dispute ever comes up.
- [ ] **Verify the operator's legitimacy before transferring any money.** A legitimate operator asks for your pass and proof of enrolment, not the other way around. If a landlord is happy to skip all the checks, that's a warning sign, not a convenience.
