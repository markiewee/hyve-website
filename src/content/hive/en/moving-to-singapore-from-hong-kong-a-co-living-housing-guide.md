Moving to Singapore from Hong Kong starts with a visa question that trips people up: HKSAR passport holders get 30 days visa-free entry, enough for a job interview or a week of room viewings, but not enough to actually live here. Living and renting requires an issued Student Pass, Employment Pass, or S Pass, not just an offer letter. Once that pass is in hand, the practical move for most people is a furnished co-living room for the first few months, then a longer-term decision once the pass and the job or course are both confirmed.

Hong Kong has sent a steady stream of finance, tech, and regional-HQ professionals to Singapore over the past several years, drawn by the city's role as a regional base. This guide skips the reasons and goes straight to what matters once you've decided to come: the paperwork, the costs, and where housing fits into your first few months.

## Do I Need a Visa Just to Enter Singapore From Hong Kong?

Not for a short visit. HKSAR passport holders get 30 days of visa-free entry to Singapore for business or social visits, enough time to attend interviews, view rooms, and sort early paperwork. That's specific to HKSAR passport holders: HKSAR Document of Identity (DOI) holders face separate visa requirements, so check which document you're travelling on before assuming you're covered.

Everyone arriving visa-free still needs to submit Singapore's SG Arrival Card within three days before landing, and your passport needs at least six months of validity left from your arrival date. None of this lets you sign a lease. Entering visa-free and holding a valid immigration pass are two different things, and only the second lets you commit to housing.

## Which Pass Do I Actually Need Before I Can Rent?

For students, it's a Student Pass, applied for by your school through ICA's SOLAR system once you have an offer. Processing typically takes four to eight weeks, and the pass fee is S$90.

For working professionals, it's an Employment Pass or S Pass, sponsored by your employer. As of 2026, the Employment Pass salary floor is S$5,600 a month (S$6,200 in financial services), assessed against the COMPASS points framework and rising again from 1 January 2027. The S Pass floor for younger applicants sits at S$3,300 a month (S$3,800 in financial services).

A posting letter from a Hong Kong head office, an offer letter, or an In-Principle Approval isn't the same as an issued pass, and no properly run operator will sign a lease against any of those alone. ## How Far Ahead Should I Book Housing?

Line your housing search up against your pass timeline, not your flight date. A Student Pass typically clears four to eight weeks after your school submits it, and an Employment Pass or S Pass moves on a broadly similar timeline, sometimes faster with an efficient corporate HR or relocation team behind it.

Book something short and flexible to land in, then commit to a longer lease once your pass is confirmed and your start date is set. This is where co-living earns its keep: you're not locking into a rigid 12-month tenancy before you know your exact pass issue date.

## What Does Housing Actually Cost?

Rooms in Singapore's shared housing market run from about S$600 for a basic budget room up to S$2,500 for a private ensuite, with most standard common rooms landing between S$1,000 and S$1,600 a month. Co-living rooms, furnished and often bundled with utilities and wifi, typically sit in the S$1,200 to S$2,500+ range depending on operator and location.

If you're mentally converting from Hong Kong prices, the comparison is closer than you'd expect. A one-bedroom apartment in Central or Admiralty commonly runs the equivalent of roughly SGD 4,300 to 6,000 a month, while a comparable one-bedroom condo in Singapore's CBD or Orchard corridor runs SGD 3,500 to 5,000. Singapore's overall cost of living sits somewhat higher than Hong Kong's, but rent for a whole private unit is often on par or slightly cheaper, and a shared co-living room undercuts both by a wide margin.

Add food, transport, and a local SIM, and a realistic all-in monthly budget for a newcomer renting a room lands somewhere between S$1,800 and S$2,800. For upfront cash, budget a security deposit (standard practice is one month's rent) plus your first month, so two months' rent in hand before you get the keys is the safe assumption. We've broken deposit terms down further in our guide to [security deposits in Singapore co-living](https://www.lazybee.sg/hive/security-deposit-coliving-singapore). An all-inclusive room also removes a layer of guesswork, since utilities, service charges, and wifi aren't billed here the way they are back home, and one number covers all of it.

## Where Do Hong Kong Professionals and Families Actually Live?

Unlike some diaspora communities in Singapore, there isn't one clear Hong Kong enclave. Professionals mostly gravitate toward areas close to the CBD and regional-HQ office clusters, Raffles Place, Tanjong Pagar, River Valley, and Robertson Quay among them, largely for the commute rather than for community.

Cantonese speakers generally find it easier to settle into Singapore's food and social scene than newcomers from further afield, given the size of Singapore's own Chinese population and the number of Cantonese-friendly kitchens around Chinatown and beyond. It's a smaller adjustment than the visa paperwork, in practice.

For families with school-age children, Singapore doesn't run a dedicated Hong Kong curriculum school the way it does for a few other nationalities. Most families slot into the mainstream international school system instead, and those wanting a bilingual or Mandarin-heavy programme over a fully English one have several established options worth comparing before you commit to a lease near any one campus.

## Opening a Bank Account and Getting Set Up

You'll need your issued pass, not just an offer letter, to open an account at most banks. DBS accepts applications online and will work from an ICA In-Principle Approval plus an employer or school letter if your pass hasn't landed yet, while UOB generally expects a branch visit with your pass already in hand. Have your passport and proof of local address (your co-living agreement works for this) ready either way.

Once your card lands, day-to-day spending is easy. The bigger adjustment is usually getting used to PayNow, since a lot of everyday payments here, from splitting a bill to paying a landlord, run through PayNow rather than a card or a Hong Kong-style Octopus tap.

## Mistakes to Avoid

The most common one is booking a rigid, long lease before your pass is actually issued, then scrambling when the issue date shifts by a couple of weeks, which happens often enough to plan around. A second is assuming your 30-day visa-free window leaves room to settle into a normal lease; it's for viewings and paperwork, nothing more. A third is over-indexing on finding "the Hong Kong area" of Singapore, when a room on a direct MRT line to your office or campus will serve you better day to day than proximity to any particular expat cluster.

A fourth, common among arrivals on an internal transfer or regional posting, is signing a 12-month lease sight unseen against a housing allowance without checking exactly what it covers. A short co-living booking for the first month keeps you flexible until you've actually seen the commute and neighbourhood in person, rather than choosing off a floor plan from Hong Kong.

## A Quick Checklist Before You Book Anything

- [ ] Confirm your pass application is submitted and get a realistic issue-date estimate from your school or employer
- [ ] Check whether you're travelling on an HKSAR passport or DOI, since the visa rules differ between the two
- [ ] Budget two months' rent in cash for deposit plus first month, on top of flight and settling-in costs
- [ ] Check what's actually included in any room price (utilities, wifi, cleaning) before comparing two listings on rent alone
- [ ] Set up PayNow once your bank account is active, since a lot of local payments assume it
- [ ] If you're on a company relocation package, confirm what the housing allowance covers before signing anything long-term

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need a visa to fly to Singapore from Hong Kong for a viewing?**
No, if you're travelling on an HKSAR passport. You get 30 days visa-free, enough for interviews and room viewings, but it doesn't count as a long-term stay or let you sign a lease. HKSAR Document of Identity holders face different requirements, so check which document applies to you.

**Can I rent a room in Singapore before my Employment Pass is issued?**
No. You need the pass itself in hand to sign a standard lease or licence agreement. A short-term booking that doesn't require a long-term pass is the practical way to bridge the gap while your pass processes.

**Is there a specific neighbourhood where Hong Kong expats live in Singapore?**
Not really. Unlike some other communities, Hong Kong arrivals tend to spread across areas close to the CBD and their office rather than clustering in one district. Picking a room near your commute usually matters more than picking a district for its expat profile.

**Can I open a Singapore bank account before my pass arrives?**
Some banks, DBS in particular, will start the process using an ICA In-Principle Approval and an employer or school letter. Most still want the actual pass before the account is fully active.

**Does a company relocation package change what kind of lease I should sign?**
Not automatically. Confirm exactly what the housing allowance covers before committing to a long lease, and consider a short, flexible booking for your first weeks while those details get finalised.
