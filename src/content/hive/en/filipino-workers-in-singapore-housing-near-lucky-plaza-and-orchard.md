You don't need to live inside Orchard to stay close to Lucky Plaza. A room on a well-connected MRT line, whether that's a direct ride on the North-South Line or the newer Thomson-East Coast Line, can put you 20 to 30 minutes from Lucky Plaza without paying the rent that comes with living in the Orchard planning area itself. This guide covers what Lucky Plaza actually is today, how the wider Filipino community in Singapore looks across different jobs, and what to weigh up when picking a room.

Filipino workers in Singapore span a wide range of roles, from domestic and service work to nursing, IT, engineering, finance, and hospitality. Housing needs differ across that range too, so there's no single "right" answer, just tradeoffs worth understanding before you sign anything.

## What Is Lucky Plaza to the Filipino Community Today?

Lucky Plaza, at 304 Orchard Road, has been closely associated with Singapore's Filipino community for decades and still functions that way in 2026. It's home to Filipino eateries, remittance and money transfer counters, grocery stores carrying Filipino products, phone card shops, and services like document processing and legal referrals aimed at the community.

Sundays are when this is most visible. Domestic workers get their day off on Sunday, and Lucky Plaza and its surrounding corridors fill up from early morning through the evening, particularly between 11am and 4pm, as people meet friends, send remittances, eat, and catch up. It's a genuine community space, not just a shopping centre with Filipino tenants.

The Philippine Embassy treats it that way too. In March 2026, the Philippine Ambassador visited Lucky Plaza with embassy staff specifically to engage the community and support Filipino-owned businesses there, which is a fair signal that the mall's role as a cultural hub, not just a retail address, is still active and recognised at an official level.

Worth knowing: Lucky Plaza is a gathering point and a place to run errands, not a place most people live. Very little residential housing sits directly above or immediately around it, and Orchard Road rents run high across the board. Most people who spend their Sundays there live elsewhere on the island and commute in.

## Who Actually Makes Up "Filipino Workers in Singapore"?

It's a mistake to picture one type of worker here. Estimates put the number of Filipinos working in Singapore at around 200,000, and that figure spans several distinct groups.

- **Domestic and caregiving workers.** A large share, with estimates in the tens of thousands, work as helpers or caregivers, often living in their employer's home rather than renting independently.
- **Healthcare professionals.** Filipino nurses have worked in Singapore's public hospitals for years, and Singapore has been actively looking to recruit more Filipino health and allied health workers as the sector grows.
- **IT, engineering, and finance professionals.** A meaningful and growing number hold Employment Passes or S Passes in tech, engineering, banking, and corporate roles, most of them renting their own place rather than living with an employer.
- **Hospitality and service workers.** Restaurants, retail, and hotels across Singapore employ Filipino staff in customer-facing and operational roles.



What connects these groups isn't the job. It's that Lucky Plaza, Filipino churches, and community networks remain touchpoints regardless of what a person does Monday to Friday. A nurse on an EP and a domestic worker on her day off might both value being able to reach Orchard reasonably easily, even if their daily routines look nothing alike.

## How Far Is Lucky Plaza From Where You'd Actually Live?

Renting directly in the Orchard area is expensive relative to most of the island, and there's limited residential stock immediately around it anyway. The more realistic question is how long a commute you're accepting in exchange for lower rent further out.

Thomson Grove has a real advantage here. Orchard sits directly on the Thomson-East Coast Line, so there's no transfer involved, just a straight ride down from Lentor. None of these are "next door" to Lucky Plaza, and that's normal. Very few affordable rooms are. What matters is landing on a line that gets you there without multiple transfers eating into your one day off.

## What Does Co-Living Actually Offer Here?

Co-living solves a specific problem for someone moving to Singapore for work: you need a room fast, furnished, and without chasing a local guarantor, and you often don't know the island well enough yet to judge one neighbourhood against another.

A few things that matter in practice:

- **Everything's already set up.** Bed, wardrobe, wifi, and shared kitchen and living space are ready from day one. You're not furnishing an empty unit in your first week in a new country.
- **Verification instead of a guarantor.** Most co-living operators check your pass and income directly rather than requiring a Singapore-based guarantor, which is often the hardest part of renting solo as a new arrival.
- **Shorter, more flexible lease terms.** Useful if your pass or contract runs on an annual cycle rather than the standard 12-month tenancy a private landlord usually wants.
- **Built-in company.** Moving to a new country alone is easier with people already around, even if you keep mostly to yourself during the week.

## What Should You Check Before Committing to a Room?

A few practical points that matter more than which neighbourhood looks nicest on a map.

**Match the lease to your pass or contract length**, not to the standard term a listing defaults to. If your job or placement runs 12 months with a possible renewal, don't lock into an 18-month lease on day one. Our guide on [renting on an EP, S Pass, or Student Pass](/blog/renting-on-ep-s-pass-student-pass-singapore) covers this in more detail.

**Check the deposit terms before signing.** A refundable deposit, usually one month's rent, is standard practice. Our [security deposit guide](/blog/security-deposit-coliving-singapore) explains what's normal and what to watch for when getting it back.

**Think about your actual weekly pattern, not just Sunday.** If Orchard is a once-a-week trip, a 20 to 30 minute ride is a reasonable tradeoff for a quieter, more affordable home base the other six days. If you need to be in the Orchard area more often for work, that changes the calculation, and it's worth weighing the commute honestly rather than assuming you'll "figure it out."

Yes. As of 2026 it remains a central gathering point, especially on Sundays, with Filipino eateries, remittance services, and community organisations based there, and continued engagement from the Philippine Embassy.

There's very little residential housing directly around it, and Orchard-area rents are among the highest in Singapore. Most people who spend time at Lucky Plaza commute in from elsewhere on the island.
