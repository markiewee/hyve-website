01

What this brand is for

Lazybee leases apartments from private owners, furnishes them, fills them, and runs them. The owner does nothing and is paid on the first of the month whether the rooms are full or empty. Tenants get a room in a properly run home instead of a stranger's spare bedroom.

Two audiences, one promise, and the promise is relief. Not luxury for its own sake. The restraint in this identity is doing a job: it is what a serious counterparty looks like when they are about to sign a three year lease with you.

The one line

Be a lazy landlord.

Everything else in the copy system is downstream of that. Plain verbs, concrete numbers, no adjectives doing work that a fact could do.

02

The mark

A seven-pointed star, drawn as a heraldic mullet with concave edges. One point falls straight down.

The seven points are not decoration. Read them as a bee seen from above and they are, in order from the bottom: the stinger, then a pair of legs, then the long pair of wings, then a pair of antennae. Nobody has to see that for the mark to work, and everybody can see it once told, which is the correct order for a symbol.

Drawn as a single closed path with no strokes, so it survives any scale, any colour and any printing method.

03

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space equals the height of one point, measured from the centre to the tip of the stinger. Nothing enters that boundary: no type, no rule, no image edge, no other logo.

Minimum sizes. 24px on screen for the mark alone, 120px wide for the full vertical lockup, 15mm wide in print. Below 24px the short legs and antennae start rounding away and the mark goes lopsided, so use the square icon crop and nothing smaller.

04

Lockups

The vertical lockup is primary and is the only one that carries the place name. The horizontal exists for the site header and any narrow band. The mark stands alone anywhere it is already obvious who is speaking.

How much of each

Ground dominates, ink carries the reading, accent is rationed. If a page has more than three accent elements on screen at once, one of them is wrong.

The accent appears on: the primary button, the place name in the lockup, and the current item in a navigation. That is the whole list.

Rules

Wide tracking belongs to the wordmark and to small uppercase labels only. Body copy is set normally. Letterspaced sentences read as a perfume advertisement.

Money is always monospaced, with tabular figures, so a number reads as a readout rather than a claim.

Sentence case everywhere except labels. Title Case On Headings makes the brand sound like a brochure.

08

Voice

The typography carries the luxury. The words stay plain. Every sentence should survive being read aloud to a sceptical landlord who has been called by four agents this week.

09

Photography

Real rooms, daylight, no wide angle. A distorted lens says property listing, and a property listing is the thing we are trying not to be.

Evidence of people, rarely people. A made bed, a used mug, shoes at the door. Occupancy without models.

Never an empty staged lobby, never a stock family, never a rendered interior. If it did not happen in one of our three homes, it does not go on the site.

10

Layout and motion

Space is the material

Spacing runs on a four point scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 130. Section padding never goes below 64 on desktop. When a layout feels cheap, the answer is almost always more space, not more ornament.

One hairline rule at a time, always in --line. No shadows, no gradients behind text, no rounded corners above 2px except on app tiles.

Motion is slow and few

Entrances rise 14 to 18 pixels and fade over 650 to 800 milliseconds on an ease-out curve. Stagger in 60 millisecond steps. Nothing bounces, nothing spins, nothing loops except live data.

Every animation ships behind prefers-reduced-motion. That is not a nicety, it is part of the quality floor along with visible keyboard focus and a page that works at 320 pixels wide.
